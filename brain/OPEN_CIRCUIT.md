# OPEN CIRCUIT

> Approved durable First-Life support authority. Canonical prose wins every conflict. This is development pressure, not fixed chapter choreography.

## Core

Greg's climb from useful C-class-adjacent support to S-class must be earned through a compounding method rather than a hidden gift:

observe -> intervene -> solicit feedback -> compare result to expectation -> revise -> repeat.

Greg is not infallible. He often interprets too quickly. His unusual advantage is that he notices feedback, tolerates being proven wrong, and iterates aggressively. Competent critics must sometimes be fully correct about the implementation in front of them.

## Normal support doctrine

Conventional support is rational. A professional support normally maintains personal aura/self-reinforcement, works from a relatively stable protected pocket, minimizes expensive target switching, uses standardized broad patterns that work with unfamiliar teams, concentrates sustained support on predictable high-value roles, and values reliability/breadth/day-one deployability.

The meta exists because losing the support can collapse a party. Stability improves precision. Self-aura makes incidental hits survivable. Repeated reacquisition, motion, distance, and unfamiliar targets waste mana and attention.

Greg does not prove this doctrine stupid. He discovers a narrower specialization whose costs he becomes unusually capable of paying.

## Barrier -> Draw -> network

Barrier remains Greg's root discipline. Its primitive is boundary control: what crosses, what remains stable, where pressure goes, and who or what carries it.

A serious Barrier specialist studies what defeats barriers. That naturally leads Greg to Draw.

Draw is primarily an anti-ward / draining technique. Rather than overpower a maintained ward, Draw attacks sustaining flow by pulling mana out faster than the structure replenishes it. Greg learns it to understand Barrier failure, not because he is searching for a convenient party-sharing spell.

His key conceptual discovery is that Barrier and Draw manipulate the same system from opposite directions:

- Barrier defines the boundary/valve.
- Draw creates or redirects flow across it.

The progression pressure is:

Barrier specialization -> study counters -> Draw as ward drain -> diagnostic Draw -> controlled allied pressure relief -> redistribution -> mana cycling -> linking -> multi-person capability architecture.

Do not jump stages simply because later authority knows where they lead.

## Aura conflict / open circuit

Personal aura is effectively a continuously maintained self-boundary/reinforcement field. Full aura materially interferes with tactical Draw-routing because aura tries to keep mana coherent and bound to Greg while routing requires mana to enter, pass through, and leave.

Greg eventually chooses to stop maintaining continuous aura while his mature network is active.

This is a real sacrifice. He becomes more vulnerable to debris, falls, stray attacks, magical pressure, and fatigue-driven mistakes. He can use reactive/local Barrier defenses, but he cannot simply run full passive aura and receive all benefits of open routing.

First-Life Greg becomes an OPEN CIRCUIT: less capacity spent making himself a closed durable unit, more capacity available to move through relationships between people.

## Mana cycling

Cycling never creates mana. Transfers have loss, range cost, switching cost, foreign-mana friction, throughput limits, source risk, target risk, and cognitive/physical cost to Greg.

Party demand is uneven. One member bursts while another recovers. One has deep reserve and slow recovery. Another has shallow reserve and fast regeneration. A full reservoir can waste potential regeneration because there is no room to refill.

Greg learns to move small amounts of existing capacity toward the current bottleneck and sometimes draw a safe margin from a nearly full member so their natural regeneration continues producing useful capacity.

He does not equalize everyone. He learns individual operating windows.

A stronger party can make Greg's mature network more valuable because elite people possess larger reserves, stronger regeneration, greater self-sufficiency, and more consequential windows. It also makes the network harder and more dangerous to control.

Greg remains the bottleneck. Complexity grows faster than headcount. He cannot casually link armies.

## Diagnostic Draw

Tiny controlled Draw can become another feedback channel. It may reveal resistance/flow behavior, reserve pressure, strained channels, recovery state, or bottlenecks. It never reads thoughts or emotions.

This compounds Greg's existing habit of studying posture, pain, compensation, fatigue, equipment, food, timing, and technique.

## Adaptive allocation

Greg's mature specialty is not "buff everyone." Mana, attention, position, and switching speed remain scarce.

He becomes unusually good at asking who or what benefits most from support now.

The anchor may already be safe enough. A striker may have a two-second window. A caster may need stabilization rather than reserve. A recovering member may temporarily be a source. Terrain or equipment may be the best place to carry a consequence.

The value of support moves during real operations. Greg becomes unusually good at moving with it.

Normal support can outperform him in clean, predictable, unfamiliar, mass-support, or low-trust situations.

## Mobility / stamina

Cycling is the engine. Mobility is the delivery system.

Proximity improves feedback, lowers transfer loss, speeds reacquisition, and improves precision. Conventional support makes the party remain near the support. Greg trains himself to move through the party.

His C-class warrior history provides useful chassis: hostile spacing, footwork, falling, route choice, and functioning while threatened. It does not make him an elite warrior.

Greg spends years building extreme work capacity through running, hills, intervals, loaded movement, casting while moving, target changes under high heart rate, and long field days. Aerobic fatigue should visibly degrade fine magic early.

Greg never becomes the fastest fighter. He becomes efficient at interception and positioning. Observation lets him move before geometry changes.

## Party design

Greg eventually helps create/assemble a party around adaptive reallocation rather than abolishing ordinary roles.

Best-case conventional allocations remain correct when a fight stays clean.

His party is selected/trained for self-sufficiency when support leaves, honest feedback, complementary demand/recovery rhythms, clean handoffs, trust around controlled Draw, and ability to create movement lanes for Greg.

The human coordination precedes the mature magical network.

## Why S-class wants Greg

Elite people already possess enormous aura, reserve, reinforcement, and technique, so ordinary additive support can experience diminishing returns.

Greg instead manages the reasons they cannot safely use existing capacity: recoil, channel strain, load concentration, unstable transitions, recovery windows, and failure propagation.

A useful intervention may last less than a second. Greg's mature promise is effectively: you fight; I will keep up.

He does not require an old S-class monster to relearn a lifetime of fighting around a support formation. He learns the monster.

This is why S-class people can become protective or possessive of access to Greg. They know their ceiling. Greg gives them usable margin they thought no longer existed.

## Arcrutus / workshop

Greg actively asks powerful people and specialists what fails first when they push hard. Fighters, supports, medics, artificers, engineers, runners, climbers, and ward-breakers can all become sources of useful feedback.

Arcrutus remains sparse catalyst, not mentor engine. He attacks Greg's framing and can impose absurd practical tests, including controlled beasts chasing Greg while Greg must run, think, cast, protect targets, or manage competing priorities.

Arcrutus supplies occasional pressure. Greg turns it into years of method.

## Professional pushback

Future prose needs real disagreement.

Experts may correctly say Greg has poor breadth, switches too much, moves too much, wastes mana, depends too heavily on repeat partners, makes himself fragile, uses an aggressive drain technique unsafely, and builds parties that become vulnerable if he goes down.

Sometimes Greg's attempt should fail and the critic should be right.

Greg can still be right that the accepted answer is incomplete. His response is narrower claims, evidence, and iteration, not speeches about everyone else being stupid.

## Life-Two inversion

First-Life peak is relational/open: outward links, repeated partners, trust, movement, shared rhythms, minimal continuous self-aura while routing.

After catastrophe, Greg deliberately moves toward the opposite architecture before losing the leg: self-reinforcement, self-stabilization, terrain anchoring, hostile Draw/routing, stored mana/artifacts, narrow loops, and reduced dependence on partners.

The ruined leg later makes the old mobile relational style even harder to recover, but disability is not the original cause. Choice comes first; injury compounds it.

First Life: I am strongest between people.
Life-Two revenge: I will make myself a complete circuit.
