## First-Life state after Record 055

Greg is about thirty-five. Roughly three and a half to four years have passed since the second tournament. Progress has come through contracts, mistakes, injury, recovery, training, repeated partners, seasonal conditioning, and ordinary absence rather than uninterrupted power-up scenes.

### Record 045 · THE POCKET
Greg learns why conventional support doctrine favors self-aura, stable protected positioning, predictable target priority, and reserve. He tries to solve a moving support problem by running late and discovers that seeing the useful place is not enough if his body and casting precision cannot reach it cleanly. He begins asking how maintained Barriers are actually defeated.

### Record 046 · THE BREAKER
Late in the north-road contract, Greg watches ward-breaker Mara Quill dismantle an old maintained ward with Draw rather than brute force. He learns that Draw attacks sustaining flow and moves mana rather than simply destroying it. Mara explicitly warns that using aggressive Draw on living aura is not support. The three-month party then actually ends, leaving ordinary absence behind.

### Record 047 · THE DRAIN
Over roughly the next year Greg studies breaker Draw while continuing ordinary work. He is bad at it for months. His personal aura repeatedly interferes with foreign flow, and lowering aura makes Draw cleaner while making Greg more physically vulnerable. He becomes competent at breaker Draw, not exceptional, and remains warned away from using it as a shortcut for living transfer.

### Record 048 · THE OPENING
On a later quarry contract Greg uses a tiny emergency Draw on Orla's overloaded aura while she is holding shifting stone. The intervention partly works and the mana is immediately reused in one of Greg's Barriers, but Greg violates consent and cannot measure exactly what he removed. He spends months studying legitimate relief and transfer methods instead of repeating the trick casually.

### Record 049 · THE CHASE
Greg asks Arcrutus about switching and mobility. Arcrutus reframes the problem: Greg is already late before he starts moving. Controlled ridge-hound drills force Greg to run, think, maintain fine Barriers, manage aura, and anticipate where support will be needed. Arcrutus leaves after providing the hostile test. Greg spends seasons turning it into road running, hills, loaded movement, and casting-under-fatigue practice.

### Record 050 · THE BORROWED PART
The old tournament four reunite for crawler suppression. Greg tests a narrow redistribution method: with explicit consent, Draw a small amount from one teammate and immediately spend it on Greg's own support elsewhere. Drawing from Calder at the correct recovery moment works. Drawing from Veda because she appears idle ruins a prepared burst. The party creates crude availability signals. Greg has found a mechanism, not a mature network.

### Record 051 · THE SIGNAL
The hand signals survive the contract and become shared language among repeat partners. Greg discovers the method degrades sharply with unfamiliar people when Renn Fale signals availability but her untrained body reflexively tightens aura during the pull, contributing to a wrist strain. Greg's repeat-team advantage is reciprocal: partners learn him as much as he learns them. Signal is not training.

### Record 052 · THE WRONG DRAW
Even trained consent is not enough. During live terrain collapse Veda signals a small draw, conditions change half a second later, and Greg executes the now-stale decision anyway. The pull removes reinforcement from her ankle as footing shifts, causing a ligament injury and seven weeks away from hard field work. A contractor and Guild review correctly restrict live ally Draw until Greg demonstrates safer revocation behavior. Greg pays part of Veda's lost contract income and earns actual fear of the technique.

### Record 053 · THE FULL CUP
During supervised recovery work Greg notices that full reserve can suppress additional recovery opportunity, but experts force the caveat: full is not waste, reserve is not a bucket, high starting pressure may be required, and repeated cycling itself has bodily cost. Greg begins tracking recovery rhythms rather than visible reserve alone. Controlled reinstatement follows only after months of safer training.

### Record 054 · THE ROTATION
Greg helps choose a five-person North Mason roster and recommends nominally weaker Noll over stronger Deren because Noll's spending/recovery rhythm complements Calder and Veda instead of clustering with them. The decision looks bad for days before a long contract shows why complementary demand matters. The party trains losing support as deliberately as receiving it. A field report calls the method effective with trained rosters and unsuitable for ad hoc crews. Both are true.

### Record 055 · THE EVALUATION
Greg attempts B-class support evaluation and fails. By-the-book support Sel Aven outperforms him in unfamiliar-team support, emergency breadth, static formation work, and sustained self-protection. Greg's specialty course with trained partners is exceptional in select conditions, but evaluators correctly judge the method too roster-dependent, unsafe, narrow, and difficult to reproduce for B-class credit. Greg remains C-class support while the official letter also records that attached-team performance exceeds his current classification in select conditions.

## Approved First-Life climb pressure

Durable development direction:

Barrier specialization -> study what defeats Barrier -> Draw as ward-drain/counter-technique -> controlled relief of allied magical pressure -> redistribution/cycling -> linking -> multi-person capability architecture.

The approved design lives in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-15-first-life-open-circuit-support-design.md` and is mirrored in `brain/OPEN_CIRCUIT.md` on the working branch.

Important constraints:
- conventional support doctrine is rational and often safer;
- competent experts should tell Greg he is wrong, and sometimes they should be completely right about the implementation in front of them;
- Greg's advantage is fast feedback and iteration, not infallibility;
- personal aura/self-reinforcement materially conflicts with tactical Draw-routing;
- Greg's eventual open-circuit style sacrifices passive self-protection for outward flow rather than granting a free efficiency upgrade;
- mobility and extreme stamina matter because proximity improves sensing, transfer, reacquisition, and precision;
- Greg's C-class warrior history gives him a movement chassis, not secret elite combat ability;
- mana cycling never creates mana; it reallocates existing reserve and otherwise-idle regeneration opportunity;
- recovery itself has conditions and costs; a full reserve can suppress additional recovery without making fullness inherently wasteful;
- stronger and more complementary parties give mature Greg more useful margin to allocate, while also increasing control complexity;
- Greg is currently much better with repeat partners than unfamiliar crews and the classification system is rational to care about that;
- S-class people eventually value Greg because he can release capacity trapped behind actual bottlenecks rather than merely add raw power;
- Arcrutus is catalyst, not conventional mentor;
- the First-Life climb is relational and outward. Life-Two revenge later turns the same intelligence toward increasingly closed/self-contained loops.
