## First-Life state after Record 060

Greg is about thirty-six, give or take. Roughly four to five lived years have passed since the second tournament. He remains officially C-class support after the failed B evaluation, but stronger repeat parties and contractors increasingly treat his attached-team work as operating above that label under specific conditions.

### Record 045 · THE POCKET
Greg learns why conventional support doctrine favors self-aura, stable protected positioning, predictable target priority, and reserve. He tries to solve a moving support problem by running late and discovers that seeing the useful place is not enough if his body and casting precision cannot reach it cleanly. He begins asking how maintained Barriers are actually defeated.

### Record 046 · THE BREAKER
Late in the north-road contract, Greg watches ward-breaker Mara Quill dismantle an old maintained ward with Draw rather than brute force. Draw attacks sustaining flow and moves mana rather than simply destroying it. Mara explicitly warns that using aggressive Draw on living aura is not support.

### Record 047 · THE DRAIN
Over roughly the next year Greg studies breaker Draw while continuing ordinary work. He is bad at it for months. Personal aura interferes with foreign flow, and lowering aura makes Draw cleaner while making Greg more physically vulnerable. He becomes competent at breaker Draw, not exceptional.

### Record 048 · THE OPENING
On a later quarry contract Greg uses a tiny emergency Draw on Orla's overloaded aura while she is holding shifting stone. The intervention partly works and the mana is immediately reused in one of Greg's Barriers, but Greg violates consent and cannot measure exactly what he removed. He spends months studying legitimate relief and transfer methods.

### Record 049 · THE CHASE
Greg asks Arcrutus Dray about switching and mobility. Arcrutus reframes the problem: Greg is already late before he starts moving. Ridge-hound drills force Greg to run, think, maintain fine Barriers, manage aura, and anticipate where support will be needed. Greg spends seasons turning the hostile test into conditioning and casting-under-fatigue practice.

### Record 050 · THE BORROWED PART
The old tournament four reunite for crawler suppression. Greg tests a narrow redistribution method: with explicit consent, Draw a small amount from one teammate and immediately spend it on Greg's own support elsewhere. Drawing from Calder at the correct recovery moment works. Drawing from Veda because she appears idle ruins a prepared burst. The party creates crude availability signals.

### Record 051 · THE SIGNAL
The hand signals survive the contract and become shared language among repeat partners. Greg discovers the method degrades sharply with unfamiliar people. His repeat-team advantage is reciprocal: partners learn him as much as he learns them. Signal is not training.

### Record 052 · THE WRONG DRAW
Even trained consent is not enough. During live terrain collapse Veda signals a small draw, conditions change half a second later, and Greg executes the now-stale decision anyway. The pull contributes to a serious ankle injury and seven weeks away from hard field work. Contractor and Guild review correctly restrict live ally Draw until Greg demonstrates safer revocation behavior.

### Record 053 · THE FULL CUP
During supervised recovery work Greg notices that full reserve can suppress additional recovery opportunity, but experts force the caveat: full is not waste, reserve is not a bucket, high starting pressure may be required, and repeated cycling itself has bodily cost. Greg begins tracking recovery rhythms rather than visible reserve alone.

### Record 054 · THE ROTATION
Greg helps choose a North Mason roster and recommends nominally weaker Corin Hart over stronger Deren because Corin's spending/recovery rhythm complements the group instead of clustering with it. The decision looks bad for days before a long contract shows why complementary demand matters. The party trains losing support as deliberately as receiving it. A field report calls the method effective with trained rosters and unsuitable for ad hoc crews. Both are true.

### Record 055 · THE EVALUATION
Greg attempts B-class support evaluation and fails. By-the-book support Sel Aven legitimately outperforms him in unfamiliar-team support, emergency breadth, static formation work, and sustained self-protection. Greg's trained-party specialty is exceptional in select conditions, but evaluators correctly judge it too roster-dependent, unsafe, narrow, and difficult to reproduce for B-class credit.

### Record 056 · THE REQUEST
Six weeks later Greg applies for White Stair and discovers he has already been specifically requested by Aren Dray, Arcrutus's forty-two-year-old A-class eldest son. Aren becomes the first fighter to deliberately alter his own timing, lines, and positioning around Greg's support access. His key question is: **Why only one direction?** The party begins asking not only what support they need, but what they can change to make support cheaper and the operation better.

### Record 057 · THE TEMPER
Greg notices Aren commits cleaner when mildly irritated. Greg crosses a line once by invoking Arcrutus on purpose to trigger that state. Aren catches it and sets the durable boundary: **I'm allowing you to know me. Do not confuse knowing with owning.** The party begins volunteering non-magical performance conditions of their own: reassurance, silence, fatigue tells, confidence, irritation, and timing. Greg stops pretending support ends at mana.

### Record 058 · THE ROSTER
For Red Basin, Aren helps build a stronger four-month party around Greg rather than asking Greg to fit into an existing one. A-class channel caster Jessa Rook, B shield Sella Tor, and B combat Corin Hart join Aren and Greg. Greg rejects stronger-on-paper Varo Tess initially because his demand rhythm clusters badly with Aren's. The party begins adapting in both directions, including Jessa delaying a stronger personal attack because the operation becomes faster overall. Contractors start sending requests specifically for Greg.

### Record 059 · THE OPEN SIDE
Greg's increasingly valuable Draw-routing exposes the conflict with continuous self-aura. Dropping aura makes routing and reacquisition cleaner but leaves him physically vulnerable and frees attention for the external network. He is actually cut while open on unstable footing. The party's trained failure response works. They add formation consent to source consent: Greg may have available mana to move, but the group must also be able to afford his temporary vulnerability. Other members deliberately spend small amounts of position and attention to create safe open windows.

### Record 060 · THE SUBSTITUTE
Corin is temporarily sidelined by mundane illness, forcing the party to integrate Varo Tess, the stronger-on-paper fighter Greg originally rejected. The method degrades, exactly as critics predicted. Aren begins teaching the reciprocal-adaptation principle before Greg does. Varo does not become Corin; the entire party changes around Varo's different rhythm until acceptable field performance returns in two days. Corin's return reveals another flaw: Greg's detailed models can themselves become stale. A contractor report explicitly records that method knowledge now exists in party members without Greg prompting them. The Guild invites Greg to B-class re-evaluation, but he does not immediately apply.

## Approved First-Life climb pressure

Durable development direction:

Barrier specialization -> study what defeats Barrier -> Draw as ward-drain/counter-technique -> controlled relief of allied magical pressure -> redistribution/cycling -> reciprocal party adaptation -> linking -> multi-person capability architecture.

The approved design lives in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-15-first-life-open-circuit-support-design.md` and is mirrored in `brain/OPEN_CIRCUIT.md` on the working branch.

Important constraints:
- conventional support doctrine is rational and often safer;
- competent experts should tell Greg he is wrong, and sometimes they should be completely right about the implementation in front of them;
- Greg's advantage is fast feedback and iteration, not infallibility;
- personal aura/self-reinforcement materially conflicts with tactical Draw-routing;
- Greg's eventual open-circuit style sacrifices passive self-protection for outward flow rather than granting a free efficiency upgrade;
- mobility and extreme stamina matter because proximity improves sensing, transfer, reacquisition, and precision;
- Greg's C-class warrior history gives him a movement chassis, not secret elite combat ability;
- mana cycling never creates mana; it reallocates existing reserve and otherwise-idle regeneration opportunity;
- recovery itself has conditions and costs; a full reserve can suppress additional recovery without making fullness inherently wasteful;
- stronger and more complementary parties give mature Greg more useful margin to allocate, while also increasing control complexity;
- reciprocal adaptation is now party knowledge, not only Greg's insight;
- knowing a teammate's emotional/performance state does not grant Greg ownership or permission to manipulate it without boundaries;
- Greg's open-aura state requires party compensation and formation consent, preserving a real vulnerability;
- Greg is improving portability, but he still gets worse when personnel change and must retrain around actual current people rather than stale models;
- S-class people eventually value Greg because he can release capacity trapped behind actual bottlenecks rather than merely add raw power;
- Arcrutus Dray is catalyst, not conventional mentor;
- the First-Life climb is relational and outward. Life-Two revenge later turns the same intelligence toward increasingly closed/self-contained loops.

## Pressure for Record 061

Do not immediately cash the B-class re-evaluation invitation into a badge.

Greg now has stronger evidence than he had at 055, but the more important development is that the method is beginning to exist between people rather than solely inside Greg. The next stretch should test whether this can become deliberate architecture without jumping straight to mature magical linking.

Useful pressure includes:
- Greg deciding what knowledge belongs to the party versus what only he should track;
- another trained member teaching a newcomer or correcting Greg;
- artifacts, markers, or low-level techniques beginning to reduce reacquisition/switching cost without becoming a magical network yet;
- Greg selectively repairing breadth failures exposed by Sel rather than becoming a generic support;
- contractors or Guild evaluators trying to formalize a method that depends on relationships and current state;
- stronger people asking for Greg by name while his official class continues to lag field reputation;
- calendar time, money, recovery, friendships, contract endings, and ordinary life continuing to make the climb feel lived.

Do not jump to A-class or mature linking. B-class should be earned through broader operational proof rather than granted as vindication for the earlier failure.
