## RECORD 025

## THE CONFESSOR

Nessa made me confess into a ledger.

Not sins.

That would have required more paper.

She made three columns.

MEMORY.

NOW.

MEANING.

I hated the third one immediately.

“This is stupid.”

“You ruined a man's interview because another version of him did something eight years from now.”

“When you say it like that.”

“How else should I say it?”

“With context.”

“That was the context.”

We were at the table.

Again.

Apparently every major development in my second life required bad furniture.

Verren's name sat at the top of the page.

I had told Nessa the story when I came home from Market Five.

Not because she asked.

That mattered.

Our old rule had been narrower.

If a memory changed a choice that touched her, tell her.

If I made a decision about our life because I was comparing it to the first one, tell her.

Reduced mysterious bullshit.

Verren did not belong to our household.

I could have kept it to myself.

I did not.

Growth or guilt.

Most growth looks like guilt before it learns better clothes.

Nessa listened while washing cups.

Then she said, “You need columns.”

“No.”

I had not even seen the columns yet.

Instinct was correct.

She opened a blank ledger.

Different from the household one.

Thank God.

Money already knew too much about me.

“Memory,” she said.

“I know what memory means.”

“Prove it.”

So I told her.

First life.

Verren Holt. Forty-six. Senior load assessor. False test readings. Delay avoided. Seven injured. Two deaths later. Hearing. License suspension.

Nessa wrote short phrases.

“Now.”

“Thirty-eight. Qualified. No discipline. Interview answers reasonable.”

She wrote each one.

“That sounds painful,” she said.

“Fuck you.”

“Continue.”

“Market Five. Fresh separation. Supervisor wanted access open. Verren closed it.”

Wrote.

“Put his own name on the decision.”

Wrote.

“Stair collapsed twenty-two minutes later.”

Wrote.

“No injuries.”

Wrote.

Then she tapped MEANING.

I leaned back.

“That is where the problem is.”

“Yes.”

“What do I write?”

“You tell me.”

“First-life Verren became willing to falsify safety evidence.”

Nessa shook her head.

“That is memory.”

“It is also meaning.”

“No. It happened.”

I hated that she was right.

“Fine.”

I thought.

“Current Verren might become the same kind of man.”

She wrote it.

Underlined might.

“Current Verren might not.”

She wrote that too.

“Market Five is evidence against treating the old behavior as fixed character.”

Wrote.

“Different history means I don't know what caused the first-life decision.”

Nessa stopped writing.

“That one.”

“What?”

“That is the one you keep forgetting.”

“I don't forget it.”

“You absolutely do.”

“I know outcomes are not mechanisms.”

“You say that about machines.”

I stopped.

East Four had taught me not to confuse a remembered disaster with understanding why it happened.

I had applied that lesson to walls, stations, sleeves, and load paths.

Then Verren walked into a room and I treated a person like his mechanism was obvious.

Bad man later.

Therefore bad man now.

People deserved more uncertainty than walls.

Annoying.

Nessa saw my face.

“You got there.”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

“I don't like it.”

“Also good.”

We kept the ledger.

Not for every memory.

That would have become another disease.

I remembered hundreds of useless things every week. Old food prices. Buildings that used to stand somewhere else. A Guild clerk who eventually became bald. Taverns under different owners. Rainfall from years that no longer matched.

We needed a threshold.

Nessa made one.

“If the memory makes you want to interfere with another person's choice, write it.”

“That is broad.”

“Yes.”

“If I remember someone walks into traffic tomorrow?”

“Interfere first. Write later.”

“Good.”

“If I remember someone becomes an asshole in ten years?”

“Write first.”

“Reasonable.”

“If I remember a station fails?”

“Current evidence?”

“I hate you.”

“Write that under meaning.”

The ledger became ridiculous quickly.

That helped.

MARA VEL.

MEMORY: none.

NOW: good operator, terrible handwriting.

MEANING: unfamiliar does not mean safe.

Nessa wrote that one after I admitted Mara felt easier to trust because no dead future version of her lived in my head.

NORTH RIVER PRINT.

MEMORY: closes years later. Cause and order uncertain.

NOW: profitable three years. Medical-printing contract. Lena floor manager.

MEANING: old closure does not authorize controlling Lena's job.

Nessa wrote ASSHOLE in the margin.

I crossed it out.

She wrote it again.

Then Bren.

I did not want to do that one.

Nessa knew.

“Then don't.”

I looked at the blank page.

“No. I should.”

Memory filled most of the column.

Friend.

Brother.

North Vey.

Ilya.

Gray Pass.

Death at fifty-three.

I stopped before details that belonged to Bren more than the exercise.

NOW was shorter.

A-class. Whole ankle. Different route. Worked East Four survey. Shook my hand. Left.

No current friendship.

MEANING stayed empty for a while.

Then I wrote:

I miss someone who is alive.

Nessa read it.

Did not correct me.

I added:

That does not create a debt.

She touched my wrist once.

Then turned the page.

The ledger was not therapy.

I want that stated clearly.

It was worse.

It was procedure.

Procedures survive moods.

A month later I used it without Nessa.

Halden sent a survey roster for West Two.

One name caught me.

Jalen Rusk.

First life, Jalen died in a mine collapse at thirty-four. I remembered the casualty notice because the collapse triggered a Ranker rescue contract I took afterward.

Second-life Jalen was thirty-three.

Assigned to West Two field support.

My first response was immediate.

Remove him.

Different worker.

No reason to risk it.

Easy.

Kind, even.

That is how old certainty dresses when it wants access.

I opened the ledger.

MEMORY.

Jalen Rusk dies in mine collapse around this age.

NOW.

Works district Line support. Not mining. West Two unrelated. North Vey divergence already changed regional contract patterns.

MEANING.

No current evidence West Two threatens him. Changing roster moves ordinary risk to somebody else. Remembered death does not make me his scheduler.

I left him on the roster.

Nothing happened.

That was the point.

He worked.

Complained about rain.

Lost a glove.

Found it in Tova's tool case.

Tova accused him of planting it there.

He denied everything.

Then he went home.

I did not save him.

I did not doom him.

I managed not to turn memory into authority for one day.

That counted.

At first Nessa had to pull the ledger out when I became strange.

Then I did it myself.

Then sometimes I ran the columns in my head.

Memory.

Now.

Meaning.

Three different things.

Years later those headings changed when other people needed something similar without hearing my impossible history.

PRIOR RECORD.

FIELD EVIDENCE.

WORKING THEORY.

That came later.

Do not give thirty-two-year-old me credit for inventing an evidence method at the kitchen table.

I was trying not to be an asshole to coworkers.

Scale arrived afterward.

One night Nessa came home late and found me at the table with the ledger open.

A contractor's name had triggered another first-life fraud memory.

Current record clean.

No action taken.

Nessa put her bag down.

“You doing columns?”

“Yes.”

“Need me?”

I looked at the page.

Then at her.

“No.”

She nodded.

“Good.”

That was all.

She went to wash.

I remember it because trust sometimes sounds like someone leaving the room.

Not checking.

Not approving.

Leaving you responsible for the thing you said you learned.

I finished the entry.

Closed the book.

Then made dinner badly.

Some systems improved faster than others.
