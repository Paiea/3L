## RECORD 125

## THE BAD QUESTION

I asked badly.

Ithar had requested it.

So.

“The western Line is failing below the maintenance layer.”

He waited.

Good start.

“Humans know how to repair the parts we can reach.”

Still.

“We do not know why the deep anchors are changing load preference.”

His eyes narrowed slightly.

There.

Interest.

“Changing how?”

“Returning pressure.”

“From?”

“Ground paths.”

“To?”

“Adjacent stations. Sometimes back into active bands.”

His head lifted.

More.

“Adaptive?”

“We do not know.”

“Alive?”

“No.”

I hesitated.

“Probably.”

His eyes changed.

Bad word around ancient beings.

Fair.

“Continue.”

I described Station Fourteen.

The east return.

The lower anchor.

The maintenance layer intact.

The foundation below it changing behavior nobody believed it possessed.

Then Nhal.

Not everything.

Enough.

Vey.

Containment.

Load-bearing prison.

How breaking one piece risked releasing more than a prisoner.

How the whole system behaved less like a cage and more like an argument held in tension.

Ithar stopped me.

“What did you call her?”

“Nhal.”

Stillness.

Actual.

Not dragon waiting.

Different.

His foreclaw stopped halfway through a small adjustment.

The cave became quiet around him.

I noticed.

Of course.

“You know her.”

Not a question.

Ithar looked at me.

“Continue.”

No.

Interesting.

“Nhal.”

His eyes narrowed.

“Greg.”

There.

Name.

Warning.

Good.

“You know her.”

“Yes.”

Simple.

Everything changed.

Not scale.

Not kingdom.

Not millions.

One name.

Nhal.

I sat straighter.

Ithar noticed.

Too late.

“How?”

“Continue.”

“Bad answer.”

His tail moved once.

Stone under it.

I had learned from Tracy.

Questions can wait.

Sometimes.

I continued.

Nhal’s form.

Not a formal species label.

Body built from too many predatory ideas.

Lion-like mass.

Spined tail.

Human-like intelligence.

Old.

Contained longer than anyone at Vey understood.

Pressure architecture built around her and something deeper.

Her warning.

Her gift.

Ithar’s hidden name.

The route.

Access conditions.

This cave.

Ithar went very still again.

“She gave you my name.”

“Yes.”

“Which?”

I told him.

Not the public dragon-name.

The old one.

The one Nhal had spoken once and made everyone else in the chamber feel like the air had changed.

Ithar closed his eyes.

Only for a moment.

When they opened, Greg was no longer the most important thing in the cave.

Good.

That was useful.

“How was she?”

The question surprised me.

Not:

What did she know?

Not:

What did she guard?

How was she.

I thought.

“Angry.”

His mouth shifted.

Maybe expected.

“Tired.”

Still.

“Interested.”

“In you?”

“Unfortunately.”

“Why?”

“I was useful.”

That almost amused him.

Good.

“She was trapped.”

“Yes.”

“Damaged?”

“I do not know.”

“Starved?”

“No.”

“Sleeping?”

“Sometimes.”

“Lucid?”

“Yes.”

“Always?”

“I do not know.”

His questions came faster now.

Not frantic.

Precise.

Personal.

“When did you last see her?”

“First Life.”

“How many years ago to you?”

I gave the number.

He hated it.

Not visibly.

Enough.

“And in this history?”

“I never went to Vey.”

His eyes sharpened.

There.

That mattered.

“Did anyone?”

“Different expedition history.”

“How different?”

“I do not know.”

Bad.

True.

Ithar’s claws pressed slightly into stone.

Not threat.

Thought.

“Nhal’s containment predates your first life.”

“Yes.”

“Your return does not erase what existed before you.”

“No.”

“Then unless your changes reached Vey deeply enough…”

“She may still be there.”

He looked at me.

“Yes.”

There.

Not abstract.

Nhal might be alive.

Now.

Trapped.

Ithar cared.

Good.

Maybe dangerous.

“What was she to you?”

I asked.

He looked at me.

“No.”

Fair.

“Friend?”

“No.”

“Enemy?”

“No.”

“Mate?”

His head moved so fast I almost laughed.

Bad.

“No.”

“Good.”

“Why good?”

“Would have complicated the climb.”

His eyes narrowed.

Worth it.

Then he said:

“She was Nhal.”

That was all.

Enough.

Some relationships resist category.

Good.

I understood.

Ithar looked toward the cave mouth.

Not seeing it.

Seeing Vey.

Maybe.

Then back to me.

“The Line.”

“Yes.”

“You believe the architecture resembles her containment.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Load returns.”

“Many systems return load.”

“Failure is redistributed instead of released.”

His eyes sharpened.

“Better.”

“Deep anchors appear to adapt.”

“Maybe.”

“Human maintenance layers sit above something older.”

“Yes.”

“The containment did too.”

“Yes.”

Ithar thought.

Then:

“They are not the same structure.”

Relief.

Too early.

“But?”

His eyes met mine.

“They may share a grammar.”

There.

That was enough to make everything worse.

“What grammar?”

“No.”

I stared.

“You know.”

“Yes.”

“Tell me.”

“No.”

I hated dragons.

“Why?”

“Because you do not yet know what you are asking me to help you alter.”

“That is why I climbed.”

“No.”

His voice remained calm.

“You climbed because humans are losing control of a machine they inherited.”

Fair.

“And because Nhal gave you a route.”

“Yes.”

“And because you want another beginning.”

There.

Personal question.

Back.

I looked away.

Ithar did not.

Good dragon.

Bad dragon.

“Those are three different requests.”

“They overlap.”

“Perhaps.”

“Do they?”

He ignored that.

Of course.

“What do you want first?”

Bad question.

No.

Good question.

I thought.

Line.

Millions.

Nhal.

Possibility.

Nessa.

Children.

Kelan.

Me.

Scale did not create obligation for Ithar.

He had said versions of that before.

I knew.

So did he.

I could say Line because it was morally largest.

Would not mean he cared most.

I could say Nhal because now I knew he cared.

Manipulative.

I could say me.

Honest.

Ugly.

I tried something else.

“I want to know whether the Line can be stabilized without making the deeper failure worse.”

Ithar watched me.

Good.

Real.

“And Nhal?”

“I want to know whether she is still there.”

His eyes changed.

“And if she is?”

“I do not know.”

Better.

“And yourself?”

There.

No safe version.

I looked at my hands.

“I want to know what happened to me.”

“That is not the same as wanting another life.”

“No.”

“Do you want another?”

Silence.

He waited.

Of course.

“I want the option to be real before I decide whether I want it.”

There.

Ugly.

True.

Ithar’s head lowered.

“Human.”

“Thank you.”

“Not praise.”

“Current data.”

He stared.

Wrong audience.

I smiled anyway.

Then he said:

“You assume options are harmless until used.”

I stopped smiling.

Good.

“No.”

“You trained Kelan around an option.”

There.

Fuck.

“You altered your own life around the possibility of another.”

“No.”

His eyes narrowed.

“Partly.”

Worse.

I looked away.

He continued:

“The Line is failing.”

“Yes.”

“Nhal may remain inside related architecture.”

“Yes.”

“You may have returned through a mechanism you do not understand.”

“Yes.”

“You ask whether I can help with all three.”

“Yes.”

“No.”

Silence.

Not refusal?

Maybe.

I waited.

Ithar shifted.

The cave floor trembled slightly under his weight.

Then:

“I can help you ask better.”

Annoying.

Useful.

“How?”

“We begin with Nhal.”

There.

Personal priority.

Not hidden now.

“Why?”

His eyes fixed on me.

“Because unlike your speculation about another life, she is either trapped or she is not.”

Fair.

“And unlike your Line, I know the age of the architecture that held her.”

There.

Important.

“You know it.”

“I know its kind.”

“Who built it?”

“No.”

“Ithar.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because names are not free.”

That sounded old.

Not metaphorically.

Ancient rule.

Useful.

He continued:

“If Nhal remains contained, reaching her without understanding the load may kill her.”

Good.

“Or free her.”

“No.”

His voice changed.

Harder.

“Do not confuse those.”

There.

Containment again.

Breaking is not freeing.

Important.

“What do we do?”

I asked.

Ithar looked at the cave wall.

Then me.

“You tell me everything she said.”

“I already told you most.”

“Everything.”

“Why?”

“Because Nhal lies carefully.”

I stared.

That sounded personal.

“Friend.”

“No.”

“Enemy.”

“No.”

“Ex.”

He showed teeth.

Good.

I stopped.

For now.

We began again.

Vey.

The chamber.

Pava.

The pressure rod.

Greg.

My hand.

Fuck.

Nhal.

Every word.

Every pause.

Every look I remembered.

The Line could wait one record.

Apparently so could reincarnation.

Nhal could not.
