## RECORD 128

## THE COMMON THING

“Continue.”

I looked at the two marks on the floor.

Seven days.

Forty years.

“That is all I remember.”

“No.”

Good start.

Ithar tapped the shorter mark.

“You fell into water.”

“Yes.”

“Did you breathe after?”

“I do not know.”

“Did you wake somewhere else?”

“Yes.”

“Earlier.”

“Yes.”

He tapped the longer mark.

“You linked three people inside an ancient structure.”

“Yes.”

“The structure took your Draw route.”

“Yes.”

“You experienced something you cannot classify.”

“Yes.”

“Then?”

I thought.

That memory did not like sequence.

Light through green stone.

Black veins.

Aren.

Tracy.

Pressure without texture.

Barrier responding before I understood what needed separating.

My Draw opening.

Then too much.

Not pain.

Not exactly.

The sense that everything attached to me had suddenly become farther away.

Then my mother asking if I wanted eggs.

“I woke at nineteen.”

“You skipped the end.”

“There was no end.”

“There was.”

“I do not remember it.”

“Better.”

I hated better.

Ithar looked at the marks.

“What do the events share?”

“Me.”

“More.”

“Return.”

“Outcome, not cause.”

“Something goes wrong.”

“Meaningless.”

“I stop experiencing one life.”

“Better.”

I stared at him.

“You want me to say death.”

“Yes.”

“No.”

His nostril opened.

Not amusement.

Maybe disappointment.

Good.

“I have nearly died more times than I can count.”

“Nearly is a human word invented for events that did not happen.”

“That is annoyingly accurate.”

“Yes.”

“I have been unconscious.”

“Not dead.”

“My heart stopped once.”

“How long?”

“Tracy said twelve seconds.”

“Did you return?”

“No.”

“Then perhaps your heart is not the relevant boundary.”

I rubbed my face.

“You are doing a lot of work to murder me.”

His eye shifted.

There.

I had meant it as a joke.

Mostly.

Ithar did not laugh.

Bad.

“I am identifying common conditions.”

“Death.”

“Possibly.”

“You have said possibly so many times it has stopped meaning anything.”

“It continues to mean exactly what it means.”

“Wonderful.”

His claw drew a small circle around the shorter mark.

“First event. You remember falling. No surface. No rescue. Then earlier.”

Another circle around the longer mark.

“Second event. Your route is taken by a structure operating beyond your comprehension. Then earlier.”

“You make my career sound insulting.”

“Your career is not relevant.”

“Worse.”

He ignored me.

“What happens when a system reaches an invalid state?”

There.

Line again.

“Fallback.”

“Sometimes.”

“Return.”

“Sometimes.”

“Failure route.”

“Yes.”

I looked at the marks.

“No.”

Ithar waited.

“I am not a machine.”

“No.”

“Good.”

“You are a system.”

I hated dragons.

“Fine.”

“What if your current state becomes invalid?”

“Define invalid.”

“No.”

“Ithar.”

“You are attempting to convert ignorance into vocabulary.”

That was rude.

Correct.

I looked away.

He continued.

“You experienced a state at nineteen.”

“Yes.”

“Later, a discontinuity occurred.”

“Twice.”

“Yes.”

“Both times, you found yourself nineteen.”

“Approximately.”

His eye sharpened.

There.

“What?”

“The first time I was still nineteen.”

“Yes.”

“Seven days earlier.”

“Yes.”

“The second time I went forty years.”

“Yes.”

“So nineteen may not be a single moment.”

“Good.”

Fuck.

He drew a longer line around both marks.

Not destination.

Range.

I watched.

“A valid region?”

I asked.

“Perhaps.”

“Age?”

“Perhaps.”

“Body state?”

“Perhaps.”

“Channel state?”

His claw stopped.

There.

That mattered.

I knew immediately.

At nineteen my channels were barely developed.

Not open.

Not closed.

Not anything yet.

Potential.

Uncommitted architecture.

Ithar saw my face change.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“No.”

I sighed.

“My channels.”

“Yes.”

“They were young.”

“Yes.”

“Before the routes hardened.”

“Yes.”

He said it too quickly.

“You already thought of that.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because you have spent several days explaining how much of your life was determined by where your channels learned to go.”

There.

First Life outward.

Second Life inward.

Current body carrying decisions made years ago.

At nineteen?

Almost none of it.

A body before the architecture chose.

“That could matter.”

“Yes.”

“Or not.”

“Yes.”

I hated accuracy.

Ithar looked toward the cave wall.

Not at anything.

Thinking.

Then:

“Barrier.”

“What about it?”

“You describe it badly.”

“That seems unfair.”

“You create walls.”

“Yes.”

“No.”

Here we go.

“I have used Barrier for eighty subjective years.”

“Yes.”

“I know what it does.”

“You know what you do with it.”

Different sentence.

Annoying.

I waited.

Ithar drew two small marks in the dust.

Separated.

“What is a barrier?”

“A division.”

“Better.”

“Between things.”

“Yes.”

“What is allowed across.”

“Yes.”

“What remains distinct.”

His eye sharpened.

There.

“Closer.”

I stared at the two marks.

Not wall.

Distinction.

A wall was one implementation.

A physical answer to a deeper question.

This remains here.

That remains there.

I hated that immediately.

“And Draw?”

I asked.

“Your word is also poor.”

“Of course.”

“What do you believe it does?”

“Moves load.”

“Sometimes.”

“Pulls.”

“Sometimes.”

“Transfers difference.”

His head moved.

There.

Good.

Bad.

I looked at the two marks again.

Barrier defines a difference.

Draw gives that difference somewhere to go.

I did not like where my mind went.

“No.”

Ithar said nothing.

“No.”

Still nothing.

“You think something defined nineteen.”

“I think something may have.”

“As what?”

“I do not know.”

“Valid.”

“Human word.”

“Saved.”

“Worse human word.”

“It communicates.”

“Poorly.”

“Still communicates.”

He allowed that through silence.

I continued.

“Then something happens later.”

“Yes.”

“Current state becomes…”

I stopped.

Dead?

Invalid?

Impossible?

Disconnected?

Too expensive?

None felt right.

Ithar did not help.

Good.

I tried again.

“The difference between current me and nineteen becomes routable.”

His head lowered.

Closer.

Fuck.

“And Draw sends it back.”

“No.”

I looked at him.

“No?”

“You are still thinking of yourself as cargo.”

I frowned.

“Then what moves?”

“I do not know.”

That answer from Ithar felt different.

Bigger.

Good.

He continued.

“Your memory persists.”

“Yes.”

“Your body does not.”

“Yes.”

“Your injuries do not.”

“Yes.”

“Your relationships do not.”

That one hit harder.

He knew.

Of course.

“Nessa does not.”

“No.”

“Children do not.”

“No.”

“Your rank does not.”

“No.”

“Then saying you moved backward explains almost nothing.”

I looked at my hands.

Young once.

Old now.

Young again once.

Maybe again?

No.

Stop.

“What persists?”

I asked.

“Enough of you to make the question dangerous.”

Good.

Terrible.

“Can you reproduce it?”

“No.”

Fast.

Useful.

“Can anyone?”

“I do not know.”

“Do you know what created the nineteen-state?”

“No.”

“Can you tell if it still exists?”

Ithar did not answer immediately.

That was new.

Greg noticed.

Of course Greg noticed.

“You can.”

“No.”

“That pause was terrible.”

“I can test consequences.”

“Meaning?”

“I may be able to determine whether your current architecture behaves as though an older distinction remains.”

I sat straighter.

“How?”

“Not yet.”

I hated not yet.

“Why?”

“Because you will interfere.”

“With what?”

“The test.”

“I am the test.”

“Exactly.”

Fair.

I looked back at the two marks.

Seven days.

Forty years.

“You think death triggers it.”

“I think death is the cleanest common hypothesis.”

“Cleanest.”

“Yes.”

“Not safest.”

“No.”

“Not proven.”

“No.”

“Not even particularly comforting.”

“That is not a scientific property.”

I laughed.

Could not help it.

Then stopped.

Because Ithar was still watching me.

“What?”

“You are less frightened than expected.”

“I have died once already by your hypothesis.”

“Twice.”

“Right.”

That was worse.

I looked toward the cave mouth.

Somewhere down the mountain was the rest of my life.

Kelan.

Tracy.

The Line.

People who would notice if I did not come back.

Maybe.

Eventually.

I looked at Ithar again.

“You are thinking about testing it.”

“Yes.”

There.

No hesitation.

I stared.

“With what?”

“You.”

“Fuck you.”

His nostril opened.

That one was amusement.

Good.

Bad.

“You want to kill me.”

“I want to know whether death returns you.”

“That is the same experiment from my side.”

“Yes.”

I laughed again.

This conversation had become ridiculous too gradually.

“You brought me into your cave, refused to save millions of people, interrogated eighty years of my life, and now your leading theory is murder.”

“I did not bring you here.”

“Technicality.”

“You came voluntarily.”

“Currently.”

Ithar’s eye narrowed.

Good.

I pointed at him.

“If you kill me and I stay dead, this was a bad hypothesis.”

“Yes.”

“That bothers you at all?”

“Yes.”

There.

Important.

“How much?”

“Enough that I have not killed you.”

I considered that.

Fair.

Not comforting.

“What would make you willing?”

“Evidence.”

“What kind?”

“More than you have given me.”

“Good.”

I meant that.

He noticed.

Then Ithar looked past me.

Into the hoard.

Not the Line.

Not Nhal.

The hoard.

His eyes moved once.

Twice.

Thinking.

“What?”

“Nothing yet.”

“That is my line.”

“It remains poor.”

He looked deeper into the gold.

I followed his gaze.

Coins.

Old weapons.

Boxes.

Stone.

Things kingdoms had stopped remembering.

“What are you looking for?”

Ithar did not answer.

Then one claw moved through the hoard.

Slowly.

Not playing this time.

Searching.

“There may be ways,” he said, “to make another beginning less wasteful.”

I stared at him.

“That is a very dangerous sentence.”

“Yes.”

His claw disappeared beneath the gold.

And kept going.
