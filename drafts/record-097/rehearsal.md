## RECORD 097

## THE CONSULTATION

The first consultation lasted fifty-three minutes.

Tracy charged for an hour.

I respected that.

Not immediately.

At minute fifty-four I said, “We’re done?”

“Yes.”

“I paid for an hour.”

“You paid for a consultation.”

“That was fifty-three minutes.”

“And?”

I looked at the clock.

Then at her.

“Seven minutes.”

“You are not buying time by weight.”

That sounded familiar.

Not from her.

From me.

Different life.

I sat back.

The room was small.

Not Guild space.

A rented treatment room above a pharmacy three streets from the branch.

Two chairs.

One examination table.

One cabinet.

One window that did not close properly.

Tracy had complained about the window before touching me.

Of course.

“Why rent this?” I asked.

“Cheaper than clinic space.”

“Bad window.”

“Yes.”

“Cold.”

“Yes.”

“Why not somewhere better?”

“Because I do two consultations a week.”

There.

Scale.

Current Tracy did not own buildings.

Did not run medical staff.

Did not have a half-share.

She rented fifty-three minutes at a time.

I had to remember that.

She had me remove the prosthetic.

Not dramatically.

Medically.

Socket off.

Liner off.

Residual limb inspected.

Skin.

Pressure points.

Scar.

Heat.

Swelling.

She touched the end of the stump with two fingers.

“Pain?”

“Yes.”

“Sharp?”

“No.”

“Burning?”

“Sometimes.”

“Now.”

“Pressure.”

She moved.

“Now?”

“Sharp.”

“Good.”

“That is not how I would describe it.”

“It means you can still tell the difference.”

Fair.

She had me stand.

Without the peg first.

Holding a rail.

Then with it.

Walk ten steps.

Turn.

Come back.

Again.

Slower.

Again.

She watched everything.

Not the leg.

Everything.

Shoulder.

Hip.

Hands.

Breathing.

Where my eyes went when balance changed.

“What?”

“You’re protecting the right side.”

“I’m missing part of it.”

“No.”

She pointed at my torso.

“You’re protecting the idea of the right side.”

I stared.

“That sounds fake.”

“It probably is.”

“Comforting.”

“Walk again.”

I did.

She stopped me halfway.

“You move your left shoulder before the turn.”

“I know.”

“You knew?”

“Old injury.”

“That is not what I asked.”

Right.

“I know it hurts.”

“You compensate before it hurts.”

I looked at her.

She looked back.

Not old Tracy.

Current Tracy.

Still right.

“Why?”

“Because falling is expensive.”

“Not financially.”

“Everything is financial eventually.”

There.

Early version.

Not yet polished.

Still her.

She wrote something.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Face.”

She looked up.

“What?”

“Never mind.”

Good.

I almost did it again.

Treating her like she owed me recognition of a shared joke.

She had no idea what face meant.

I let it go.

Progress.

She asked what rehabilitation I was doing.

I told her.

She asked frequency.

I lied.

Not technically.

“Most days.”

“How many?”

“Four.”

“That is not most.”

“Four is more than three.”

“There are seven days.”

“Unnecessarily many.”

She wrote something.

“Do not write ‘noncompliant.’”

“I wrote ‘annoying.’”

I looked.

She had.

Good.

“What do you actually want?” she asked.

We had covered this.

“Maintenance.”

“No.”

I waited.

She tapped the paper.

“You want a body that can survive a fight.”

“Yes.”

“With the man who took the leg.”

“Yes.”

“Say that.”

“I just did.”

“No. You keep calling it maintenance.”

I looked at her.

“Because that is what you do.”

“No.”

There.

Hard.

“This is what you keep doing.”

“What?”

“Deciding what I am before I say it.”

That landed worse.

I went quiet.

She noticed.

Good healer.

Not always kind.

“Who was she?”

I looked up.

“Who?”

“The person you keep talking to when you look at me.”

Fuck.

I did not answer.

Tracy waited.

Not old Tracy’s waiting.

Current Tracy’s.

Less patient.

More suspicious.

“You told me you knew someone like me.”

“I said that badly.”

“Yes.”

“Very badly.”

“Yes.”

“I knew you.”

Silence.

She did not move.

Then:

“No.”

“I know how that sounds.”

“No, I mean no.”

Fair.

“I knew a version of you.”

“That is worse.”

“Yes.”

“From where?”

I looked at the door.

Then the window.

Then her.

There were explanations that become lies because you give them before the listener has enough structure to hold them.

Reincarnation was one.

“I can’t explain it cleanly yet.”

“Then don’t.”

That surprised me.

“What?”

“You do not get to say something insane and then make me responsible for understanding it.”

Fair.

“Good.”

She pointed at the leg.

“We can talk about this.”

Body.

Current.

Real.

I nodded.

“Fine.”

“What is the goal?”

I thought.

Kill Hale.

Too broad.

Become dangerous.

Worse.

Rebuild S-class.

Impossible in the old way.

Not with this body.

Not quickly.

Maybe not ever.

“Stand without thinking about standing.”

That answer came out before I prepared it.

Tracy stopped.

Better answer.

“Good.”

Then:

“Walk without sacrificing the shoulder.”

“Good.”

“Run eventually.”

“Maybe.”

“Fight.”

“Later.”

“Very later.”

“Hale is alive.”

“So are you.”

I looked at her.

She continued.

“That is the only reason this consultation exists.”

Not moral.

Medical.

Useful.

“If you optimize every step around killing one man, you will make choices that are bad for the rest of your body.”

I knew.

That was the problem.

“What choices?”

“Too early to know.”

“Guess.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because guesses become plans around people like you.”

There.

Another lesson.

I almost smiled.

Stopped.

She noticed anyway.

“Why do you keep doing that?”

“Doing what?”

“Looking pleased when I disagree with you.”

Good question.

“Someone I trusted used to do that.”

“Disagree?”

“Constantly.”

“Sounds exhausting.”

“It was.”

“Then why are you pleased?”

Because you’re alive.

Because you’re still yourself.

Because some things survived without surviving me.

I looked at the floor.

“Because it helps.”

She did not understand.

Did not pretend.

Good.

We moved to channels.

That was where the consultation changed.

Most healers looked at my current execution and saw an injured low-rank support with unusually precise control.

Tracy did too.

Then she asked why some pathways were overdeveloped for the amount of output I could sustain.

I gave a bad answer.

“Old habits.”

“You are not old.”

“Spiritually.”

She stared.

“Thirty-something is not old.”

“Debatable.”

“No.”

She examined the channel mapping again.

Not magical imaging exactly.

Pressure response.

Flow tests.

Barrier placement.

Small Draw.

Reinforcement.

She had me repeat the same cheap boundary twelve times.

By the ninth, my control sharpened.

By the twelfth, the shoulder started compensating.

She stopped.

“Again.”

“You said stop.”

“I changed my mind.”

I did another.

The placement was worse.

“There.”

“What?”

“You know the spell better than your body knows the spell.”

Silence.

That was closer than she knew.

“How?”

“Your correction happens before the mistake.”

I looked at her.

She continued.

“You anticipate failure patterns you should not have enough experience to anticipate.”

I said nothing.

She leaned back.

“Interesting.”

Finally.

Not suspicion.

Professional interest.

Better.

“What does that mean?”

“It means your head is ahead of your tissue.”

Yes.

Exactly.

“That is dangerous.”

Of course.

“You will repeatedly feel capable of things your body has not earned.”

“Yes.”

“You already knew that.”

“Yes.”

“Then why are you here?”

Because I needed someone to make it expensive to lie to myself.

I did not say that.

Not yet.

“Because knowing is not the same as doing.”

Tracy nodded.

Good answer.

She wrote three things.

Then turned the paper toward me.

1. SOCKET FIT  
2. SHOULDER LOAD  
3. CHANNEL ENDURANCE

“That is it?”

“For now.”

“Hale?”

“No.”

“Combat?”

“No.”

“Speed?”

“No.”

“Power?”

“No.”

“This is boring.”

“Yes.”

“I hate it.”

“Good.”

“Why good?”

“Because boring things are usually cheap.”

There.

There she was.

Not the woman I remembered.

The principle.

Earlier.

Smaller.

Current.

I looked at the list.

“Three things.”

“Yes.”

“I have more problems than three.”

“I know.”

“Twelve, maybe.”

“I know.”

“You have not asked.”

“I do not care yet.”

I almost laughed.

She pointed at the paper.

“Thursday is before next year.”

That sentence landed hard.

Not because I remembered it.

I did not.

Because it was exactly the kind of thing old Tracy would have said.

Maybe she had always been this person.

Maybe I had only met her later.

That mattered.

“Fine.”

“Do these.”

“How long?”

“Two weeks.”

“Then?”

“Then we see.”

“Can I accelerate?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Tissue.”

“Magic.”

“Tissue.”

“Channels.”

“Tissue.”

“Annoying.”

“Yes.”

She stood.

Consultation over.

Fifty-three minutes.

I looked at the clock.

Seven minutes remained.

“Any food recommendations?”

She looked at me.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

Good.

Do not force old rituals.

I put the prosthetic back on.

She watched.

Adjusted one strap.

Tiny correction.

Immediate improvement.

I hated how much.

“How did you see that?”

“You keep touching it.”

“I do not.”

“You touched it five times.”

“Did I?”

“Yes.”

Current data.

Not memory.

Earned.

At the door she said:

“Greg.”

I turned.

“If the goal changes, tell me.”

“What?”

“If this stops being rehabilitation and becomes preparation for violence, I need to know.”

I looked at her.

“It is both.”

“No.”

“What?”

“It can become both.”

She pointed at me.

“Right now it is rehabilitation.”

I wanted to argue.

Old reflex.

Old relationship.

Wrong life.

I looked at the list.

Three things.

“Okay.”

Tracy nodded.

That was the first time I listened because she asked.

Not because I remembered eventually learning to.

Small difference.

Important.

Ithar’s tail shifted in the cave.

“You paid for fifty-three minutes.”

“Yes.”

“And obeyed?”

“For two weeks.”

“That is not what I asked.”

“No.”

Fair.

“Mostly.”

He waited.

I sighed.

“Fourteen days is a long time when someone you hate remains alive.”

“What happened on day nine?”

Of course he knew there was a day nine.

Dragon.

Annoying.

“I tested something.”

“Combat?”

“Not exactly.”

“What?”

I looked at my hands.

“The first piece of the thing that eventually cost me my old support ceiling.”
