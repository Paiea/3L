## RECORD 092

## THE PERSON

Rellan asked the question badly.

That helped.

We were at Marrin’s.

Of course.

Late.

One of the back tables.

Tracy had gone upstairs to argue about a medical supply contract.

Arcrutus was eating something he had not paid for.

Again.

Rellan had come from an Authority review.

He looked tired.

That made him less polished.

I liked him better that way.

“You know what people say about you.”

“No.”

“You absolutely do.”

“I know many things people say about me.”

“That is not an answer.”

“Then ask the question.”

He did.

“If I take all this away, what are you?”

I looked around.

Restaurant.

Staff.

Upstairs offices.

People moving through.

Our transport schedules pinned behind one door.

Medical records behind another.

Tracy somewhere above us making a supplier regret waking up.

“What is all this?”

“You know.”

“Then define your question.”

Rellan leaned back.

“Take away Tracy.”

“Rude.”

“The yard.”

“Also rude.”

“The washhouse.”

“Expensive.”

“The contracts.”

“Very.”

“The people who answer when you call.”

“Now you are just making me lonely.”

Arcrutus chewed.

Rellan ignored him.

“Take it away.”

I waited.

“What are you?”

“Worse.”

That stopped him.

Not because the answer was clever.

Because he expected resistance.

Rellan frowned.

“You admit it.”

“Of course.”

“Then how much of what people call your power is actually yours?”

I looked at Arcrutus.

He raised one shoulder.

No help.

Typical.

“Depends what you mean by mine.”

Rellan sighed.

“Do not do philosophy.”

“I wasn’t planning to.”

“Good.”

He leaned forward.

“You are S-class.”

“Yes.”

“You earned that before any of this.”

“Yes.”

“Vey.”

“Yes.”

“Fine.”

He tapped the table.

“But now people talk about Greg as if you can move freight, field healers, secure buildings, source medicine, place crews, call Authority offices, and make contracts appear.”

“I can.”

“No. You know people who can.”

“Yes.”

“That is different.”

“Yes.”

Again.

He hated when I agreed.

That was becoming useful.

“So where is the line?”

“I don’t know.”

“That bothers me.”

“Why?”

“Because rank is supposed to describe a person.”

There it was.

Rellan’s actual objection.

Not jealousy.

Not only.

His magic reinforced a body.

His training sharpened a body.

His power could be demonstrated in a room.

Put him in front of a wall.

He broke the wall.

Put him in front of a monster.

He fought it.

Put him in front of an evaluator.

There he was.

Legible.

I had never been that.

Even before the businesses.

Support required other people to become fully visible.

My best work had always happened in relationships.

Draw.

Barrier.

Load.

Signals.

Trust.

Bodies learning bodies.

The organization only made the problem larger.

“What happens if you lose your spear?” I asked.

Rellan looked at me.

“I get another.”

“So the spear is not you.”

“No.”

“Armor?”

“Equipment.”

“Training?”

He frowned.

“What?”

“Take away every teacher.”

“That already happened.”

“No. Take away what they taught you.”

“That is nonsense.”

“Why?”

“Because it is in me.”

“Good.”

I pointed upstairs.

“What Tracy taught me is in me.”

“That is not the same.”

“Some of it is.”

“The wagons are not in you.”

“Thankfully.”

Arcrutus made a noise.

Possibly laughter.

Rellan continued.

“You know what I mean.”

“I do.”

That mattered.

He was not wrong.

There was a difference between learned capacity and accessible infrastructure.

Between my body and my phone book.

We did not call it a phone book.

Wrong life.

Between my body and the list of people who would answer.

Rellan said, “If I arrive alone, I am still me.”

“Yes.”

“If you arrive alone?”

“I am still me.”

“You are less.”

“Yes.”

He stared.

I shrugged.

“So are you.”

“Not like that.”

“No.”

That was true too.

I did not need to win every distinction.

Rellan looked almost relieved.

Then suspicious.

“Why are you agreeing?”

“Because you are right.”

“Then why does nobody around you act like it matters?”

I thought about that.

“Because alone is not how I usually arrive.”

Silence.

Arcrutus stopped chewing.

Interesting.

Rellan’s expression changed.

Not anger.

Recognition.

Maybe that was the line he had been looking for.

I continued.

“You train so that if everything falls away, you still have the highest possible individual capability.”

“Yes.”

“I train that too.”

“Not enough.”

“Probably.”

He almost smiled.

“But I also spend money and time making sure everything does not fall away.”

He looked around.

The restaurant became different in the silence.

Not luxury.

Redundancy.

Relationships.

A place people knew.

A place where information moved.

A place where you could find somebody before you needed them.

Tracy came downstairs carrying papers.

Saw Rellan.

Saw me.

Saw Arcrutus stealing food.

“Why does this feel annoying?”

“Rellan is trying to define Greg.”

“No.”

She kept walking.

Rellan turned.

“No?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Waste of time.”

He looked offended.

Good.

Tracy stopped at our table.

“Define him for what?”

Rellan paused.

There.

She had narrowed it.

“For rank.”

“Authority already did.”

“For power.”

“What problem?”

He looked at me.

I smiled.

Mentorship.

Rellan frowned.

“I am asking how much of his operational capability belongs to him versus the organization.”

Tracy nodded.

“Better.”

Then she set the supplier papers down.

“For which operation?”

Rellan stared.

She waited.

Field fight?

Emergency response?

Marsh extraction?

Regional logistics?

Poison event?

Political negotiation?

Three-person contract?

Hundred-person mobilization?

The answer changed.

That was her point.

She took a piece of Arcrutus’s food.

He objected.

She ignored him.

Rellan said, “That is exactly the problem.”

“No,” Tracy said. “That is exactly the answer.”

He looked at her.

She chewed.

“Capability is contextual.”

“Rank exists to simplify context.”

“Yes.”

“So we need a useful simplification.”

“Then use one.”

“What?”

“Greg is S-class support.”

Rellan’s jaw tightened.

“We know.”

“Good.”

She pointed around the room.

“This is something else.”

There.

Simple.

I liked it immediately.

My support was mine.

The organization was not my rank.

It was not proof I deserved S.

Vey had already done that.

This was what happened after.

Money.

People.

Trust.

Contracts.

Properties.

Capacity external to the body.

Rellan looked at me.

“So you agree this does not make you more S-class.”

“Yes.”

Another surprise.

“Then what does it make you?”

I thought.

“Harder to remove.”

Arcrutus laughed.

Actually laughed.

Rellan did too after a second.

Tracy did not.

She was reading the supplier papers again.

“Not funny,” she said.

“Why?”

“Because if he gets removed, I inherit all of you.”

Arcrutus looked at her.

“Clerk.”

“Old man.”

He smiled.

That was new.

Rellan looked between us.

Then back at me.

“You know people are starting to call this a syndicate.”

“Yes.”

“Does that bother you?”

“No.”

Tracy said, “Their asset estimates bother me.”

Of course.

Rellan shook his head.

Then became serious again.

“There is another version of this.”

“What?”

“Take away the organization and see what remains.”

I looked at him.

He meant it as a test.

Maybe even a warning.

Years later I would do something worse.

I would take away the organization myself.

Not immediately.

Not in First Life.

Second Life.

After Nessa.

After the leg.

After Hale.

I would look at everything First-Life Greg had built outward and decide I needed a kind of power nobody could remove by taking other people away.

Rellan had no idea he was describing my future mistake.

Neither did I.

At the time I only said:

“That sounds lonely.”

He nodded.

“It is.”

Then Tracy pushed the supplier contract toward me.

“Sign this.”

I looked at it.

“What is it?”

“Cheaper medical glass.”

“Good?”

“Yes.”

“Enough?”

“Yes.”

I signed.

Rellan stared.

“You did not read that.”

“Tracy did.”

“That is insane.”

“Probably.”

Tracy took the paper back.

“Profitable insanity.”

That was our answer then.
