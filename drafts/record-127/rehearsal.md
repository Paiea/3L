## RECORD 127

## THE FIRST TIME

“That,” Ithar said, “is a better question.”

Good.

I hated it.

I looked at the Line scratched into the floor.

Return paths.

Fallback.

A system changing because something underneath it had changed first.

Nhal’s containment.

My closed loop.

Too many things suddenly using the same verbs.

Ithar erased the Line with one claw.

Stone dust gathered beneath the black point.

I stared.

“That took you a while to draw.”

“Yes.”

“And now?”

“Now I have what I needed.”

“I would have liked to keep it.”

“You remember it.”

“Apparently that is becoming less reassuring.”

His claw stopped.

Then moved to a clean section of stone.

No diagram.

Just one short line.

“What did you do before you returned?”

I knew which return he meant.

I disliked that immediately.

“We were discussing the Line.”

“We are.”

“No, we are discussing me again.”

“Yes.”

“That is different.”

“Only if your return is unrelated.”

There.

Of course.

I leaned back against the stone.

“My first life ended in a ruin.”

“Ended.”

“Yes.”

“You died.”

“I do not know.”

“You ceased experiencing that body.”

“Yes.”

“And next experienced yourself at nineteen.”

“Yes.”

“That is sufficient for now.”

“No, it is not.”

“For me.”

Dragons.

I rubbed at my jaw.

The beard had become irritating on the mountain.

Everything had.

“Fine.”

“What did you do immediately before?”

“Support.”

“Meaningless.”

“Linked the team.”

“Better.”

“How many?”

“Four.”

His eye shifted.

There.

Fuck.

I had told him three before.

Not directly.

Close enough.

“Four,” he repeated.

“Yes.”

“Names.”

“No.”

His head lowered slightly.

“Why?”

“Because they are not relevant.”

“That is rarely why you refuse a name.”

I said nothing.

He waited.

I hated patient creatures.

“Tracy.”

Still.

“Aren.”

Still.

“One more.”

Still.

“And me.”

His eye narrowed.

“That is four humans including you.”

“Yes.”

“How many external links?”

“Three.”

There.

Correct.

Better.

“Stable?”

“Mostly.”

“Mostly is not a measurement.”

“Stable enough that none were failing when the chamber shifted.”

“Chamber.”

“Yes.”

“What chamber?”

“Old structure.”

“What kind?”

“We did not know.”

“Material?”

I frowned.

“Stone.”

“Color?”

“Gray.”

“No.”

I looked at him.

“No?”

“You corrected yourself internally.”

I stared.

“How would you know that?”

“Your breathing changed.”

Fuck dragons.

“Green.”

His eye sharpened.

“Green what?”

“Translucent.”

“How translucent?”

“Enough that light carried through the edges.”

“Veining?”

I stopped.

There.

Black.

Thin black lines inside the material.

I had forgotten those.

No.

Not forgotten.

Filed under decorative.

“Black.”

Ithar went very still.

Interesting.

“Why?”

“Continue.”

“No. You did the thing.”

“What thing?”

“The useful stillness.”

“I am always useful.”

“Debatable.”

His nostril moved.

Good.

Not enough.

“What does green with black veining mean?”

“Nothing yet.”

Bad answer.

He continued before I could push.

“You linked three people.”

“Yes.”

“Barrier?”

“Yes.”

“Draw?”

“Yes.”

“Sequence?”

I thought.

That memory came differently.

Hands.

Pressure.

Aren swearing somewhere to my left.

Tracy saying my name without raising her voice.

Not fear.

Instruction.

A sound beneath us.

Not cracking.

Changing.

“Barrier first.”

“Why?”

“The room was separating.”

“Physically?”

“Yes.”

“No.”

I stared at him.

“You are very confident for someone who was not there.”

“Were the walls moving?”

“No.”

“Floor?”

“No.”

“Ceiling?”

“No.”

“Then what was separating?”

I looked at the scratched stone between us.

I had called it pressure.

At fifty-nine, I had called many things pressure because pressure was a useful word.

The chamber had developed boundaries where there had not been boundaries before.

Sections of space had stopped agreeing with each other.

My Barrier responded.

Not because I cast a wall.

Because I recognized separation.

Fuck.

“I do not know.”

“Better.”

“I hate when you say that.”

“I know.”

Ithar settled lower.

Not relaxed.

Focused.

“What did Draw do?”

“Nothing at first.”

“Then?”

“Too much.”

His eye narrowed.

There.

“How much?”

“I do not know.”

“From where?”

“I do not know.”

“To where?”

“I do not know.”

“Greg.”

“I do not know.”

He waited.

I heard my own answer.

Three times.

Bad.

True.

Maybe.

I looked away.

“The structure took the route.”

His head moved slightly.

There.

“What route?”

“Mine.”

“Explain.”

“I opened Draw because the separated sections were building differential.”

“You attempted to equalize them.”

“Yes.”

“Through yourself.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“That was my job.”

“No.”

Fuck him.

“That was what I was good at.”

“Better.”

I rubbed my palm against my knee.

Old habit.

Wrong leg.

Still mine.

“The room found the route faster than I expected.”

“Room?”

“Structure.”

“Better.”

“It pulled.”

“Mana?”

“No.”

His eye sharpened.

There.

I had never said that out loud.

Not even to Tracy.

Especially not Tracy.

“What?”

“I do not think it was mana.”

“Why?”

“Mana has texture.”

“Human word.”

“You know what I mean.”

“Yes.”

“This did not.”

“Force?”

“No.”

“Heat?”

“No.”

“Memory?”

I looked at him.

He waited.

Fuck.

“I do not know.”

“You reacted.”

“I react to many stupid questions.”

“That was not one.”

Silence.

I looked toward the cave mouth.

The passage remained useless.

Good consistency.

Ithar spoke again.

“Had you experienced return before that chamber?”

“No.”

The answer came immediately.

Too immediately.

Ithar’s eye stayed on me.

I stayed on him.

Nothing moved.

Good.

Then he said:

“How many days?”

My stomach dropped.

Not much.

Enough.

“What?”

“How many days did you lose the first time?”

I said nothing.

Ithar waited.

There are lies that collapse because someone proves them wrong.

There are others that collapse because the other person asks the question behind them.

This was worse.

“I did not lose days.”

“Return, then.”

“Ithar.”

“How many?”

I looked at the floor.

The erased Line.

The remaining short mark.

Fucking dragon.

“Six.”

His head moved.

“No.”

I looked up.

“What?”

“You thought seven.”

I stared at him.

“You are guessing.”

“Yes.”

Fuck.

I looked away again.

“Seven.”

Silence.

Ithar did not celebrate.

That annoyed me.

“You baited me.”

“Yes.”

“You did not know.”

“No.”

“You just picked a number.”

“Yes.”

I laughed once.

Mean.

“Good talk.”

“Continue.”

I hated him.

Seven days.

Maybe.

That was the problem.

It had happened forty years before the forty-year return.

Then forty more years after.

Eighty years of memory had rounded the edges until even I could not tell whether it had been six days, seven, or a fever dream stitched across both.

“At nineteen,” I said.

Ithar’s eye narrowed.

“How old?”

“Nineteen.”

“Before Guild Hall?”

I looked at him.

That question hit harder than it should have.

“No.”

True.

Good.

“After?”

“Yes.”

“How long after?”

“Days.”

“How many?”

“I do not remember exactly.”

“Approximately.”

“Less than two weeks.”

“And you returned seven days.”

“Something like that.”

“To where?”

“Home.”

“Your bed?”

“Yes.”

“Same morning?”

“No.”

There.

Important.

Ithar leaned closer.

Not much.

Dragon not much.

“What morning?”

I thought.

Rain.

My mother yelling from the kitchen.

My father already at work.

My sister arguing about something through the wall.

No Guild Hall.

Not that morning.

Later.

A different morning.

“I had already registered.”

“Yes.”

“I already had the ridge contract.”

“Yes.”

“You returned to a point after obtaining it.”

“Yes.”

My first little impossible event had not sent me to the beginning of adulthood.

Just backward.

Seven days.

Ithar asked:

“What killed you?”

“I do not know that I died.”

“Last memory.”

There.

That one I had.

Water.

Not a lake.

Dark.

Cold.

A pressure door.

A hand slipping.

Someone shouting.

Then my ceiling.

“I fell.”

“From?”

“An access bridge.”

“Into?”

“Water.”

“Depth?”

“Enough.”

“Did you surface?”

“I do not remember.”

“Did anyone recover your body?”

“Ithar.”

“Did anyone tell you what happened after?”

“No.”

“Because?”

“I woke before it happened.”

There.

Simple.

Ridiculous.

His eye held mine.

“And you decided this was stress.”

“I decided I had dreamed several days.”

“Seven.”

“Yes.”

“Accurately.”

“Not accurately.”

“What differed?”

“Things.”

“Name one.”

“Kett.”

“The foreman.”

“Yes.”

“What changed?”

“He told the same joke.”

Ithar waited.

I hated that this was the example.

“What joke?”

“No.”

“Greg.”

“It was about a goat.”

“Continue.”

“I knew the end.”

“And?”

“I said it before him.”

“What happened?”

“He called me an asshole.”

Ithar said nothing.

“That proves very little.”

“No.”

“Then a cart axle broke.”

“You knew?”

“Yes.”

“You prevented it?”

“Yes.”

“And?”

“Then the next day stopped matching.”

There.

That was the whole thing.

I had changed one stupid axle.

The sequence diverged.

Rain came at a different hour.

Someone took a different shift.

The access bridge inspection moved.

The accident never happened.

No second proof.

No body.

No witnesses.

No artifact glowing in my hand.

Just seven days I remembered that nobody else did.

At nineteen, that was terrifying for approximately a month.

Then expensive.

Then inconvenient.

Then life.

“I thought I had dreamed it,” I said.

“You did not.”

“You do not know that.”

“No.”

Good.

At least he admitted it.

“But you no longer believe that.”

I looked at him.

“No.”

“When did you stop?”

There.

That answer was easy.

“When I woke at nineteen again.”

Ithar became completely still.

Not Nhal stillness.

Not useful-word stillness.

Different.

He was assembling something.

I watched him do it.

That was new.

Usually I was the thing being assembled.

“What?” I asked.

He ignored me.

His claw touched the short line he had drawn.

Then he made another.

Seven days.

Then another much farther away.

Forty years.

Two returns.

Same person.

Different scale.

He looked at me.

“Your dungeon was irrelevant.”

I frowned.

“What?”

“The place where your first life ended.”

“We do not know that.”

“No.”

“Then why say it?”

“Because you have treated location as cause twice.”

I stopped.

The access bridge.

The final chamber.

Different places.

Different structures.

Maybe different continents.

I had spent decades assuming the second place mattered because the second return was enormous.

But the first return had happened somewhere else.

Ithar tapped the two lines.

“What is common?”

“Me.”

“Yes.”

“Death.”

“Possibly.”

“Draw.”

His eye narrowed.

“Was it present the first time?”

I thought.

Young Greg.

Barely trained.

No mature Draw architecture.

But the ridge crew had used basic pressure work.

I had touched the mechanism.

Maybe.

“I do not know.”

“Barrier?”

“I do not know.”

“Artifact?”

“No.”

“You are certain?”

“No.”

“Good.”

I hated good.

I looked at the two marks.

Seven days.

Forty years.

Then a thought arrived.

Bad.

Very bad.

“Ithar.”

“Yes.”

“What if nineteen is not where it sends me?”

His eye sharpened.

Good.

He understood.

“What if nineteen is where I was already saved?”

Neither of us spoke.

There.

Ugly word.

Wrong word.

Useful word.

Saved.

A valid state.

A boundary.

Something that had persisted while everything else changed.

Ithar looked at the marks again.

Then at me.

For the first time since I entered the cave, the dragon looked excited.

Only slightly.

Enough.

“You’re excited.”

“No.”

I stared.

His eye did not move.

“That was a lie.”

“Yes.”

I smiled.

There.

Finally.

“Good talk.”

Ithar’s nostril opened.

“Continue.”
