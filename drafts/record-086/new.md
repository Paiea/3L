## RECORD 086

## THE NETWORK

Pava was dead.

The load was not.

Survey said, "Six counts."

Then, "Seven."

Mael looked at the lower bridge.

Not at Pava.

That looked cruel until I understood what he was doing.

If he looked at her, he might become a person for thirty seconds.

We did not have thirty seconds.

"What failed?" he asked.

Survey answered.

"Nothing physical yet. Load is migrating back toward the original center path."

Structural checked the flex plate.

"Bridge is holding."

"Then why rise?"

I knew.

I did not want to.

"Pava wasn't only carrying force."

Everyone looked at me.

"She was correcting the bridge before it moved. The plate damps after motion starts. Pava kept some motion from starting."

Arcrutus stared at the place she had been sitting.

The healer still had one hand on Pava's shoulder.

Mael said, "Can you replace that function?"

No.

Correct answer.

I almost said it.

Then I looked at the network we still had.

Arcrutus.

Mael.

Two rails.

Three keys, one damaged.

Flex plate.

Expedition anchors.

Survey instruments.

Structural's map.

Nhal above us.

One freed foreleg.

A tail relation older than the prison.

And me.

Could I become Pava?

No.

Stupid question.

Could all of us together reproduce the one function the system currently missed?

Different question.

"Maybe."

Mael said, "Use fewer words than Greg usually requires."

I pointed at Survey.

"She sees movement earliest with instruments."

Survey frowned.

"Pava felt it earlier than instruments."

"Yes. So we make the instruments earlier."

Structural said, "How?"

"More of them. Closer to the failure points. We stop asking one trace to describe the whole fracture."

Mael said, "Then?"

I pointed at Arcrutus.

"Raw correction west."

At Mael.

"East."

At the rails.

"Residual into physical bridge."

Arcrutus said, "That is what we already have."

"No. Before, Pava decided what correction meant. We were force sources around her."

He looked at Pava.

Then back to me.

"Now who decides?"

I hated the answer.

"Nobody alone."

Silence.

Survey understood first.

"Distributed calls."

I nodded.

"You call direction and magnitude change. Structural calls bridge tolerance. Mael decides operation response. I move the correction into whichever source can take it without breaking geometry."

Mael said, "Latency."

Good.

That was the problem.

Humans talking are slower than Pava being Pava.

"Short calls," I said. "No explanations. Left. Right. Rise. Fall. Hold. Stop."

Survey said, "Still slower."

"Yes."

Structural said, "Then we predict from precursor vibration."

Everyone looked at him.

He pointed at the wall.

"We had vibration before translation. We ignored it because Pava was already correcting."

Survey moved immediately.

Good.

Three instruments became six.

Then eight.

Two went on the bridge.

Two on the east face.

Two west.

One above the hinge collar.

One under the ledge where path twelve had failed.

The riggers ran them while the rest of us kept the existing hold.

Nhal's tail trace climbed another count.

Eight.

The speaking tube from above carried a voice.

Not healer.

Nhal.

Rough.

"Greg."

First time she had said my name.

Bad time to appreciate it.

I went to the tube.

"Here."

Pause.

Then:

"Not... one."

I closed my eyes.

Of course.

Not one.

Not one carrier.

Not Pava.

Not Nhal.

Not me.

I looked at Mael.

He had heard.

"Build it," he said.

Not command of the expedition.

Command to me.

My job.

Finally clear.

I lowered my aura.

The network was ugly.

Good.

Pretty systems are usually hiding labor.

I opened narrow relations between the west brace, east brace, physical bridge, and three pressure anchors we had previously kept separate.

Not full links.

Permissions.

If west correction rises beyond this threshold, east does not mirror it automatically.

Bridge accepts only what does not change angle.

Anchor one accepts short recoil.

Anchor two accepts sustained low correction.

Anchor three stays unused unless Mael calls emergency.

Arcrutus said, "What do I do?"

"Nothing until called."

He hated that.

"Then why am I here?"

"Because when called you can move a building."

He considered.

"Fair."

Survey called, "East vibration rise."

Structural said, "Bridge neutral."

Mael said, "East two."

I moved the call into his brace.

Mael loaded.

The vibration fell.

Survey said, "Good."

Then west.

Arcrutus took it.

Too much.

"Back one."

He backed one.

The bridge stayed centered.

Small.

Human.

Slow compared with Pava.

Working.

Another precursor.

Then another.

We started catching movement before translation.

Not because any one of us could feel what Pava felt.

Because eight instruments, three people watching them, one structural specialist, two S-class force sources, and one support who hated himself were collectively almost as fast.

Almost.

That word did a lot of work.

At the fifth correction, Survey called late.

The fracture shifted.

Flex plate caught it.

Load jumped toward Nhal.

Tail trace nine.

Nhal made a sound above us.

Arcrutus's head snapped toward the tube.

"Again," he said.

Not to her.

To us.

We adjusted.

Survey moved one instrument farther east.

Structural changed the vibration threshold.

I shortened the path from call to response by pre-opening two conditional relations.

Dangerous.

Faster.

Mael approved them one at a time.

Next correction caught earlier.

Then earlier again.

Tail trace eight.

Then seven.

Good.

The healer finally spoke.

"I need to move Pava."

No one answered for a second.

Wrong kind of sentence in the middle of mechanics.

Mael looked at the body.

Then at the bridge.

"Can we?"

He asked me.

Not because I was leader.

Because I was holding the relationships.

I checked.

Arcrutus west.

Mael east.

Bridge stable.

Two anchors active.

One reserved.

Could Arcrutus move?

No.

Could Mael?

No.

Could the healer and riggers carry her without crossing an active boundary?

Yes.

I marked a path on the floor.

"Here. Stay inside this line. Do not touch the west plate."

Healer nodded.

Two riggers came.

They had both worked with Pava for the whole expedition.

I had forgotten their names twice.

They had not forgotten hers.

They lifted her carefully.

Arcrutus stared straight ahead.

His jaw moved once.

Mael did not look.

Again.

People become cruel-looking when grief has to wait.

The healer carried Pava up.

The load changed while they were halfway to the stairs.

Of course.

Survey called west rise.

Arcrutus took it.

His eyes never left the stair opening.

"More," I said.

He gave more.

"Hold."

He held.

Pava disappeared upward.

The bridge stayed.

That was the first impossible thing after she died.

Not magic.

Continuing.

We held the distributed correction for forty minutes.

Then an hour.

The system kept trying new balances.

Some were old failures finding us.

Some were responses to our bridge.

Some may have been Nhal changing her own distribution above.

We learned to ask.

The rods could not reach us below, so Nhal used the tension cord.

One pull for yes.

Two for no.

Three for not like that.

A terrible language.

Enough.

Survey would call a shift.

I would ask up the tube, "Yours?"

One pull.

Then we did not fight it.

Or two.

Then we corrected.

Once, three.

We misunderstood the question.

Nhal said through the tube, slowly:

"Mine... and... yours."

I laughed.

Could not help it.

"Yes. That is unfortunately the whole problem."

No one else laughed.

Fair.

By the second hour, I had stopped thinking of the system as supports.

It was people and objects with different limits.

Arcrutus had enormous force and bad delicacy.

Mael had control, endurance, and a knee that was beginning to fail under the east angle.

The rails had persistence but no judgment.

Anchors had clean capacity and no recovery.

Survey had information but not authority.

Structural knew what the bridge could survive but not what Nhal could.

Nhal could feel relationships none of us could detect but was still bound inside the thing we were changing.

I had almost none of the best traits.

What I had was access between them.

That was enough.

For a while.

At hour two and nineteen minutes, Mael's knee buckled.

Not fully.

Enough.

He said, "Change east source."

Arcrutus said, "I can take both."

"No," I said.

He looked at me.

"You literally said I can move a building."

"Wrong shape."

He swore.

Good.

We moved east correction into two physical anchors and Mael's upper-body brace instead of his stance.

Less capacity.

Better joint.

The network changed without collapsing.

That mattered.

At hour three, one pressure anchor reached limit.

We did not replace it with a person.

We redistributed before it failed.

That mattered more.

At hour four, Nhal's tail trace returned to the level from before key two snapped.

Survey said the number twice because nobody trusted it.

Seven.

Then six.

Then baseline.

The chamber above sent one pull.

Yes.

Mael finally looked tired enough to be human.

"Can we release the emergency network?"

I checked every path.

Not alone.

Survey confirmed vibration.

Structural confirmed bridge tolerance.

Nhal confirmed from above.

Arcrutus confirmed he could still feel his fingers.

Mael confirmed nothing because nobody had asked him anything medical.

Healer would have been furious.

I said, "Slowly."

We dismantled the emergency network over twenty-two minutes.

No one source came out first.

That felt important.

West reduced as east reduced.

Anchors took more while people took less.

Bridge accepted steady load only after the chatter fell below threshold.

Nhal adjusted her internal distribution twice.

I closed each relation only after something else already held its job.

Nothing disappeared.

Everything went somewhere.

At the end, the physical bridge held.

Not forever.

Not enough to free Nhal.

Enough to let Arcrutus sit down.

He did.

Right where he stood.

Mael sat too.

Nobody ordered it.

I stayed standing because my legs had forgotten how sitting worked.

Then they remembered suddenly.

I sat.

The structural specialist leaned against the wall.

Survey put both hands over her face.

For maybe thirty seconds, nobody spoke.

Then Arcrutus said, "She should be here."

No one corrected him.

Mael looked toward the stairs.

"Yes."

That was all.

No speech.

No lesson.

Pava should have been there.

She was not.

After another minute, Mael looked at me.

"You said you were insurance."

"You said that."

"I did."

He looked at the bridge.

Then at Arcrutus.

Then at the instrument web.

Then upward toward Nhal.

"Not anymore."

I did not ask what I was.

Bad time.

Also I was afraid of the answer.

Above us, Nhal pulled the cord once.

Yes.
