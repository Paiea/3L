## RECORD 087

## THE REVIEW

We were given forty minutes to be people.

Mael chose the number.

Of course he did.

The lower bridge was holding.

Survey had two watchers on the instrument web.

Structural had three physical checks running on the flex plate.

Nhal's tail trace was back near the level from before Pava died.

Near.

Not the same.

Nothing was the same.

The healer had Pava wrapped in expedition cloth beside the upper wall.

Not hidden.

Not displayed.

There is probably a correct ritual for the body of an S-class human.

We did not have it.

We had a clean ground cloth.

A coat under her head.

Her pressure rods lined beside one hand because the healer had found them in the chamber and apparently decided equipment could count as dignity.

I understood that.

I sat six feet away.

Arcrutus sat closer.

Mael stood for the first seven minutes.

Then his knee reminded him he had a body and he sat too.

Nobody spoke.

That surprised me.

Arcrutus usually treated silence as a personal enemy.

He stared at Pava's wrapped hand.

There was a dark mark on the cloth where her fingers rested.

Instrument dye.

Blue.

Of course.

I kept expecting her to complain about something.

My routing.

Survey's wording.

The healer's wrap.

Mael saying possibly when he meant no.

The fact that Arcrutus breathed too loudly.

Anything.

Dead people become rude by absence.

You keep leaving space for them to interrupt.

They do not.

At minute eight, the healer brought water.

Nobody wanted it.

She handed it out anyway.

Good healer.

At minute eleven, Mael said, "Drink."

We did.

Leadership.

At minute sixteen, Arcrutus said, "I should have taken it."

Nobody asked what.

We all knew.

The last correction.

The excess.

The thing Pava closed around herself.

Mael looked at him.

"Could you have shaped it?"

Arcrutus's jaw tightened.

"No."

"Then no."

"I could have taken the force."

"Wrong problem."

Arcrutus looked at me.

I hated that.

"Wrong problem," I agreed.

He looked back at Pava.

"Still should have been me."

Mael said, "That is grief pretending to be analysis."

Arcrutus stood.

Fast.

The healer looked up.

I did too.

Mael did not move.

Arcrutus towered over him.

"Careful."

Mael's face stayed flat.

"No."

There are people you can calm by being gentle.

Arcrutus was not one of them.

"You want me to say she chose correctly because she was the only one who could? Fine. She chose correctly. She is still dead."

Mael nodded.

"Yes."

Arcrutus's hands closed.

Then opened.

"Fuck you."

"Later."

That almost got a laugh out of me.

Almost.

Arcrutus sat again.

Minute twenty-three.

Nobody timed it aloud.

I did in my head because apparently Mael was contagious.

The upper tension cord pulled once.

Change.

Every person in the chamber turned toward it.

Survey checked the slate.

"Minor deep rise. Within correction."

Structural checked the bridge.

"Holding."

The cord pulled once again.

Not our signal.

Nhal.

I looked toward the speaking tube.

The healer said, "Go."

I did.

Nhal's breathing came faintly through the tube.

Then her voice.

"Greg."

Still rough.

Less broken.

"Here."

Long pause.

"Dead?"

There it was.

No room in that word.

"Yes."

Silence.

I heard one slow breath through the tube.

Then another.

"Name?"

I closed my eyes.

Of course.

We had never told her.

We had been very busy using Pava's name around her.

That was not the same as telling someone who a dead person was.

"Pava Kett."

Nhal repeated it slowly.

"Pava."

Arcrutus looked away.

Mael did not.

Nhal said nothing else.

That was enough.

At minute thirty-two, Mael unfolded the operation map.

Not because grief was over.

Because forty minutes had not been permission to pretend the foundation stopped.

He put the map on the floor between us.

Pava's handwriting covered half of it.

Tiny marks.

Numbers.

Corrections to Survey's corrections.

One rude note beside a thermal path:

NOT HEAT. DELAYED COMPRESSION. STOP CALLING IT HEAT.

Survey saw me reading it.

"She was wrong for six hours."

"Then?"

"Then she was right."

I nodded.

Pava remained annoying.

Useful.

Mael waited until everyone was looking at the map.

"We review continuation."

Arcrutus said, "We continue."

Mael looked at him.

"Review."

"We know the answer."

"You know your answer."

Arcrutus opened his mouth.

Mael held up one finger.

"One S-class dead. One pressure anchor spent. One damaged. Two primary restraints altered. Lower foundation actively degrading. Unknown remaining failure progression. We are beyond original mission scope, below planned casualty tolerance, and dependent on field-built infrastructure we did not bring in."

Arcrutus closed his mouth.

Mael looked around.

"That is not a speech for retreat. It is the situation."

Good leader.

I hated that too.

Survey said, "Return route remains open."

Structural nodded.

"For now."

Healer said, "We can move Pava's body safely. We can move everyone currently ambulatory."

Mael looked at me.

"Greg?"

I checked the map.

Not because he needed my permission.

Because my piece had become whether the relationships survived our movement.

"If we withdraw slowly and leave the lower bridge in place, yes. We can get out."

Arcrutus said, "And Nhal?"

I looked at the empty left forelimb restraint.

Her freed leg.

The altered geometry.

The lower bridge.

All the things we had changed.

"Maybe we can reseat the foreleg restraint."

The healer's face hardened.

I raised a hand.

"I said can. Not should."

Mael said, "Would reseating restore the original distribution?"

"No."

Survey answered with me.

"No."

We looked at each other.

She continued.

"The lower bridge now carries part of a path that previously went through Nhal. The fracture faces have shifted. Even if we put the limb back exactly where it was, exactly is gone."

Structural said, "We can leave our bridge."

"For how long?" Mael asked.

He did not answer immediately.

That was answer enough.

"Best case?"

"Days. Maybe weeks if load stays like this."

Arcrutus said, "And worst?"

"It fails while we are climbing out."

Good.

Possibly.

Mael looked toward the tube.

"We need Nhal's assessment."

Nobody argued.

I went back.

"Nhal."

"Greg."

She had improved my name.

I decided not to be pleased.

Failed.

"We are reviewing whether to continue the extraction or withdraw and return with more equipment."

Pause.

Then:

"Return."

I waited.

"Yes. Return with more equipment."

Another pause.

"No."

Arcrutus exhaled hard behind me.

I lifted a hand without looking back.

"You mean we should not return?"

"No."

Three pulls on the cord.

Not like that.

Right.

I tried again.

"If we leave now, is returning later unsafe?"

One pull.

Yes.

"Because the foundation is failing faster?"

One.

"Because our changes make it worse?"

Two.

No.

"Because the existing failure will continue whether we are here or not?"

One.

I looked at Survey.

She was already writing.

"Can you estimate how long until the current configuration becomes substantially more dangerous?"

Silence.

Then Nhal said, "No."

Actual word.

Good.

I almost smiled.

"Can you tell whether it is getting worse faster than before we arrived?"

One pull.

Yes.

Survey said quietly, "That matches our traces."

Mael nodded.

I kept going.

"If we stop changing anything and leave the bridge, can you maintain the current system?"

Long silence.

Nhal's answer came through the tube.

"For... time."

Not yes.

Not no.

I looked at Mael.

He said, "Ask whether she knows if that time is enough for us to reach surface, resupply, and return."

I did.

Nhal was quiet for so long I thought she had not understood.

Then:

"No."

The chamber became very still.

Arcrutus said, "There."

Mael said, "Not there."

Arcrutus turned on him.

"What else do you need?"

"Her choice."

That stopped him.

Mael came to the tube himself.

He did not take it from me.

Just stood beside me.

"Nhal," he said.

Pause.

"Mael."

His face changed a fraction.

She knew his name too.

"We have lost Pava. Continuing may kill more of us. Stopping may leave you in greater danger before we can return. Do you want us to continue trying to free you now?"

No one breathed normally.

Nhal answered with words.

"Yes."

Then, after a pause:

"Before... it chooses... for me."

There.

Not rescue as sentiment.

Not Pava's death as obligation.

Choice before the system removed choice again.

Mael looked at the map.

Then at each of us.

"We continue if the operation remains technically defensible. Pava's death is not a vote. Nhal's consent is not a waiver. Either one of those ideas gets us killed if we use it lazily."

Arcrutus nodded.

Survey nodded.

Structural nodded.

Healer said, "Medical limits remain mine."

"Yes."

She looked at me.

"Including his."

"Why me specifically?"

Nobody answered.

Rude.

Mael said, "Next question. What can we free without depending on Pava?"

We looked at the map.

The right forelimb was the obvious answer.

Which meant it was probably wrong.

Survey pointed at the chest interface instead.

"This fell after the first step."

Structural shook his head.

"Too many functions."

"Mapped functions."

"Exactly. Too many mapped ones means probably more unmapped."

Arcrutus pointed at the right forelimb.

"Match the first."

I said, "Geometry changed after the first. Matching is not matching anymore."

He looked offended by reality.

Good.

We worked.

Pava's absence appeared everywhere.

Not emotionally.

Mechanically.

We had two pressure questions nobody could answer with her speed.

One shaping path she would have owned automatically.

Three annotations on the map that only made sense if you already knew what she meant.

Survey cursed at one of them.

"What is a crooked three?"

I looked.

"Could be a five."

"She never wrote fives like that."

Arcrutus said, "Ask her."

Nobody moved.

He closed his eyes.

"Fuck."

That was grief again.

This time nobody named it.

We reconstructed the mark from adjacent readings.

Fourteen minutes.

Pava would have answered in three seconds.

That was the new price of every unknown.

We kept working.

Eventually Survey found a candidate.

Not the whole right forelimb.

One restraint collar inside it.

A later correction layer that limited rotation but no longer carried significant foundation load.

The healer checked the tissue.

"This one is hurting her."

Arcrutus looked at Mael.

Mael said, "Evidence."

Healer pointed to the hide beneath the collar.

Dark material had cut through the mineral-textured surface in one narrow line.

Not fresh.

Centuries old and still irritated.

The collar moved every time the lower fracture corrected.

Tiny movement.

Repeated forever.

I hated it instantly.

Still not evidence.

The evidence was the tissue.

Survey traced the collar's relationships.

"Low structural significance. High motion control."

Structural said, "Removing it changes stance."

I looked at Nhal's freed left paw.

"Which may be useful."

Mael said, "Ask her."

I went to the tube.

Explained the collar.

Explained what we thought it did.

Explained what we did not know.

Nhal listened.

Then said, slowly:

"That one... is not mine."

First full sentence.

Five words.

It landed harder than anything complicated could have.

I looked at the black collar.

"We know."

Nhal breathed through the tube.

"Good."

Arcrutus made a sound that might have been a laugh and might have been a problem.

Mael ignored him.

"Proceed to planning. No release today. We are tired."

Arcrutus said, "We just decided to continue."

"Correct."

"And now we sleep."

"Correct."

"You are difficult."

"Alive people often are."

That ended the argument.

We built watches.

Survey took first instrument watch with one rigger.

Structural second.

I got third because the healer threatened Mael until he removed me from second.

Arcrutus was not assigned a watch.

He objected.

Mael said, "You are our largest reserve. Sleep."

Arcrutus looked at me.

"I preferred being insurance."

"No you didn't."

"True."

Before lying down, he walked to Pava.

He did not touch her.

Just stood there.

Then said, "We are continuing."

No one told him dead people could not hear.

He knew.

Maybe saying things is sometimes for the living.

I lay down beside the map.

Bad choice.

Pava's handwriting was six inches from my face.

NOT HEAT.

I turned the map over.

Worse.

Blank cloth.

I turned it back.

At least she was annoying on that side.

The tension cord pulled once from above.

I opened my eyes.

Not change.

Nhal had started using one pull for something else when no question had been asked.

Presence, maybe.

I pulled once in return.

No protocol.

Probably bad survey practice.

The cord went still.

I slept.
