## RECORD 129

## THE RECENT THINGS

Ithar kept digging.

Not quickly.

That would have implied urgency.

Dragons apparently searched hoards the way humans searched old drawers after deciding the object had probably been there for thirty years and could wait another minute.

Gold moved.

Metal clicked beneath metal.

A helmet surfaced.

Then disappeared again.

A box the size of my chest shifted three feet and revealed nothing useful.

I watched.

“How much of this do you actually remember?”

“All of it.”

“No.”

His eye moved toward me.

Good.

“Most of it.”

“No.”

“Enough.”

“That I believe.”

He continued.

“What are you looking for?”

“Things I acquired recently.”

I looked around the cave.

“Define recently.”

“Within the period you claim to have repeated.”

“Forty years.”

“Yes.”

I waited.

He kept searching.

“That is recent to you.”

“Yes.”

“Fuck dragons.”

“You have said that before.”

“Recently.”

His nostril moved.

Good.

Then a claw emerged holding a dark metal plate.

Not armor.

Part of armor.

Maybe.

He placed it on the stone between us.

“One.”

I leaned closer.

“What does it do?”

“Redirects physical trauma into stored magical stress.”

I stopped.

“That sounds excellent.”

“It is.”

“Where was it when I was nineteen?”

“Attached to a man.”

“Alive?”

“Yes.”

“Rank?”

“A.”

I looked at the plate.

Then at Ithar.

“Could I take it?”

“Yes.”

I smiled.

“He would kill you.”

“Ah.”

“Probably before you reached the clasp.”

“Less good.”

Ithar moved the plate aside.

“Two.”

He dug again.

This time he produced something round.

Glass.

No.

Crystal.

A lens about the width of my palm, set inside a tarnished brass frame.

I reached.

His claw lowered.

I stopped.

“Touching?”

“Safe.”

“Then why?”

“You have poor hands.”

Fair.

“What does it do?”

“Reveals active channel structure.”

My entire body changed.

Not much.

Enough.

Ithar noticed.

“Inside a person?”

“Yes.”

“How clearly?”

“Very.”

“Can it distinguish load routes?”

“Yes.”

“Old damage?”

“Sometimes.”

“Channel preference?”

“Yes.”

I stared at the thing.

That was not a lens.

That was decades of arguments with Tracy turned into an object.

“Where?”

“An observatory.”

“When I was nineteen.”

“Yes.”

“Accessible?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Sealed beneath an inhabited city.”

I looked toward the sword-shaped absence that did not exist yet.

No.

Not yet.

“Could I open it?”

“No.”

“You answered very quickly.”

“Yes.”

I pointed at the lens.

“You own it now.”

“The city suffered an earthquake twenty-six years later.”

“So I wait twenty-six years.”

“You wanted an advantage at nineteen.”

“Still an advantage at forty-five.”

“Then perhaps.”

Ithar moved the lens aside.

I watched it go.

That one hurt.

“Three.”

A little longer this time.

He pulled out a narrow wooden case.

Opened it.

Three pale metal arcs lay inside.

Thin.

Curved.

Unimpressive.

“What is that?”

“It preserves channel shape.”

I looked at him.

“What?”

“A route may be impressed into it. Partially.”

“For how long?”

“Depends.”

“I hate that word.”

“I know.”

“Could I train from it?”

“Possibly.”

“Could I show someone else a route?”

“Poorly.”

“Could I show myself?”

His eye narrowed.

There.

Good question.

“Perhaps.”

I stared at the three arcs.

“That one.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“At nineteen it belonged to a woman who understood what she owned.”

“Alive?”

“Yes.”

“Ranker?”

“No.”

“Then this sounds solvable.”

“She refused kings.”

“I am not a king.”

“Correct.”

“That sounded unnecessary.”

“It was precise.”

“Where?”

“South coast.”

“Same continent?”

“No.”

Fuck.

“How far?”

“Far.”

“Helpful.”

“Three countries. One sea.”

I looked at the little arcs.

Not impossible.

Not within a comfortable year either.

“Name?”

Ithar paused.

There.

“Sava Ren.”

I kept it.

Maybe.

Future me had too many names already.

Ithar closed the case.

“Four.”

This one took less searching.

A small dark object.

Palm-sized.

Smooth.

Almost black.

“What?”

“Memory.”

I frowned.

“Whose?”

“Whoever uses it.”

That got me.

“How much?”

“Minutes.”

“Images?”

“More.”

“Technique?”

“Sometimes.”

“Where?”

“Underwater.”

I laughed.

“Of course.”

“Deep.”

“How deep?”

“Enough.”

“Recoverable?”

“Not when you were nineteen.”

“Why?”

“No one knew it was there.”

“That has not stopped this conversation before.”

“The wreck that exposed it occurred fourteen years later.”

I stopped.

“Wreck.”

“Yes.”

“Ship?”

“Yes.”

“Name?”

Ithar looked at me.

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because you will begin planning to sink it.”

“I was going to plan to be nearby.”

“No.”

“Different crime.”

“No.”

I sighed.

“Fine.”

He moved the memory object aside.

Four things.

All absurd.

All useless.

Mostly.

I looked at the hoard.

“You said five.”

“Yes.”

His claw stopped moving.

There.

Different.

He knew where this one was.

He reached beneath a slope of old coins and pulled.

Something long shifted under the gold.

Metal scraped stone.

Then a sword emerged.

Not ceremonial.

Not jeweled.

Dark blade.

Simple guard.

Old leather around the grip.

The kind of weapon that looked almost disappointing until you noticed Ithar had stored it under enough wealth to purchase a city.

He placed it on the floor.

The cave changed.

Not visibly.

I felt it.

Pressure.

Contained.

Quiet.

Too quiet.

“What is that?”

“A sword.”

I stared at him.

“Thank you.”

“You asked.”

“What does it do?”

“It accumulates mana.”

“How?”

“By drawing it.”

“From?”

“Sources.”

“That is not an answer.”

“It is the answer.”

I looked at the blade.

“Who made it?”

“No.”

“You know?”

“Yes.”

“Ancient?”

“Yes.”

“How ancient?”

“Old enough that the distinction will not improve your decision.”

Fair.

“What happens when it fills?”

“It stops drawing aggressively.”

“Aggressively.”

“Yes.”

I did not like that word.

I liked the rest.

“And then?”

“It may release what it stores.”

“Back out.”

“Yes.”

“How much?”

Ithar looked toward the cave mouth.

That was not reassuring.

“How much?”

“A great deal.”

“Human great deal or dragon great deal?”

He considered.

Good.

Bad.

“Somewhere between.”

I looked at the sword again.

“Where was it when I was nineteen?”

“In a lake.”

I waited.

“That’s it?”

“Yes.”

“Owned?”

“No.”

“Guarded?”

“No.”

“Known?”

“No.”

“Monster?”

“Not one that matters.”

“Distance?”

“Reachable.”

That word hit harder than all the others.

Reachable.

I looked at the armor.

A-class owner.

No.

Lens.

Buried under city.

No.

Route arcs.

Sava Ren across countries and sea.

Maybe later.

Memory object.

Underwater until a wreck years away.

No.

Sword.

Lake.

Reachable.

“How difficult?”

“For you at nineteen?”

“Yes.”

“Annoying.”

I smiled.

“That’s all?”

“You would require travel, money, diving equipment, and enough competence not to drown.”

“Annoying.”

“Yes.”

“Within a year?”

“Yes.”

There.

The cave got smaller.

Not actually.

Possibility does that.

I walked around the sword without touching it.

“Why did nobody get it?”

“Because nobody knew where it was.”

“How do you?”

“I own it.”

“That does not explain where it was.”

“I know its provenance.”

“Of course you do.”

“Yes.”

“You remember where somebody pulled a sword out of a lake decades before you acquired it.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Ithar looked offended.

“I purchased it.”

Right.

Dragon accounting.

I pointed at the weapon.

“Is it full?”

“Yes.”

“Now?”

“Yes.”

“When it was in the lake?”

His eye narrowed.

There.

He knew why I was asking.

“Yes.”

“How full?”

“Entirely.”

I stared.

“Nineteen-year-old me can retrieve that sword fully charged.”

“Yes.”

“And whatever it does when it releases…”

“Yes.”

“I get one.”

“Potentially.”

I looked at him.

“What does potentially mean there?”

“You may die before reaching the lake.”

“Helpful.”

“You may fail to retrieve it.”

“Less likely.”

“You may decide not to use it.”

I looked at the sword.

Then at him.

“Now you’re just being silly.”

His nostril opened.

Good.

I crouched near the blade.

Still did not touch it.

Not yet.

“What is the catch?”

“It is a powerful artifact.”

“That was not the question.”

“It has several.”

“Better.”

“It draws from what contacts it.”

“Including the wielder.”

“Yes.”

“How badly?”

“When full, little.”

“And when empty?”

Ithar looked at me.

There.

Bad.

“How badly?”

“You should not touch it empty.”

I stood back up.

“Good sword.”

“Yes.”

“That sounded sincere.”

“It is an excellent object.”

“Would you recommend nineteen-year-old me build around it?”

“No.”

Fast.

Interesting.

“Why?”

“You have not asked the correct questions yet.”

“I asked if it kills me.”

“You asked one correct question.”

I looked at the sword.

Five recent things.

Four impossible or inconvenient enough to be nearly impossible.

One sitting at the bottom of a lake waiting for a nineteen-year-old idiot with eighty years of memory to discover it early.

Assuming I returned.

Assuming I returned to nineteen.

Assuming the sword was still there.

Assuming history did not move under my feet the moment I changed something.

Assuming.

Too many.

Good.

Ithar watched me.

“What?”

“You are planning.”

“Yes.”

“You have not agreed to die.”

“No.”

“You do not know whether death causes return.”

“No.”

“You do not know whether the return state still exists.”

“No.”

“You do not know whether another return would preserve memory.”

“No.”

“And yet.”

I looked down at the sword.

“Within a year.”

Ithar’s eyes narrowed.

“That was not approval.”

“No.”

I smiled.

“It was a deadline.”
