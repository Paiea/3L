## RECORD 115

## THE LAST YEARS

Years five through nine were less dramatic than people would like.

Good.

Drama is expensive.

I worked.

Trained.

Investigated.

Ate.

Slept badly.

Slept better.

Argued with Tracy.

Paid Tracy.

Sometimes remembered on time.

Growth.

Hale remained alive.

Always.

That mattered less hourly.

More structurally.

He became the fixed point.

Not obsession in the old way.

Not waking thought.

Not every meal.

A destination can stay in the map without being the road.

Year five.

I reached B-class support cleanly enough that Authority stopped arguing.

Not because one test proved it.

Field consistency.

Structural response.

Combat support.

Hostile stabilization.

Solo survivability.

Team utility still lower than First-Life Greg would have tolerated.

Enough.

B.

Legitimate.

No asterisk.

That mattered.

People treated it like arrival.

It was not.

It was scale.

Useful.

Present Greg at fifty-nine would still be B.

That did not mean nothing changed afterward.

B can contain years.

People confuse letters with endpoints.

Bad habit.

Tracy celebrated by ordering food I could not afford comfortably.

I complained.

“You are B-class now.”

“That does not increase cash already in my account.”

“Future earnings.”

“Dangerous accounting.”

She looked proud.

Not of rank.

Of future billing.

Current Tracy.

Good.

Year six.

Another member of Hale’s old group surfaced.

Not fighter.

A man named Corin Ase.

Transport.

He had moved demolition stock to the hill.

Then evacuated two injured avengers after.

Authority had already interviewed him years before.

No charges.

I found him anyway.

He knew what he had transported.

Did not know the final plan.

Did not know the lower route.

I believed him.

That surprised me.

Not because he seemed honest.

Because the records matched.

He had done enough to make revenge tempting.

Not enough to make killing clean.

I left him alive.

No arrest.

No threat.

Just left.

That was harder than killing Sera in a different way.

No action feels less satisfying than restraint.

I told Tracy.

She said:

“Good.”

“Stop saying that.”

“No.”

Year seven.

One of the original hill guards died in prison.

Not my doing.

Age.

Illness.

I felt cheated.

That was ugly.

True.

Revenge wants ownership.

Even of endings.

I hated that part of myself.

Kept it anyway.

Year seven also brought money.

Not First-Life money.

Nothing close.

Enough.

Named work paid.

B-class paid.

Authority contracts paid better when people trusted you not to collapse buildings while solving them.

Low bar.

Useful.

I bought better equipment.

Not property.

Not businesses.

That was another difference.

Second-Life Greg did not build outward much.

Some of that was grief.

Some deliberate.

Every coin went toward mobility.

Training.

Information.

Prosthetics.

Medical work.

Travel.

Tools.

Revenge.

Lean system.

Portable.

Harder to remove.

That was what I wanted.

Tracy saw it.

“You know you are building the opposite life.”

“Yes.”

“On purpose.”

“Yes.”

“Do you like it?”

No.

Important.

“No.”

She nodded.

Good.

Not good.

Accurate.

Year eight.

Hale and I worked the same flood again.

Different river.

Of course.

No argument this time.

That was worse.

We had procedures now.

Not formal.

Habits.

He asked load.

I gave it.

I asked output.

He gave it.

Tracy watched both of us with disgust.

Reasonable.

We saved people.

Again.

Afterward Hale said:

“You are nearly there.”

I knew what he meant.

“No.”

He looked at me.

“Closer than last time.”

“Yes.”

“Still waiting.”

“I know.”

He left.

No threat.

No note.

We had become horrifyingly efficient at postponing murder.

Year eight also changed Tracy.

Not rank dramatically.

Relationship.

She stopped billing every consultation.

I noticed after three weeks.

“Invoice?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“You bought dinner twice.”

“That is not equivalent.”

“No.”

“Then?”

She shrugged.

“Stop making it weird.”

Current Tracy.

There.

No contract.

No half.

No Number Two.

Still.

But friendship had appeared while we were busy measuring damage.

Different route.

Real.

Year nine.

The graph flattened.

Not outward.

That had already fallen.

The loop.

Closed architecture had stopped producing dramatic gains.

Good sign.

Bad sign.

Mature.

The body had become what we had trained.

Hostile support no longer felt improvised.

Terrain.

Objects.

Opponent structure.

Self.

All one problem.

I could not beat every A-class.

Important.

Probably not most.

Also important.

Hale was specific.

I had studied him for years.

He had studied me.

That narrowed the gap differently.

Tracy ran a full evaluation.

Two days.

Hated both.

Strength.

Endurance.

Channel recovery.

Loop duration.

Outward bandwidth.

Socket tolerance.

Shoulder.

Hip.

Balance.

Pain.

Sleep.

Everything.

At the end she put one page down.

No graph.

Just conclusion.

“B-class.”

“I know.”

“Do not.”

“Sorry.”

“Stable B.”

“Yes.”

“Solo operational effect higher in favorable terrain.”

“Yes.”

“Potentially A-class value.”

“Not A-class.”

“No.”

Good.

Precise.

“Against Hale?”

She hated the question.

Still answered.

“You can kill him.”

There.

First time she said it.

Not maybe.

Not possible.

Can.

I went still.

“How sure?”

“Enough.”

Bad.

Good.

Both.

“Can he kill me?”

“Yes.”

Immediate.

Fair.

“Who wins?”

She stared.

“That is not medicine.”

“Guess.”

“No.”

“Tracy.”

“No.”

Good.

She had boundaries.

Still.

“What changed?”

“You stopped needing him to make mistakes.”

There.

That mattered.

First fight required ignorance.

Second try used years of adaptation but still leaned on surprise.

Now?

Architecture.

Execution.

Body.

Enough.

“You can create your own openings,” she said.

That was the line.

I knew.

So did she.

“Then I go.”

“No.”

I looked at her.

She closed her eyes.

Then corrected.

“I mean... wait.”

“For?”

She looked at me.

“One month.”

“Why?”

“Because you just got the answer you wanted for nine years.”

Fair.

“So?”

“So do not make the biggest decision of your life in the first hour after hearing yes.”

There.

Preventative medicine.

Still.

I hated that she was right.

“Two weeks.”

“One month.”

“Three.”

“One month.”

“Fine.”

She smiled.

Small.

“Growth.”

“Fuck you.”

“Current data.”

I waited one month.

Exactly.

Not a day more.

During that month I worked.

A tunnel.

Two minor reinforcement reviews.

One school roof.

That one annoyed me.

Children.

Normal.

Loud.

Alive.

Good.

I ate with Tracy twice.

Did not talk about Hale first dinner.

Barely second.

Progress.

On day thirty, I wrote Hale.

One line.

READY.

No location.

No date.

He replied the next morning.

SO AM I.

That was the beginning of the end.
