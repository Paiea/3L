## RECORD 100

## THE SEALED ROAD

The second time I went after Hale, I brought four people.

That sounds like growth.

It was.

Also expensive.

Two B-class combatants.

One C-class restraint specialist.

Tracy.

She objected to being counted.

“I am not part of the attack.”

“You are physically present.”

“At the extraction point.”

“Still present.”

“No.”

Fine.

Three and Tracy.

The target was not Hale.

That mattered.

Joren Pell had been with him at the town.

Not a singer’s invention.

Not scenery.

He had helped breach the lower administrative wall.

Helped hold the market approach while Hale went uphill.

Helped prevent local responders from reaching the fire line.

His name appeared in the later First-Life inquiry.

Not immediately.

Years later.

After records opened.

After people stopped caring as much.

I remembered him because one article had a terrible headline.

PELL MELL.

Writers should be punished more often.

In the current history, Joren had not been publicly identified.

Authority knew him.

Hale knew him.

The other avengers knew him.

Most people did not.

That was useful.

More useful was where he hid.

Not now.

Then.

The sealed road.

The official report eventually described an old maintenance route beneath East Gate.

Closed cistern.

Dry overflow channel.

One iron ladder.

Two exits.

Hale’s group had used it after the town operation before separating.

That detail became public in First Life almost twenty years later.

In Second Life, it was still sealed.

I told Tracy.

She stared.

“How do you know that?”

“First life.”

“That answer is becoming irritating.”

“It is still the answer.”

“Could you have gotten it from the inquiry?”

“No.”

“Someone inside Authority?”

“No.”

“Hale?”

“No.”

“You expect me to believe a twenty-year-old future report.”

“Yes.”

“No.”

“Come see.”

That was why she came.

Not because she trusted me.

Because curiosity is a dangerous professional trait.

The two B-class combatants were sisters.

Mara and Tovin Krell.

Not twins.

They corrected everyone.

I never asked why everyone assumed.

They fought differently enough that the assumption should have died immediately.

Mara used a short spear and direct reinforcement.

Tovin used pressure lines and a hooked blade.

Good.

Not great.

Exactly what I needed.

People who knew what they were doing and did not think they were legends.

The restraint specialist was named Oss.

Just Oss.

I asked once.

He said:

“Just Oss.”

Fine.

Oss built binding frames.

Portable.

Ugly.

Reliable.

He also charged by the hour once the target was restrained.

I respected this.

Tracy hated the rate.

Good.

We went at night.

Not because night was tactically magical.

Because fewer civilians used the East Gate service district after dark.

That mattered.

I had already learned one thing from Hale.

Do not turn people into scenery if you can avoid it.

The sealed access door was exactly where I remembered.

Different rust.

Same door.

Tracy stopped.

Looked at me.

Then at the wall.

“That could be coincidence.”

“Yes.”

“You knew which brick covered the latch.”

“Yes.”

“Could still be coincidence.”

“Very committed coincidence.”

She ignored me.

We went down.

The cistern was dry.

Old lime on the walls.

Iron ladder.

Two exits.

Exactly.

Tracy became quieter.

Good.

Not belief.

Evidence.

Joren was there.

That surprised even me.

Memory had given me the route.

Not the schedule.

I had expected to wait.

Instead he sat beside a lantern repairing a boot.

Ordinary.

Of course.

Everyone became ordinary between atrocities.

He saw us.

Looked at the peg.

Then at my face.

Recognition.

Not personal.

Headline-level.

Town survivor.

Hale’s leg.

Whatever people called me then.

He stood.

“Greg.”

I did not like that he knew my name.

“Joren.”

He liked that less.

His eyes moved to the team.

Then Tracy.

Then the exits.

Good.

He understood.

“What do you want?”

“You.”

“That seems personal.”

“Yes.”

He sighed.

Not fear.

Tiredness.

Apparently Hale’s entire revenge circle had learned calm.

Annoying.

“Authority already interviewed me.”

“I know.”

“They know where I am.”

“No.”

That changed him.

Small.

“How do you know this place?”

I smiled.

Not because it was funny.

Because I wanted him uncomfortable.

Bad reason.

Useful.

“Later.”

He moved.

Fast.

B-class.

Maybe high B.

The boot repair had been camouflage or bad timing.

Does not matter.

Mara met him.

Spear.

Impact.

Tovin cut the exit line.

Oss dropped the first frame.

Joren broke it.

Good.

Expected.

I linked Mara first.

Not full First-Life work.

Could not.

Small reinforcement route.

Her front shoulder.

Spear hand.

Hip.

Enough to let her take his next impact without losing structure.

She felt it.

Looked at me.

Half a second.

Too long.

“Later.”

She nodded.

Tovin needed different support.

Less reinforcement.

More timing.

I put a thin Barrier along the inside of her pressure line so Joren’s counter could not bleed through the same path.

He noticed.

Good fighter.

Changed.

I changed too.

There.

Old life.

Not gone.

Just small.

For several seconds I remembered what it felt like to be relational.

Not strong.

Correct.

People becoming more capable because I was attached.

It hurt more than expected.

Then Hale arrived.

Of course.

Not through the entrance.

Second exit.

The one I knew existed.

The one I had left open intentionally.

Tracy saw my face.

“You expected him.”

“Yes.”

“You said target was Joren.”

“It is.”

“You expected Hale.”

“Yes.”

“You left that out.”

“Yes.”

Bad.

She looked furious.

No time.

Hale stepped into the cistern.

Missing finger.

Same calm.

Then he saw the team.

Then me.

Then the wall behind me.

His expression changed.

“How.”

One word.

Not hello.

Not surprise about pursuit.

The route.

“How do you know this place?”

Joren looked at him.

So did Tracy.

Good.

Independent confirmation.

I said:

“South inquiry annex. Appendix seven. Released year nineteen.”

Hale went still.

Nobody else understood.

He did.

“What?”

“Bad maps.”

His face changed again.

Not fear.

Something close.

“That report is sealed.”

“Now.”

Silence.

Tracy looked at me.

There.

Evidence.

Hale took one step.

Mara raised the spear.

Tovin shifted.

I linked both.

Small.

Fast.

Hale saw it.

He had seen me alone.

This was different.

“You brought people.”

“Yes.”

“Better.”

I hated the approval.

He looked at Joren.

“Did they hurt you?”

“No.”

“Yet,” Oss said.

I liked Oss.

Hale’s eyes moved to him.

Oss reconsidered being memorable.

Reasonable.

“We are taking Joren,” I said.

“No.”

Hale did not threaten.

Did not explain.

Just no.

“Authority can have him.”

“They already did.”

“Not enough.”

“That is not yours to decide.”

“Neither was my leg.”

Hale looked at it.

“True.”

Again.

Always willing to concede the part that did not change his action.

Infuriating.

Joren said, “Hale.”

Something in the name.

Warning.

Hale glanced at him.

Joren shook his head.

Interesting.

He did not want the fight.

Maybe because he thought Hale would kill us.

Maybe because he did not want more people dead for him.

Maybe both.

I said:

“He comes with us. Alive.”

Hale looked at me.

“Why alive?”

Good question.

Because dead was easy.

Because I needed names.

Because I needed the process.

Because I wanted him afraid.

Because I was trying not to become Hale.

All true.

“Because he can answer.”

Hale’s eyes sharpened.

“About what?”

“The town.”

“You know what happened.”

“Not enough.”

He understood then.

I was not hunting only bodies.

Information.

Structure.

Who ordered what.

Who blocked responders.

Who chose escalation.

Who knew civilians were present.

Who left.

Who helped afterward.

Hale looked almost sad.

“You are going to spend years on this.”

“Yes.”

“I know.”

Of course.

Then he switched on.

This time I was ready for the change.

Not enough.

Ready is contextual.

Mara took the first impact.

With my support.

Still moved six feet.

Not twenty.

Progress.

Tovin caught the second line.

Her hook almost reached Hale’s shoulder.

He broke the pressure route.

I rebuilt it through Mara.

Hale saw that.

Adjusted.

I closed the small loop through my peg and floor.

Held position.

That let me keep both links while his pressure tried to move me.

Pain through the socket.

Shoulder lag.

Useful.

Bad.

Tracy saw it.

“Greg.”

“No.”

Wrong.

She hated that.

Hale came for me.

Of course.

He remembered.

Remove support.

Everyone gets worse.

Correct doctrine.

Mara stepped in.

Tovin crossed.

For three seconds Hale had to treat them as real.

That was all we needed.

Oss dropped the second restraint frame around Joren.

This one was not meant to hold him forever.

Only long enough.

Joren broke one arm free.

Mara hit him with the shaft.

Tovin cut the second exit line.

I dropped my support on them both.

Not because I wanted to.

Because extraction mattered more than winning the exchange.

“Go.”

Oss dragged the frame.

Joren swore.

Tracy moved.

Not toward me.

Toward the exit.

Good.

Plan.

Hale saw.

He could stop them.

Or stop me.

Not both immediately.

That was the operation.

Not defeat Hale.

Give him two problems.

He chose me.

Of course.

I had counted on that too.

His hand closed around my coat.

Same side as before.

Missing finger changed the grip slightly.

I noticed.

He noticed me noticing.

“Do not.”

I smiled.

Bad habit.

“Your hand changed.”

He hit me.

Fair.

The loop caught some.

Not enough.

I hit the floor.

Peg held.

Socket screamed.

I stayed conscious.

Progress.

Hale reached again.

I used the old failure route from our first fight.

Not to kill.

He knew it now.

Of course.

He countered before I finished.

Better.

Then I used his counter.

New route.

Barrier at the wrist.

Draw at the shoulder.

Tiny.

He shifted.

Exactly.

I rolled under.

Not elegant.

One-legged revenge rarely is.

By the time he turned, Oss and Joren were gone.

Mara too.

Tovin last.

Tracy remained at the exit.

Furious.

“Greg.”

I moved.

Hale could have followed.

He did not.

That was the strange part.

He stood in the cistern.

Looked at the sealed wall.

Then at me.

“Year nineteen.”

“Yes.”

“Appendix seven.”

“Yes.”

“Who are you?”

There.

Not rank.

Not grief.

Not town survivor.

Something else.

I said:

“Later.”

Petty.

Worth it.

Then left.

We got Joren to an Authority office before sunrise.

Not because I trusted Authority completely.

Because public custody created friction.

Friction was useful when you wanted someone to remain alive.

The investigators were furious.

Then interested.

Then more furious when I told them where we found him.

Tracy said almost nothing.

She treated Mara’s shoulder.

Tovin’s hand.

My socket.

Then sat across from me.

“You knew Hale would use the second exit.”

“Yes.”

“You knew the appendix number.”

“Yes.”

“He said it was sealed.”

“Yes.”

“Could he have been performing for you?”

“No.”

“Could the investigator have told you?”

“No.”

“Could you have bribed someone?”

“Probably.”

She stared.

“I didn’t.”

“Bad answer.”

“Fine. No.”

She looked at the floor.

Then at me.

“Year nineteen.”

“Yes.”

“From now.”

“Yes.”

“You read a report that has not been released.”

“Yes.”

“In another life.”

“Yes.”

Long silence.

Then:

“I hate this.”

Fair.

“Do you believe me?”

“No.”

She frowned.

“I believe the explanation is becoming annoyingly competitive.”

That was enough.

For then.

“You damaged the socket.”

“I know.”

“Skin too.”

“I know.”

“Shoulder.”

“I know.”

“Loop.”

“One consultation at a time does not mean one lie at a time.”

Correct.

“That was operational.”

Fair.

“Would you have come?”

“No.”

“Then I needed you not to know.”

Again.

“No.”

“Tracy.”

“No.”

“I saw you decide my consent was inconvenient.”

Fuck.

Different scale.

Same flaw.

Growth.

Barely.

Ordinary.

About the market approach.

Because capturing a man was easier than deciding what justice meant once he was sitting in a cell.
