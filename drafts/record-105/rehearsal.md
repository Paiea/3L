## RECORD 105

## THE BETTER THING

The newspaper called it a joint response.

I hated that.

Not because it was inaccurate.

Because it looked friendly.

Hale Rusk and local support specialist Greg worked in coordination with Authority engineering teams to prevent secondary residential collapse.

Local support specialist.

Good.

No rank inflation.

No song.

Then:

Rusk credited Greg’s load-routing intervention with extending the evacuation window.

Worse.

He had said that publicly.

I knew because the reporter quoted him.

Not paraphrased.

Quoted.

I found the paper folded on Tracy’s desk.

She had circled one sentence.

Not Hale’s.

Mine.

SUPPORT LINKS REMAINED STABLE UNDER HIGH LOAD FOR APPROXIMATELY SIX MINUTES.

I looked at her.

“What?”

“Sit.”

I sat.

That word had acquired power.

She put three pages in front of me.

Before aqueduct.

After aqueduct.

Current.

Channel response.

Outward-link precision.

Return lag.

Recovery time.

The numbers were not terrible.

That was the problem.

Terrible would have been easy.

They were simply worse.

Small.

Consistent.

Real.

“You overloaded,” I said.

“We know.”

“Acute fatigue.”

“Partly.”

“Socket trauma.”

“Partly.”

“Shoulder.”

“Partly.”

I looked at her.

She waited.

Fine.

“The loop.”

“Yes.”

“How much?”

“Cannot isolate perfectly.”

“Then do not.”

“I can isolate enough.”

She tapped the outward-link test.

“Your relational recovery is slower after closed-loop sessions.”

“Temporary.”

“Maybe.”

“Tracy.”

“You asked for measurement.”

I hated when she used agreements against me.

Good agreements do that.

She moved another page forward.

“Aqueduct was the widest outward architecture you have used since I met you.”

“Yes.”

“You know what happened?”

“I helped hold a wall.”

“No.”

She pointed.

“You improved.”

I stared.

“During the event.”

“Yes.”

“That is normal.”

“Not this much.”

She had data.

Of course.

“Your outward control became cleaner under real relational load.”

“Yes.”

“Faster than it has under drills.”

“Yes.”

“Because?”

I knew.

Did not answer.

“Greg.”

“Because support develops through support.”

There.

First-Life truth.

Relational architecture was not a spell list.

It was adaptation.

Bodies.

Timing.

Trust.

Other people becoming part of the magical problem.

You could practice alone.

Only so far.

Tracy pointed at the current test.

“Then explain why you are training the opposite.”

“Hale.”

“No.”

I looked at her.

“No what?”

“That is a person, not an explanation.”

“He is the reason.”

“He is a reason.”

Annoying.

Correct.

I leaned back.

“The outward path will not get me there fast enough.”

“There.”

She waited.

I had said it.

Not cannot.

Not impossible.

Not bad.

Fast enough.

“How long?” she asked.

“What?”

“To rebuild the old architecture.”

“I do not know.”

“Guess.”

Interesting.

She had learned when guessing mattered.

“Years.”

“How many?”

“Too many.”

“That is not a number.”

“Ten. Fifteen. Maybe more.”

Her face changed.

“You had thirty.”

“In First Life.”

“To become S-class.”

“Yes.”

“You are not starting from zero.”

“No.”

“But your body is.”

“Close enough.”

She looked at the peg.

The shoulder.

The paper.

“And Hale?”

“A-class.”

“Named.”

“Yes.”

“Still active.”

“Yes.”

“Still aging.”

“Slowly relevant.”

She ignored that.

“You think the solo path gets you there sooner.”

“Yes.”

“How much sooner?”

“I do not know.”

“Guess.”

“Five years if everything works.”

Silence.

There.

Calendar.

Revenge consumed years even when optimized.

Tracy sat back.

“Five.”

“Maybe.”

“You are willing to damage fifteen-year capacity for a five-year weapon.”

“That is one way to say it.”

“It is the way you just said it.”

Fair.

I looked at the article again.

Hale’s quote.

Greg’s load routing.

Six minutes.

Families out.

“Would the old path kill him?” Tracy asked.

“Yes.”

“Eventually.”

“Yes.”

“Then this is time.”

“Yes.”

“Not capability.”

“Not only.”

“But mostly.”

I thought.

“Yes.”

That was worse.

Tracy did not moralize.

Good.

She looked at the paper.

Then said:

“You are choosing speed because you cannot tolerate him living.”

Simple.

“Yes.”

No defense.

She nodded.

Not approval.

Classification.

“What do you want from me?”

“Keep measuring.”

“That is all?”

“No.”

“What else?”

“Tell me when the cost changes category.”

Her eyes narrowed.

“Meaning?”

“When it stops being temporary.”

She looked at me for a long time.

“You want me to identify the point of no return.”

“Yes.”

“And then?”

I said nothing.

She knew.

Of course.

“You plan to cross it.”

“If I need to.”

“Bad answer.”

“Honest.”

She looked down.

That mattered.

Then:

“Fine.”

I had not expected that.

“What?”

“I will measure.”

“Why?”

“Because refusing does not make you stop.”

There.

Not old Tracy.

Current Tracy learning current Greg.

“And because?”

She glared.

I waited.

“Because if you are going to ruin something valuable, I want the date.”

Very Tracy.

I laughed.

She did not.

Then did.

Small.

We added another line to the plan.

OUTWARD CEILING PRESERVATION.

Not restoration.

Preservation.

That word hurt.

We tested weekly.

Sometimes twice.

The numbers moved slowly.

Not enough for drama.

Enough for a graph.

Tracy made one.

I hated graphs after that.

The inward loop improved faster.

Of course.

Closed problems are easier to optimize.

No partner variance.

No communication delay.

No trust.

No one else’s bad day.

Just Greg.

Peg.

Floor.

Barrier.

Load.

Return.

The loop became reinforcement.

Then movement.

Then impact handling.

Then hostile contact.

Small applications.

Nothing flashy.

Useful.

I could take a shove from someone stronger and make the ground pay part of it.

Could stabilize an opponent’s weapon at the wrong instant.

Could force a spell boundary to keep carrying load half a heartbeat after the caster wanted it released.

Support logic.

Hostile.

Wrong direction.

Effective.

The next person from Hale’s group surfaced three months after the aqueduct.

Not through future memory.

Current investigation.

That mattered.

Her name was Sera Dain.

A-class?

No.

C-class.

Maybe B in prepared terrain.

Logistics and demolition.

She had placed the lower charges.

The ones that opened the route toward the hill.

The same charges that propagated into the market foundations after the fighting changed.

Authority wanted her for questioning.

I wanted more.

Tracy read the file.

Then looked at me.

“Alive?”

I knew what she was asking.

Not operationally.

Morally.

“I do not know.”

She nodded.

Better than pretending.

“Team?”

“No.”

Her face changed.

“Greg.”

“I can take her.”

“That is not what I asked.”

“I know.”

“Team?”

“No.”

“Why?”

Because the loop was ready.

Because I needed to know.

Because if I could not defeat a C-class demolition specialist alone, Hale remained fantasy.

All true.

“This is a test.”

Tracy closed the file.

“No.”

“I am not asking permission.”

“I know.”

That hurt both of us.

Outward ceiling.

Slow decline.

Then at me.

“Is she responsible?”

“Yes.”

“For the town?”

“Yes.”

“For the civilians?”

“Partly.”

“Does she still pose a threat?”

“Maybe.”

“Yes.”

“So this is not necessary.”

“No.”

No hiding.

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Not good as approval.

Good as naming.

“Then do not call me after and say it was field work.”

“I won’t.”

“And do not use my plan as evidence you can survive it.”

I said nothing.

Her face hardened.

“Greg.”

“I won’t.”

“Good.”

She did not stop me.

That was worse.

Ithar shifted.

“To test yourself.”

“She had helped destroy my town.”

“That was not my question.”

About the room.

The charges.

The first time the loop became something more than training.

“After your family.”

“Yes.”
