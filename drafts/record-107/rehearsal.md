## RECORD 107

## THE EXCEPTION

My contract suspension lasted nineteen days.

It was supposed to last ninety.

That mattered.

The letter said thirty pending review.

Then sixty.

Then indefinite if the legal office found cause.

Nineteen.

At first I was pleased.

Then Tracy read the letter.

“Why?”

“Review complete.”

“No.”

I looked at her.

“Apparently.”

“No.”

She held the page closer.

“Why nineteen?”

“Ask Authority.”

“I am asking you.”

“I do not know.”

She frowned.

Good.

That meant she already knew something.

“What?”

“There is a bridge failure in North Ward.”

Of course.

I looked at the letter again.

Then at her.

“When?”

“Yesterday.”

“Serious?”

“Yes.”

“How serious?”

“Support serious.”

There.

I hated the phrase.

Not because it was wrong.

Because I understood immediately.

North Ward had an old elevated freight bridge crossing two streets and a canal.

Not glamorous.

Important.

One central support had shifted after foundation washout.

Traffic stopped.

Buildings evacuated.

Engineers could stabilize it eventually.

Problem was getting underneath long enough to do it.

The bridge had become a loaded trap.

Too heavy to dismantle quickly.

Too unstable to trust.

Too close to occupied structures to let fall.

A normal structural mage could reinforce sections.

A strong barrier specialist could hold one failure face.

What they needed was somebody who could read the whole load path and move pressure around long enough for physical crews to install new support.

Support serious.

Me.

I hated that too.

Tracy put the letter down.

“They unsuspended you because they need you.”

“Maybe.”

“Greg.”

“Probably.”

“Say it.”

I looked at her.

“Authority wants to use me.”

“Yes.”

“Because I am useful.”

“Yes.”

Silence.

There.

Hale.

Not same.

Close enough to hurt.

“I am not Hale.”

“I did not say you were.”

“You were thinking it.”

“No.”

“Face.”

She stared.

Then almost smiled.

Bad time.

She stopped.

“I was thinking the institution does not need you to be morally resolved before it needs a bridge not to fall.”

That was worse.

Good.

I sat.

The letter remained between us.

Temporary reinstatement for emergency structural response only.

Restrictions still active otherwise.

No independent apprehension work.

No weapons authorization beyond field necessity.

Mandatory reporting.

Supervised assignment.

Very careful language.

“Do I go?” I asked.

Tracy looked at me.

Not an easy question.

Good.

“If you do not?”

“Someone else tries.”

“Can they?”

“Yes.”

“As well?”

I hated that she asked.

“No.”

“How much worse?”

“Enough.”

She nodded.

“Then go.”

Again.

Same answer as Hale.

Not because the person deserved the work.

Because the people under the problem deserved the help.

I hated how simple that could be.

North Ward smelled like wet stone and machine oil.

The bridge had shifted four inches.

Four.

People hear that and imagine nothing.

Four inches is enormous when thousands of tons expect zero.

The east pier had sunk.

One girder twisted.

The canal wall cracked.

Every vibration moved somewhere new.

No Hale this time.

Good.

Authority field lead was a woman named Sen Corra.

A-class engineering support.

Not combat.

Different A.

She looked at my restrictions before looking at me.

Good.

“You understand the terms.”

“Yes.”

“You are here for load routing.”

“Yes.”

“Nothing else.”

“Yes.”

“If I say stop.”

“I stop.”

Tracy looked at me.

I ignored her.

Growth.

Sen pointed at the bridge.

“We need twenty-two minutes under the center span.”

“No.”

She looked at me.

“Estimate?”

“Fourteen.”

“Engineers need twenty-two.”

“Then they need to become faster.”

She frowned.

Good.

Normal field argument.

Not morality.

We worked.

No drama.

That became important.

I read the load.

Sen knew structure better than me.

Much better.

I knew failure transfer better.

Different.

We disagreed.

Adjusted.

She wanted to reinforce the east pier.

I wanted to unload it first.

She showed me why unloading would twist the upper girder.

I showed her where the twist would go.

She changed her plan.

I changed mine.

Good work.

Real work.

The kind I missed.

Not revenge.

Not training.

A problem that ended when the bridge stopped being dangerous.

There was relief in that.

Tracy stayed at medical staging.

Not needed much.

Good.

Nobody badly hurt.

Better.

At minute eleven, one jack slipped.

Load jumped.

I caught it through Barrier.

Not enough.

Sen added structural hold.

I routed into the canal wall.

Bad wall.

Temporary.

Good enough.

The engineers moved.

Physical brace.

Steel.

Bolts.

People doing things magic should not replace because steel keeps working after you go home.

Minute fourteen.

Still not done.

Of course.

“Out,” I said.

Sen looked under the span.

“Two minutes.”

“No.”

“Greg.”

“No.”

She could have ordered me.

Field lead.

Instead she looked at the load.

Saw it.

“Out!”

Good.

Everyone moved.

The brace held.

Barely.

We reset.

Took another hour.

Then went back.

Second attempt.

Twelve minutes.

Done.

Bridge stable enough for real repair.

Nobody died.

No heroics.

No town.

Good.

Afterward Sen found me near medical staging.

“You were right about the first window.”

“Yes.”

She frowned.

“Do not sound pleased.”

“I am learning.”

Tracy looked at me.

Good.

Sen handed me a field report slip.

“Authority will want your notes.”

“Fine.”

Then:

“You should be doing this regularly.”

There.

I looked at her.

“Apparently I am suspended.”

She knew.

Everyone knew.

“That is not my department.”

“No.”

“Still.”

Useful.

Again.

I hated how often useful appeared.

Tracy waited until Sen left.

Then said:

“How do you feel?”

“About?”

“Being needed.”

I looked at the bridge.

Workers moving under it safely now.

“Good.”

“About being unsuspended because you were needed.”

“Less good.”

“Why?”

I knew.

Did not want to say it.

She waited.

Fine.

“Because this is what they did with Hale.”

“Partly.”

“Do not soften it.”

“I am not.”

She sat beside me.

“Did Authority say Sera did not matter?”

“No.”

“Did they clear you of killing her?”

“No.”

“Did they erase the review?”

“No.”

“Did they say your restriction was wrong?”

“No.”

“What did they do?”

“Made an exception.”

“Why?”

“Bridge.”

“Yes.”

There.

Local.

Practical.

Not absolution.

Still uncomfortable.

“Do you think they were wrong?”

I looked at the bridge.

People crossing the closed approach now.

Workers only.

Soon everyone.

“No.”

That was the problem.

Tracy nodded.

“Then remember this when you talk about Hale.”

I looked at her.

Anger came.

Fast.

“He murdered a town.”

“Yes.”

“I killed one responsible person.”

“Yes.”

“Restrained.”

“Yes.”

“Not the same.”

“No.”

Again.

She agreed.

That took some air out.

“Then what are you saying?”

“That usefulness does not become innocence.”

“I know.”

“Good.”

She looked at the bridge.

“And guilt does not always make usefulness disappear.”

There.

Harder.

I knew that too.

Hated it.

“That is not forgiveness.”

“No.”

“Not clearance.”

“No.”

“Not fairness.”

“No.”

“Then what?”

“Tuesday.”

I stared.

“What?”

She shrugged.

“The bridge was failing Tuesday.”

Of course.

Current Tracy.

Not theory.

Schedule.

Problem existed on a day.

People needed someone.

Institution used who it had.

Life did not wait for moral closure.

I hated that enough to remember it forever.

My emergency reinstatement became limited reinstatement after another month.

Then conditional contract access.

Not because Sera stopped mattering.

Because I complied with review.

Because I did useful work.

Because Authority needed support.

Because institutions prefer managed risk to wasted capacity.

Same logic.

Different scale.

The first time someone called me an exception, I flinched.

Not visibly.

Probably.

Tracy noticed.

Of course.

“Do not become the story you hate just because the paperwork rhymes.”

Good line.

I looked at her.

“Current data?”

“Yes.”

Better.

Ithar shifted in the cave.

“So they used you.”

“Yes.”

“After you killed a restrained prisoner.”

“She was not a prisoner yet.”

He stared.

“Fine.”

Growth.

“Yes.”

“And you went.”

“Yes.”

“Would you again?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

I looked at him.

“Because the bridge did not kill Sera.”

He waited.

“And the people under it did not kill Nessa.”

There.

That was becoming a rule.

Not enough to save me from everything.

Enough to save some people from me.
