## RECORD 119

## THE EMPTY SLOT

The first month after Hale was terrible.

Not grief.

Schedule.

I woke early because I had always trained early.

Then remembered I had no target date.

I checked information channels out of habit.

No Hale.

No movement.

No assignment.

No new report.

Nothing.

The absence became a task I could not complete.

Tracy found this funny for two days.

Then less funny.

“You need work.”

“I have work.”

“You need a problem.”

“I have many.”

“You need one you cannot solve by staring at a wall.”

Fair.

I took more contracts.

Not reckless.

Just more.

Structural support.

Field stabilization.

One coastal storm.

Two mine collapses.

A bridge.

Always bridges.

People continued needing things.

Good.

Useful.

Not enough.

The loop remained.

That surprised me.

Some part of me had assumed killing Hale would make the architecture unnecessary.

Bad thinking.

Bodies do not care why they adapted.

Magic even less.

Plan B did not evaporate because the target did.

I remained B-class.

Solo-heavy.

Narrow.

Good at ugly problems.

Worse than I might have been with another decade of outward work.

That was mine now.

No refund.

Tracy made me test anyway.

Outward link.

Closed loop.

Endurance.

Recovery.

Graph.

Still hated graph.

“Why?”

“Because Hale is dead.”

“Yes.”

“So?”

“So your body remains alive.”

Annoying.

We tried reopening breadth.

Not Plan A fully.

Too late for that.

Maintenance.

Preservation.

Some recovery.

The first six months gained a little.

Then stopped.

Not zero.

Not useless.

Just expensive.

Hours for inches.

Current Greg had become the current Greg.

That sounds obvious.

It was not.

I had spent two lives treating identity like a thing memory preserved.

It did not.

Memory preserved instructions.

The body became whatever followed them long enough.

Ithar shifted.

“You could not return.”

“Not reasonably.”

“Could you have?”

“Maybe.”

“How long?”

“Years.”

“How many?”

“I do not know.”

“You keep saying that.”

“Because bodies are rude.”

He waited.

I sighed.

“Enough that I stopped asking.”

That was later.

First I tried.

Year one after Hale, I trained outward three days a week.

Hated it.

Not because outward support was bad.

Because I remembered being better.

Memory is cruel when skill returns slowly.

I could feel the missing bandwidth.

The old shape.

Not gone.

Too far.

Like remembering how large a room used to be after someone built walls inside it.

Useful room.

Smaller.

Mine.

Still.

Tracy refused nostalgia.

Good.

“If the link works, it works.”

“It used to carry four people.”

“It carries two now.”

“Exactly.”

“Two are not zero.”

“That is not the point.”

“It is today.”

Current Tracy.

Always today.

I worked.

Two became cleaner.

Not three.

Fine.

Then the thing happened by accident.

Most important things did.

Guild training hall.

Regional branch.

I was there for a support evaluation.

Not mine.

I had started occasionally reviewing strange cases because Named status turns you into unpaid expertise if you are not careful.

I charged.

Growth.

The case was boring.

A barrier specialist with inconsistent recovery.

Not relevant.

I finished.

Walked past the combat floor.

Stopped.

Twenty-three people training.

Maybe twenty-four.

One caught my attention.

Not because he was exceptional.

Opposite.

Good.

That was all.

Young man.

Early twenties.

Reinforcement combat.

Short spear.

Competent stance.

Reasonable mana control.

Too much shoulder.

Bad left-side recovery.

Overcommitted through the front hip.

Nothing special.

I watched.

He lost to someone better.

Not badly.

Learned.

Second round.

Changed one thing.

Good.

Still lost.

I kept watching.

Tracy would later call this creepy.

Fair.

The young man had something familiar.

Not face.

Not height exactly.

Not personality.

Magic.

The way load moved.

The way reinforcement entered through the front side first and spread late.

The way his channels had begun specializing around combat habits without becoming fully committed.

Young enough.

Developed enough.

Not fixed.

I knew that body.

Not his.

Mine.

Approximately.

First-Life mine.

Before support.

Before decades.

Before Arcrutus.

Before Vey.

Before everything.

I left.

That should have been the end.

It was not.

The next week I found a reason to return.

Professional.

Technically.

The barrier specialist needed follow-up.

He did not.

I went anyway.

The young combatant was there.

Same spear.

Same bad shoulder.

Different opponent.

Won this time.

Good.

Not great.

Exactly.

I asked the floor coordinator his name.

“Kelan Orr.”

There.

Name.

“Rank?”

“D provisional.”

“Age?”

The coordinator looked at me.

“Why?”

Fair.

“Support question.”

That made it worse.

“Twenty-two.”

Close.

Not identical.

Good.

Nothing should be identical.

“Background?”

“Local.”

“Family?”

Now he stared.

I stopped.

Good.

Not a specimen.

Person.

Important.

I went home.

Tracy noticed the problem before I spoke.

“Face.”

I looked at her.

There.

The joke belonged to us now.

“What face?”

“Bad idea.”

“I have no idea.”

“Worse.”

I told her about Kelan.

Not everything.

Just:

Young combatant.

Interesting reinforcement pattern.

Maybe support aptitude.

She listened.

Then asked:

“Does he want to be support?”

“I don’t know.”

“Then you have nothing.”

Fair.

“I could ask.”

“Yes.”

I did.

Kelan looked at me like I was selling religion.

“No.”

Good start.

“You didn’t hear the offer.”

“I’m a spear.”

“You can remain a spear.”

“That sounds fake.”

Fair.

“I want to test some support concepts.”

“On me?”

“With you.”

He frowned.

Better.

“Why?”

Because you resemble a younger version of me in ways I have not admitted matter.

Bad.

“Because your reinforcement is good and inefficient.”

His face changed.

Insult.

Useful.

“Fuck you.”

I liked him immediately.

Bad sign.

“Your left recovery is late.”

He stopped.

“Shoulder starts compensating by the third heavy sequence.”

Silence.

“Front hip carries too much.”

His expression changed again.

Not offense now.

Interest.

“How do you know?”

“I watched.”

“That is creepy.”

Tracy had been right.

“Probably.”

“What do you want?”

“Three sessions.”

“Why?”

“See if relational support improves your combat without destroying the combat.”

He stared.

“What does that mean?”

Good question.

I smiled.

“Exactly.”

He said no again.

Then asked how much I would pay.

Excellent.

We agreed on three sessions.

Not mentorship.

Not yet.

No destiny.

No chosen one.

Paid experimentation.

That phrasing becomes worse later.

At the time it was accurate.

First session, Kelan hated Barrier work.

Second, hated load-sharing more.

Third, he accidentally reinforced someone else’s stance during a paired drill and both fighters looked confused.

I did not.

There.

Tiny.

Nothing.

Everything.

Ithar’s head lifted.

“You found someone like you.”

“No.”

Too fast.

His eyes narrowed.

Good dragon.

“I found someone with useful starting conditions.”

“Like you.”

“Similar.”

“How similar?”

“Enough.”

“Did you tell him?”

“Tell him what?”

“That you recognized yourself.”

I looked at the cave wall.

Not yet.

Different question.

At the time I told myself I was helping a good young fighter become more useful.

That was true.

Important.

It was also not the only thing happening.

I did not say that to Ithar.

Not then.

I said:

“He was good.”

“Not great?”

“No.”

“Why did that matter?”

I looked at him.

Because prodigies contaminate experiments.

Because I had not been one.

Because if a normal good combatant could become relational support earlier than I had, then maybe the path was reproducible.

All answers.

Too many.

So I gave him the safest one.

“Great people make bad baselines.”

Ithar went still.

I should have heard myself.

I did not.
