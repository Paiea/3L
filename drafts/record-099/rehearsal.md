## RECORD 099

## THE OTHER

Tracy canceled the next consultation.

Not dramatically.

A note.

RESCHEDULE.

Then the one after that.

UNAVAILABLE.

The third had no replacement date.

I understood.

Mostly.

Understanding did not improve my mood.

The loop improved.

That did.

Bad combination.

I practiced alone.

Short sessions.

Low load.

Then higher.

Standing first.

Then turning.

Then impact.

Then controlled instability.

The peg became useful.

Not better than a leg.

Never that.

A leg changes angle.

Absorbs.

Corrects.

Feels.

The peg did none of those things naturally.

It was worse.

Which made it predictable.

Predictability can be useful.

So can a wall.

That does not make a wall a better leg.

I learned where the socket failed.

Where the strap shifted.

Where skin broke down.

Where the wooden foot slid.

Where the floor mattered.

Different surfaces changed everything.

Stone.

Wood.

Mud.

Packed earth.

Loose gravel.

The loop had to account for contact.

That became terrain anchoring later.

At the time it was mostly falling less.

Three weeks after Tracy stopped scheduling me, I went back to the Guild branch.

Not to see her.

That is technically true.

I needed medical stock.

She was at the counter.

Of course.

She saw me.

Looked away.

Professional.

I bought what I needed from another clerk.

Also professional.

Then stood there too long.

Less professional.

Tracy looked up.

“What?”

“I owe you an explanation.”

“No.”

That surprised me.

“You asked.”

“I asked when I was being paid to care.”

Fair.

“I still think you should know.”

“Why?”

Because I wanted her back.

Wrong answer.

Because she deserved context.

Better.

“Because I keep reacting to you for things you didn’t do.”

That got her attention.

Not softness.

Attention.

“After work,” she said.

Progress.

We met at a tea shop.

Not Marrin’s.

There was no Marrin’s for us.

Not like that.

Different place.

Different life.

Tracy ordered something sweet.

I noticed.

Did not treat it like discovery.

Good.

We sat.

She waited.

I had explained reincarnation before.

To myself.

To journals.

To people who did not believe me.

There was no clean version.

“I lived before.”

Tracy stared.

“Poor start.”

“Yes.”

“Try again.”

“I remember another life.”

“Better grammar.”

“Same problem.”

“Yes.”

She drank.

“Go.”

So I did.

Not everything.

Not Nhal.

Not Ithar.

Not the entire architecture of history.

Enough.

Nineteen.

Waking again.

Remembered family alive.

Different choices.

Different work.

Nessa.

Children.

Hale.

The town.

The leg.

I left First-Life Tracy for last.

Not because she mattered least.

Because that was the part I was asking her to carry.

“In the first life, I met you later.”

“How much later?”

“About twelve years.”

Her eyes changed.

Calculation.

“How old was I?”

I told her.

“And you?”

I told her.

“Rank?”

“Me?”

“Yes.”

“S-class support.”

She did not laugh.

Good.

“You?”

“D when we met.”

That did make her smile.

“Still?”

“No.”

“What happened?”

I thought about expensive fish.

“A lot.”

“Helpful.”

“You became A-class in poison and exposure medicine.”

She stopped smiling.

Not disbelief.

Professional offense.

“A.”

“In that specialty.”

“Not general healing.”

“No.”

“Good.”

Interesting first reaction.

She cared about accuracy before grandeur.

Still Tracy.

“You worked with me about ten years.”

“Doing what?”

“Everything.”

“Bad answer.”

“Poison. Field medicine. Maintenance. Business. Logistics. Contracts. Eventually most of the organization.”

She stared.

“What organization?”

“Long story.”

“You are already telling a long story.”

Fair.

“Marsh operation. Transport. Processing. Property. Restaurant.”

“Restaurant?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Food.”

That got the first real laugh.

Small.

“Of course.”

I almost said you loved it.

Stopped.

She noticed.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“No.”

I breathed.

“You liked food.”

She stared.

“That is your evidence?”

“No.”

“It is currently the strongest evidence.”

Fair.

“You also liked money.”

“That is less remarkable.”

“You liked money a lot.”

“Still weak.”

“You did not care much if people thought you were smart.”

She stopped.

“Competent.”

Still.

“Hardworking.”

Her eyes narrowed.

“You cared if they paid you.”

Long pause.

Then:

“Continue.”

So I did.

Ten-year joke.

Contract.

Half.

She interrupted.

“Half?”

“Yes.”

“You gave me half.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because it worked.”

“That is not why you did it.”

Fair.

“Because I thought it would.”

“Based on?”

“Two days.”

She stared.

“First-Life me worked for an idiot.”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

That helped.

I told her about the marsh.

Prophylaxis.

Glassroot.

House-money antidotes.

Transport.

The yard.

Pella.

She stopped me.

“Who is Pella?”

“A horse.”

“Why are you telling me about a horse?”

“Because you refused to sell her.”

“Why?”

“She worked there twelve years.”

Tracy looked down at her tea.

That was the first detail that affected her strangely.

Not A-class.

Not half.

A retired mare.

“Did she live?”

“Yes.”

“How long?”

“Years.”

“Good.”

Small word.

Important.

I told her about Marrin’s.

The bread.

The building.

The private room.

Arcrutus.

“Who?”

“S-class.”

“And he worked for us?”

“Yes.”

“Us.”

There.

First time.

I corrected.

“First-Life us.”

She looked at me.

“Good.”

Boundary already forming.

I told her about Number Two.

Not title.

Function.

If Greg disappeared, did the system continue?

Eventually yes.

Because Tracy.

She listened.

Then asked:

“How long did I work for you?”

“About ten years.”

“Did I like you?”

I laughed.

Could not help it.

“Eventually.”

She almost smiled.

“Did I trust you?”

That one hurt.

“Yes.”

Tracy looked at me for a long time.

Then set the cup down.

“Do not use hers.”

There.

Clean.

I knew what she meant.

Did not want to.

“Tracy.”

“No.”

“I am not trying to manipulate you.”

“You already are.”

That hurt because it was partly true.

“Not intentionally.”

“I believe you.”

Worse.

She leaned forward.

“You look at me and react to a woman I have not become.”

“Yes.”

“You expect jokes I have not heard.”

“Yes.”

“You expect me to notice things I may not notice.”

“Yes.”

“You get pleased when I disagree because she disagreed.”

“Yes.”

“You get disappointed when I don’t.”

I said nothing.

There.

“That is spending her trust.”

“No.”

“Yes.”

“I have not asked you to trust me.”

“You act surprised when I don’t.”

Silence.

Correct.

She continued.

“I am not saying she was not me.”

That surprised me.

“I don’t know what I think yet.”

Fair.

“But if your story is true, she had ten years.”

“Yes.”

“I have had four consultations.”

“Three and a half.”

She stared.

“Do not.”

“Sorry.”

“Ten years.”

“Yes.”

“She earned whatever she knew about you.”

“Yes.”

“She earned whatever she could say to you.”

“Yes.”

“You earned whatever you knew about her.”

“Yes.”

“Then do not bring that bill here.”

Fuck.

Good line.

Not polished.

Her.

I looked down.

“I don’t know how not to.”

First honest answer.

Tracy sat back.

That changed the conversation.

“Okay.”

“What?”

“Okay.”

“You are not mad?”

“I am.”

“Good.”

“Why good?”

“Familiar.”

Bad.

Her eyes narrowed.

I raised a hand.

“Sorry.”

She exhaled.

Then:

“I believe you believe this.”

“That is not the same.”

“No.”

“What would make you believe it?”

“I don’t know.”

Fair.

“Technical things?”

“Maybe.”

“Future events?”

“Dangerous.”

I looked at her.

Good.

She saw it immediately.

“If you tell me things about my life before they happen, I cannot unknow them.”

“Yes.”

“Do not do that casually.”

“I won’t.”

“Good.”

“Your A-class—”

She held up one finger.

I stopped.

Good.

She thought.

Then asked:

“Did I tell you no a lot?”

“Yes.”

“Did you listen?”

“Eventually.”

“Bad.”

“Yes.”

“Did I ever stop you?”

“Sometimes.”

“Did you ever do it anyway?”

“Yes.”

“How often?”

I smiled.

She did not.

“Enough.”

“And this loop?”

“First-Life you would have hated it.”

“Why?”

“Because it closes pathways I used outward.”

“Permanently?”

“Eventually.”

She went still.

“Eventually.”

“Yes.”

“You are choosing that now.”

“Starting to.”

“For Hale.”

“Yes.”

“And you want me involved.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

I thought.

Not because you owe me.

Not because you were Number Two.

Not because I miss you.

All true.

Bad reasons.

“Because you are good at telling me when I am lying to myself.”

Tracy looked at me.

Current.

Not remembered.

Current.

“That I can do.”

Hope moved.

She killed it quickly.

“For money.”

I laughed.

She smiled.

Good.

“Terms?”

“One consultation at a time.”

Not ten years.

Good.

“I do not help optimize the loop.”

“Fine.”

“I will treat consequences.”

“Fine.”

“I will tell you when I think you are making yourself worse.”

“Fine.”

“You do not get to say ‘you always’ unless I actually always do something.”

That one hurt.

“Fine.”

“You do not compare me to her in an argument.”

“Fine.”

“You do not tell me what she would do to pressure me.”

“Fine.”

“You do not expect me to become Number Two.”

I hesitated.

She saw it.

“Greg.”

“Fine.”

“Say it like you mean it.”

“I do not expect you to become Number Two.”

“Good.”

She picked up her tea.

Then:

“And if I ask about her, you answer.”

I looked at her.

“Okay.”

“That does not mean volunteer.”

“I understand.”

“Do you?”

“Probably not.”

“Better.”

We finished the tea.

No contract.

No half.

No guarantee.

No future.

One consultation at a time.

That was how Second-Life Tracy stayed.

Not as restoration.

As herself.

Ithar watched me.

“And she believed you?”

“Not then.”

“When?”

“Later.”

“How?”

I rubbed my thumb across my palm.

“There are only so many impossible technical predictions you can make before a healer becomes curious.”

His eyes narrowed.

“You proved your prior life through knowledge.”

“Partly.”

“And through Hale?”

I looked at him.

“Hale helped.”

“How?”

“He knew I should not know what I knew.”

The cave quieted.

Ithar waited.

I sighed.

“Second time I went after Hale, I did not go alone.”
