## RECORD 132

## THE OTHER THING

The climb back down took less time.

Maybe because I knew there was shopping now.

Ithar disagreed with the word.

That made it better.

We returned to the hoard chamber.

The sword remained outside.

Empty.

Hungry.

A problem for later.

Ithar moved past the armor plate.

Past the lens.

Past the memory object.

Then stopped at the wooden case.

The three pale arcs.

Right.

Those.

I had already liked those.

I liked them more now.

He opened the case.

Nothing dramatic happened.

Good artifact.

Quiet.

Suspicious.

I crouched.

“What exactly do they preserve?”

“Route.”

“You said that.”

“Yes.”

“I am asking what that means.”

“No. You are asking me to make an old thing fit your newer words.”

“Those are the only words I have.”

“Then listen carefully.”

I did.

Mostly.

He lifted one of the arcs.

It flexed slightly between two claws.

Not metal exactly.

Something that had decided to behave like metal.

“A channel opens.”

“Yes.”

“From you.”

“Yes.”

“To something else.”

“Yes.”

“Living?”

“Could be.”

“Object?”

“Could be.”

“Structure?”

“Could be.”

“Good.”

Ithar ignored that.

“The route is held while active.”

“Yes.”

“When released, the body ordinarily forgets most of the exact shape.”

I frowned.

“Not forgets.”

“Your objection is vocabulary.”

“Fair.”

“Channels settle back toward preferred architecture.”

There.

Better.

First Life.

Outward.

Second Life.

Closed.

Preference.

Habit.

Scar.

Whatever word.

Ithar tapped the pale arc.

“This can retain part of that shape.”

“For how long?”

“Hours. Sometimes days.”

“Enough to reopen it.”

“Perhaps.”

“Faster.”

“Yes.”

“Cleaner.”

“Yes.”

“More consistently.”

“Yes.”

There.

That was already useful.

Very useful.

Then I looked at the sword.

Not here.

Still.

Conceptually.

“Could it hold an object route?”

Ithar was quiet.

Good.

“Could it?”

“Yes.”

“How?”

“By doing what you already know how to do.”

I stared.

“That answer is never as reassuring as you think.”

“It is not meant to reassure.”

He placed the arc back in the case.

Then drew on the floor.

One line outward.

Me.

Something else.

“First life.”

I nodded.

“Open.”

“Yes.”

Another line.

Out and back.

“Second.”

“Closed.”

“No.”

I looked at him.

“No?”

“You describe it as closed because it became self-contained.”

“Yes.”

“That is not the same as never opening.”

I stared at the shape.

Right.

The route still moved.

It just returned.

He drew a third diagram.

Outward again.

Toward a small mark.

Then around it.

Then back.

I understood before he said anything.

“No.”

His eye shifted.

Good.

“Open outward.”

“Yes.”

“Close around.”

“Yes.”

“Object.”

“Yes.”

I looked at the little loop.

Greg.

Object.

Greg.

Not a wall.

Not simple reinforcement.

Not cast Barrier onto sword.

Not pour mana into weapon.

A route that included the object before returning.

I hated how obvious it looked.

“I’ve done both halves.”

“Yes.”

“Just not together.”

“Yes.”

“Fuck.”

“Yes.”

I looked at him.

“You knew I would hate that.”

“Yes.”

Good dragon.

Bad dragon.

“What did humans call it?”

“Many things.”

“Useful.”

“None survived cleanly into your language.”

“Less useful.”

“Most practices disappeared.”

“How long ago?”

“Depends where.”

“Here.”

“Properly?”

He thought.

“Two thousand years.”

I stared.

“Two thousand.”

“Approximately.”

“And you have seen it.”

“Yes.”

“Humans.”

“Yes.”

“Doing what?”

Ithar looked at me.

Then toward the passage where the sword waited outside.

There.

“No.”

“Yes.”

“Flying sword?”

“Sometimes.”

I laughed.

He did not.

That made it worse.

“You are serious.”

“Yes.”

“Like actually flying around.”

“Yes.”

“Without holding it.”

“Yes.”

“Attacking.”

“Yes.”

“Blocking.”

“Yes.”

“Coming back.”

“Yes.”

I stared.

Ithar continued.

“At higher mastery, the object is treated less as carried equipment and more as external anatomy.”

That stopped me.

“External anatomy.”

“Yes.”

“You feel it.”

“Yes.”

“Position.”

“Yes.”

“Load.”

“Yes.”

“Damage.”

“Yes.”

“Can move it remotely.”

“Yes.”

“How far?”

“Depends.”

“I hate that word.”

“I know.”

I looked at the loop again.

Greg.

Object.

Greg.

“What else?”

“Some stood on them.”

I looked up.

“No.”

“Yes.”

“Stood.”

“Yes.”

“On the weapon.”

“Yes.”

“In the air.”

“Yes.”

I smiled.

Ithar’s eyes narrowed.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“No.”

“I am reconsidering the sword.”

“You were already considering the sword.”

“More.”

“That was not the purpose of this explanation.”

“Flying.”

“Yes.”

“On the sword.”

“Yes.”

“That is very difficult to market against.”

Ithar closed his eyes.

Good.

I looked back at the pale arcs.

“Why did humans stop?”

“Because it is a poor compromise for most humans.”

There.

Important.

“Explain.”

“You commit development to an object.”

“Yes.”

“If the object breaks?”

“You lose the route.”

“More.”

“How much more?”

“Depending on depth, loss can resemble losing part of the body.”

I stopped.

Phantom structure.

Dead routes.

Channels ending somewhere no longer present.

That was bad.

Good.

Real.

“Can you switch objects?”

“Sometimes.”

“How long?”

“Years.”

“Fuck.”

“Yes.”

“Then why would anybody do it?”

“Because the result can be exceptional.”

I looked at the sword again in my head.

Of course.

“And because older practitioners did not always have your modern alternatives.”

“Range casting.”

“Yes.”

“Replaceable gear.”

“Yes.”

“Standard reinforcement.”

“Yes.”

“External support.”

“Yes.”

“Better compromises.”

“Usually.”

There.

Not secret superior magic.

Good.

Worse.

That made it more believable.

“What about temporary use?”

I asked.

“Without permanent binding.”

“Yes.”

“Possible.”

“How useful?”

“Less.”

“Still more than normal reinforcement.”

“Yes.”

“Could current me do that?”

“No.”

Fast.

I frowned.

“Why?”

“Your channels are mature.”

“So?”

“They prefer known architecture.”

“I can learn.”

“Yes.”

“But?”

“You have spent decades making them very good at refusing ambiguity.”

That landed.

Closed loop.

Hale.

Revenge.

Self-contained survival.

I had trained my body to choose one.

Now I wanted it to choose both.

“Show me.”

“No.”

“I want to try.”

“Yes.”

“That sounded like approval.”

“It was not.”

He pushed the case toward me.

I stared.

“Touch?”

“Yes.”

Good.

I picked up one pale arc.

Warm.

Not from magic.

From cave.

Disappointing.

I held it.

“What now?”

“Open outward.”

“To?”

“The arc.”

I did.

Small.

Careful.

A thread.

My channel extended.

The arc accepted.

Easy.

Too easy.

I smiled.

Then tried to return the route.

Nothing.

Not nothing.

My body closed it.

Back into me.

Before the arc.

I frowned.

Again.

Out.

Contact.

Return.

Snap.

Back to self.

Again.

Same.

Again.

Faster.

Same.

Ithar watched.

I hated him.

“You are choosing one.”

“I am not choosing anything.”

“Your body is.”

There.

Exactly.

I tried harder.

Worse.

The route folded inward before reaching the arc.

My current architecture was helping me.

That was the problem.

“I understand the theory.”

“Yes.”

“My channels do not care.”

“Yes.”

“Could I force it?”

“Yes.”

“Damage?”

“Likely.”

“Permanent?”

“Possible.”

I lowered the arc.

Annoying.

Useful.

“First-Life me?”

“Perhaps.”

“Better chance?”

“Yes.”

“Second-Life young me?”

“You.”

“Nineteen.”

“Yes.”

“Before I build anything.”

“Yes.”

There.

The cave got smaller again.

Not because of the artifact.

Because of youth.

A third run.

A body with options.

No outward habit yet.

No inward scar yet.

No decades of preference.

Just possibility.

“What would I have to give up?”

I asked.

Ithar did not answer immediately.

Good.

Real answer.

“Time.”

“Obviously.”

“Channel capacity elsewhere.”

“Yes.”

“Flexibility.”

“Yes.”

“Physical resilience.”

I looked at him.

“What?”

“If a meaningful portion of your channel architecture rests outside your body permanently, that portion does not sustain your body.”

I frowned.

“Channels sustain tissue?”

“Partly.”

“Enough to matter?”

“Yes.”

“How?”

“You age.”

I laughed once.

“I already do that.”

“Faster.”

There.

I stopped.

“How much?”

“Depends.”

“Of course.”

“Object matters.”

I looked toward the passage.

Sword.

Hungry sword.

Good.

Bad.

“If the object draws from me continuously…”

“Yes.”

“Worse.”

“Yes.”

“How much worse?”

“I do not know.”

“Visible?”

“Eventually.”

“Gray hair at thirty?”

“Possibly.”

“Looking fifty at forty?”

“Possibly.”

“Dead at fifty?”

“Possible.”

I looked at him.

He did not soften it.

Good.

I looked down at my hands.

Old hands.

Current hands.

Almost sixty.

Again.

I had already spent one life reaching this age.

Then another.

The number did not scare me as much as it probably should.

Ithar noticed.

“That expression is stupid.”

“What?”

“You are thinking you have already lived enough years.”

I stared.

“How do you keep doing that?”

“You are repetitive.”

Fair.

“I am not saying I would do it.”

“You are deciding the cost is acceptable because you have paid age before.”

I looked away.

There.

Bad.

Maybe true.

“Appearance is not the cost,” Ithar said.

“I know.”

“Reserve.”

“Yes.”

“Recovery.”

“Yes.”

“Later life.”

“Yes.”

“Possibly length.”

“Yes.”

I stared at the pale arc.

Then toward the sword.

Two decisions.

Maybe three.

Retrieve sword.

Use sword.

Build around sword.

Not the same.

Good.

Important.

“If I never bind permanently…”

“You keep more options.”

“Yes.”

“If I use the arcs to practice temporary object routes…”

“Possible.”

“If I later find something better…”

“You could choose then.”

I smiled.

Ithar’s eyes narrowed.

“What?”

“That sounds suspiciously like advice.”

“It is not.”

“Very good advice.”

“No.”

“Thank you.”

“Greg.”

I put the arc back.

Carefully.

Then closed the case.

“So the sword is still easier.”

“Yes.”

“This is harder to get.”

“Yes.”

“But this lets me decide what my channels become.”

“Perhaps.”

“And the sword mostly gives me power.”

“Mostly.”

I looked at him.

“That is why this is more interesting.”

“Yes.”

There.

Not stronger.

Not better.

More interesting.

Because one solved problems.

The other changed which problems I could solve later.

I hated that.

Good sign.

“Could I trade the sword for this?”

“No.”

I frowned.

“You said people trade down.”

“I would not.”

“You own both.”

“Yes.”

“Greedy.”

“Yes.”

Fair.

“What did Sava Ren want?”

Ithar went still.

There.

Good.

“You remember.”

“Yes.”

“What?”

“No.”

“Ithar.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because you are already planning around a woman you have never met in a life you may never have.”

“That has not stopped us before.”

“It should have.”

I smiled.

He did not.

Then I looked at the case.

At the sword outside.

At the diagrams.

At everything we had built from assumptions.

Death.

Return.

Nineteen.

Lake.

Sava Ren.

Object routes.

Faster aging.

Flying swords.

Mountains.

A whole life sitting there like a menu.

Ridiculous.

I laughed.

“What?”

I shook my head.

“We have planned years.”

“Yes.”

“For a person who does not exist.”

“Yes.”

“Around objects I may never touch.”

“Yes.”

“Based on an experiment where failure means I am dead.”

“Yes.”

I looked at him.

“Good.”

“No.”

I smiled.

“Now it feels like a plan.”
