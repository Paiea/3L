## RECORD 085

## THE COST

We did not leave Pava last.

We tried very hard not to.

That distinction mattered.

Mael rewrote the operation around removing her shaping field before anything else ambitious happened.

No second limb.

No tail work.

No further restraint release.

Get Pava out of the foundation.

Then breathe.

Reasonable plan.

The foundation disagreed.

For the next three hours we taught an ancient broken system not to depend on a woman it had met that morning.

Slowly.

One relationship at a time.

Survey identified which migrated paths had begun using Pava's center field.

Structural found physical capacity for some.

Arcrutus supplied raw force where equipment could not.

Mael held the east correction.

Nhal adjusted her own internal distribution when asked and when she chose.

I moved the crossings.

Pava remained the shape everything else referenced.

That was the problem.

You cannot remove the ruler while everyone is still measuring against it.

So we made new rulers.

Ugly ones.

A plate for one angle.

A rail for another.

A pressure anchor for a third.

Arcrutus's left hand for something no tool should ever have been asked to do.

He complained exactly once.

"My fingers are not equipment."

Pava said, "Today they are."

He stopped complaining.

At the start, nineteen mapped relationships depended partly on Pava's field.

After forty minutes, fourteen.

After another hour, nine.

Then six.

Progress became numbers because nobody wanted to say hope.

Pava sat against the lower wall while shaping.

That looked wrong.

S-class pressure work should have looked grander.

Hands raised.

Wind.

Light.

Something for painters.

Instead she sat with one knee up, wrapped forearm across it, breathing through her nose while a broken continent-sized piece of old infrastructure quietly used her as punctuation.

The healer checked her every five minutes.

Pava hated that.

Good.

At six relationships, the healer said, "Pulse irregular."

Pava said, "How irregular?"

"Enough that I said it aloud."

Mael looked at me.

"Can we accelerate?"

I looked at the remaining six.

Two were easy.

Three were bad.

One was Pava.

Not literally.

Functionally.

A rotational correction that changed direction every time the east face shifted against the new bridge.

Nothing physical we had installed could update that fast.

Arcrutus could.

Wrong precision.

Mael could.

Wrong position.

Nhal could.

Wrong person to load again.

Pava could.

Already was.

"Some," I said.

Mael heard the missing word.

Not enough.

He nodded.

"Do some."

Good leader.

We moved two quickly.

Four left.

Pava's hand stopped trembling.

That should have been good.

"Can you feel your fingers?"

"Yes."

"All of them?"

"Most."

"Define most."

"Not now."

Mael said, "Pava."

"Thumb and first two. Other two are distant."

Arcrutus looked at me.

"Move more."

"Move it into me."

"Not all cost is interchangeable."

So did I.

Pava's shaping was not one thing.

Part force.

Part timing.

Part continuous correction.

Part her knowing pressure so intimately that she changed it before our instruments finished admitting it had changed.

I could move force.

I could move recoil.

I could move some channel return.

I could not move being Pava Kett.

That remained inconveniently attached to Pava Kett.

We got to three.

Then two.

When I closed it out of Pava's field, she sagged against the wall.

Healer moved immediately.

Pava waved her away with the unwrapped hand.

"One."

One remaining.

The rotational correction.

The fast one.

Survey had started calling it the chatter path because every tiny motion across the fracture made it change.

I hated the name.

Accurate.

The new physical bridge could resist rotation.

It could not anticipate it.

If we moved the chatter path too early, the bridge would take alternating shocks instead of continuous load.

Metal does not enjoy surprises.

Neither do people.

Structural had a solution.

Another plate.

Of course.

Not just any plate.

A short flex plate mounted across the hinge collar to damp the first motion before the heavy rails caught it.

Simple.

Physical.

Repairable.

I loved it immediately.

"Twenty minutes."

"Medical estimate."

"I cannot promise twenty."

"Maybe."

"Fifteen?"

"No."

"You are not an estimate of yourself."

"I am an S-class pressure specialist. I know what my field is doing."

"You do not know what your heart is doing."

Pava looked at her.

"Bad?"

"Yes."

Good sentence.

Pava looked at the fracture.

"How?"

Not challenge.

Question.

Maybe not.

Maybe once.

Wrong direction twice.

Part.

Unknown.

"I don't know."

"Explain."

"Ten minutes gets that close. Fifteen maybe seats it. Emergency transfer risks the whole lower bridge now."

"Yes."

No pretending.
