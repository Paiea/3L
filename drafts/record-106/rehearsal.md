## RECORD 106

## THE FIRST

Sera Dain lived above a cooper’s shop.

That annoyed me.

Not because cooperage is offensive.

Because revenge should not have barrels underneath it.

The shop closed at dusk.

Sera entered from the alley after.

Third floor.

Rear room.

One window.

One roof exit.

One stair.

Good.

I watched two nights.

Not because I was patient.

Because I had learned.

Different.

She left in the morning.

Returned late.

Bought food once.

Carried demolition cord twice.

Spoke to nobody long.

No family visible.

No roommates.

No children.

I was relieved by that.

Then ashamed of the relief.

Then used it anyway.

Third night.

I went in.

No team.

No Tracy.

No Authority.

Just me.

That mattered.

The stair creaked.

Of course.

Old building.

Sera heard.

Good.

No surprise murder.

I reached the landing.

Door closed.

Then opened.

Fast.

A wire snapped across the hall.

I saw it.

Too late to avoid.

Early enough to answer.

Barrier.

Thin.

Not block.

Redirect.

The charge went sideways into the wall.

Wood exploded.

My peg slipped.

Loop closed.

Floor.

Hip.

Torso.

Return.

Stable.

Smoke.

Sera moved through it.

Short blade.

No armor.

Prepared channels.

C-class.

Maybe B in the room.

She had built the room.

That mattered.

First strike low.

Testing the peg.

Smart.

I turned the contact plate into the floor.

Took the impact through the loop.

Pain.

Manageable.

Returned load through the Barrier at her wrist.

Not enough to injure.

Enough to change angle.

Her blade missed.

She noticed.

Good.

“What are you?”

No greeting.

Apparently revenge people had stopped saying hello.

“Greg.”

Her face changed.

There.

She knew.

Not personally.

Story.

Hale’s leg.

East Gate.

Joren.

The man who kept coming.

“You should have brought people.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Testing something.”

She stared.

That offended her more than the attack.

Fair.

She stepped back.

The room changed.

Not physically.

Tactically.

She had marks on the floor.

Anchor points.

Prepared charges.

Two wall cavities.

One ceiling line.

Demolition specialist.

Everything had a failure she could invite.

Good.

So did I.

She pulled one line.

The floor under my left side dropped half an inch.

Not collapse.

Tilt.

Enough.

Old Greg would have widened support.

Linked structure.

Stabilized room.

Second-Life Greg did something else.

Closed.

Peg to floor.

Floor to Barrier.

Barrier to hip.

I did not save the room.

I saved me.

That distinction was the whole decade.

Sera expected correction outward.

Got none.

The floor kept tilting.

She had planned around me trying to fix it.

I stepped into the failure instead.

Her eyes changed.

Good.

I used the falling floor as load.

Draw against the hinge line.

Not stop.

Delay.

One fraction.

Her own prepared collapse pulled wrong.

She shifted.

I hit her.

Not hard.

Correct.

Palm to shoulder.

Barrier behind.

Her body could not give backward where it expected.

The force went sideways.

Shoulder dislocated.

She swore.

Blade dropped.

I kicked it away.

Bad kick.

Peg work remained inelegant.

She reached for the wall line with her good hand.

I anchored her wrist to the plaster.

Not literal glue.

Boundary.

For half a second the hand had nowhere useful to go.

I struck the elbow.

She screamed.

Good.

Alive.

The room smelled like dust and burned cord.

Below us someone shouted.

Cooper.

Probably wondering why his ceiling was becoming theoretical.

I had to finish quickly.

Sera looked at me.

Really looked.

“You learned.”

“Yes.”

“Hale said you would.”

I hated that.

“What else did Hale say?”

She laughed.

Bad choice.

I tightened the wrist boundary.

She stopped.

“What else?”

“That he should have killed you.”

Interesting.

“He told Joren otherwise.”

“Joren talks too much.”

“Joren is alive.”

That landed.

Good.

Sera looked toward the door.

I saw it.

Escape calculation.

Not attack.

“Do not.”

She moved anyway.

Of course.

Second charge.

Under the table.

Not meant to kill me.

Open floor.

Drop to shop below.

Escape route.

Smart.

I felt the trigger.

Barrier under the table.

Too late to stop detonation.

Early enough to contain direction.

The charge blew down.

Floor opened.

Sera jumped.

I caught her with Draw.

Not body.

Belt.

Cloth tore.

Enough.

She hit the edge instead of dropping.

I moved.

Loop.

Peg planted.

Load through floor.

Floor mostly absent.

Bad.

I rerouted to wall.

Shoulder screamed.

Tracy would hate this.

Useful thought.

I pulled.

Sera came back up.

She cut me with something small.

Hidden blade.

Across the forearm.

Fine.

I hit her hand.

Blade gone.

Then throat.

Not kill.

Pressure.

Air stopped.

She fought.

Strong.

Technical.

Panicked.

Human.

I held until she stopped fighting.

Not unconscious.

Almost.

Then released enough.

She coughed.

I put a restraint around her wrists.

Simple iron.

Oss would have complained.

Good enough.

That was it.

Fight over.

I had won.

I stood there breathing.

Sera on her knees.

Shoulder out.

One elbow damaged.

Wrists bound.

No weapon.

No active charge within reach.

Below us people were leaving the shop.

Good.

No civilians left.

I could call Authority.

I had a signal.

In my coat.

Left pocket.

I knew exactly where.

Sera knew too.

She looked at me.

“Going to arrest me?”

I said nothing.

She smiled.

Not because she thought I would let her go.

Because she understood.

“You came alone.”

“Yes.”

“Not for arrest.”

No.

Not really.

“You set the lower charges,” I said.

“Yes.”

“You knew the market foundations connected.”

Her smile disappeared.

“We had maps.”

“Yes.”

“Bad maps.”

“Good enough.”

She looked away.

There.

Not denial.

“What did you expect?”

“Localized failure.”

“Under houses.”

“Not that far.”

“Market.”

“I know.”

“Children.”

Her jaw tightened.

“I know.”

“Nessa.”

She looked at me.

Did not know the name.

Good.

“My wife.”

Silence.

“My son.”

Silence.

“My daughter.”

She looked away again.

I wanted Hale’s answer.

Would you still do it?

I asked.

Sera took longer than Hale.

Shorter than Joren.

“Yes.”

There.

Category.

“Why?”

“Because they buried ours first.”

Simple.

Ugly.

“They did not.”

“The town protected him.”

“No.”

“Enough did.”

“Not my children.”

“No.”

“Nessa.”

“No.”

“Me.”

She looked at the peg.

“No.”

“You still would.”

“Yes.”

No speech.

No apology.

No philosophy.

Just yes.

I reached into my coat.

Her eyes went to the pocket.

Signal.

For one second, I almost pulled it.

That mattered.

Not because I was uncertain who she was.

Because I had an alternative.

Authority.

Custody.

Trial.

Joren.

Process.

Punishment.

Real.

Available.

I knew it.

Sera knew it.

I left the signal in the pocket.

Her face changed.

Not fear yet.

Understanding.

“Greg.”

First time she used my name.

I stepped closer.

She pulled against the restraints.

No chance.

“Wait.”

There.

Not mercy.

Time.

Human.

I stopped.

Good.

“Why?”

She breathed hard.

“Because you won.”

That was not enough.

Neither was anything.

I thought about Nessa’s hand.

My son’s boot.

My daughter without the blue cup.

The sweet bread in the bag.

The market.

The road.

Hale in floodwater.

Joren behind glass.

Twenty minutes.

The aqueduct.

All of it.

Nothing became simple.

I wanted simple anyway.

“That is not why I came.”

Sera looked at me.

Then finally became afraid.

Good.

Bad.

Both.

I killed her.

Cleanly.

Not dramatically.

There is no useful reason to make it prettier than it was.

She was restrained.

I could have taken her alive.

I did not.

That is the fact.

Afterward I stood in the room.

Waited.

For what?

Relief.

Nessa.

Satisfaction.

Disgust.

Anything large enough to justify the years ahead.

What I felt first was technical.

The loop had worked.

That was worse.

I replayed the fight.

The first charge.

The tilted floor.

The wall transfer.

The belt Draw.

The missing floor.

Every place the closed architecture had made a stronger opponent manageable.

Sera was not Hale.

Not close.

But the method scaled in the correct direction.

I could see it.

Five years became plausible.

Maybe less.

That was what the kill gave me.

Not peace.

Proof.

I hated that.

Then I searched the room.

Practical.

Of course.

Documents.

Cord.

Maps.

Two names.

One route.

Money.

A letter from somebody who signed only K.

Sera had a life after the town.

Rent.

Work.

Food.

Accounts.

A half-finished coat repair.

Ordinary things.

I took what mattered.

Left the rest.

Then called Authority.

Late.

Still called.

The investigator arrived first.

Saw the body.

Saw the restraints.

Looked at me.

Professional face.

Good.

“She was bound.”

“Yes.”

“Before death.”

“Yes.”

“You killed her after restraint.”

“Yes.”

He stared.

I did not help.

“Why?”

“Revenge.”

There.

Clean word.

Dirty act.

He wrote it down.

That surprised me.

Maybe I expected euphemism.

He gave me none.

Good man.

There were consequences.

Not murder charges exactly.

The legal structure was complicated by Sera’s wanted status, active attack, outstanding charges, and the fact that I had entered to apprehend someone already under warrant.

But restraint changed things.

Everyone knew.

Authority review.

Contract suspension.

Weapons restriction for a period.

Mandatory testimony.

The investigator did not let the fight erase the execution.

I respected him for that.

Hale sent no message.

Good.

Tracy found out in two days.

Professional gossip.

Again.

She came to the treatment room.

Did not sit.

“Was she restrained?”

“Yes.”

“You could have brought her in.”

“Yes.”

“You killed her anyway.”

“Yes.”

She looked at me.

Not shocked.

Disappointed.

Different.

Worse.

“Did you need to?”

“No.”

There.

No defense.

“Did the loop work?”

“Yes.”

That hurt her too.

I saw it.

She looked at the graph.

Outward ceiling.

Slow decline.

Then at me.

“Was that the test?”

“Yes.”

“And now?”

I knew the answer.

“Now I know.”

“What?”

“That Hale is reachable.”

Tracy closed her eyes.

When she opened them, she looked tired.

Not angry.

Tired.

“Did killing her help?”

“Yes.”

She stared.

I continued before she could misunderstand.

“Not the way you mean.”

“How do I mean?”

“Emotionally.”

“Did it?”

“No.”

“Then how?”

“It removed uncertainty.”

Silence.

“About your method.”

“Yes.”

“That is a horrible reason to kill someone.”

“She was not a test subject.”

“No?”

Fair.

I looked away.

“She was responsible.”

“Yes.”

“She would do it again.”

“Yes.”

“You still tested yourself.”

“Yes.”

There.

Both true.

That was becoming the shape of revenge.

Real grievance.

Real guilt.

Real technical purpose.

Real selfishness.

Nothing canceled anything.

Tracy sat.

Finally.

“What happens now?”

“Training.”

“How much?”

“More.”

“Outward preservation?”

“Yes.”

She looked at me.

I knew.

Bad answer.

“Until?”

I thought.

“Until it becomes incompatible.”

There.

Point coming.

Not here yet.

Tracy nodded once.

Then wrote the date on the graph.

SERA DAIN.

Not a medical event.

A marker.

“What is that?”

“The day theory became behavior.”

I looked at her.

She capped the pen.

“Do not make me explain it.”

I did not.

Ithar was quiet for a long time.

Then:

“You killed a restrained woman.”

“Yes.”

“You could have surrendered her.”

“Yes.”

“You chose not to.”

“Yes.”

No excuse.

Good.

“Do you regret it?”

I looked at him.

“Some parts.”

“That is not an answer.”

“It is the only honest one.”

He waited.

I continued.

“I regret that I made her death carry more than punishment.”

“What did it carry?”

“Research.”

The word sat badly.

Correctly.

Ithar’s eyes narrowed.

“And did that stop you?”

“No.”

Of course not.

It made the next compromise easier.
