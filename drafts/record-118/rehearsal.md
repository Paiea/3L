## RECORD 118

## THE AFTER

Tracy arrived twenty-three minutes later.

I know because she told me.

I had no idea.

Time had become blood loss and stone.

She came down the maintenance path carrying the field bag.

Saw me first.

Then Hale.

Stopped.

Only once.

Then moved.

Healer.

Good.

She reached Hale before me.

That bothered me for approximately half a second.

Then made sense.

He was farther from obviously alive.

She knelt.

Hand at throat.

Chest.

Eyes.

Nothing.

She knew.

Still checked.

Good healer.

Then she came to me.

“You’re bleeding.”

“Yes.”

“That was not admiration.”

“I know.”

“Where?”

“Several.”

“Useful.”

Current Tracy.

Good.

She cut my shirt.

Found the side wound.

Arm.

Hip.

Shoulder.

Head.

Nothing immediately terminal.

Apparently.

She looked at the peg.

Still attached.

“Good.”

“Stop saying that.”

She froze.

Then laughed.

One sound.

Bad timing.

Perfect timing.

I did too.

Ribs objected.

We stopped.

She worked.

Pressure.

Seal.

Bandage.

Water.

Questions.

Name.

Date.

Place.

How many fingers.

“Mine or Hale’s?”

She stared.

“Greg.”

“Nine.”

“Hale?”

“Nine.”

“Good.”

“Stop.”

She almost smiled again.

Then not.

Work.

Eventually she sat back on her heels.

“You’re not dying.”

“Disappointing.”

“No.”

“Sorry.”

“Do not joke about that yet.”

Yet.

Good.

I looked at Hale.

Still.

Really still.

No breathing.

No tiny correction.

No useful A-class body waiting to turn back on.

Dead.

I had imagined that sight for nine years.

It looked smaller than expected.

People do.

The things around them make them large.

Rank.

Stories.

Fear.

Work.

Songs.

Then body.

Hale Rusk was a dead man in field clothes.

Missing finger.

Old scar.

New blood.

That was all.

Tracy followed my eyes.

“You killed him.”

“Yes.”

Not accusation.

Fact.

“How do you feel?”

I hated the question immediately.

“Bleeding.”

“Greg.”

“I don’t know.”

Better.

She nodded.

Good.

Not now.

She packed one instrument.

Then another.

I kept looking at Hale.

I waited for Nessa.

Not literally.

Something.

Memory.

Relief.

Collapse.

Children.

Town.

A great moral realization.

Nothing arrived on schedule.

Of course.

What I felt first was practical.

Hale could not do it again.

That mattered.

More than I expected.

Not the town.

Could not undo.

But future choice.

Gone.

The man who said yes every time I asked would never choose another town.

That was real.

Then satisfaction.

Yes.

I was satisfied.

People dislike that answer.

Too clean.

Too ugly.

Still true.

I had spent nearly a decade becoming capable of one thing.

Then done it.

Completion has a shape.

The shape felt good.

Not happy.

Not healed.

Finished.

Different.

I looked at Tracy.

“It worked.”

She frowned.

“What?”

“Revenge.”

Long silence.

She did not moralize.

Good.

“How?”

“He’s dead.”

“Yes.”

“I wanted him dead.”

“Yes.”

“Now he is.”

“Yes.”

There.

Simple.

Then the rest.

“Nessa is still dead.”

Tracy looked down.

“The children.”

“Yes.”

“Town.”

“Yes.”

“Leg.”

She glanced at it.

“Yes.”

“My old support ceiling.”

No answer.

“Still gone.”

“Yes.”

“So it worked.”

I looked at Hale.

“It just did not do anything else.”

Tracy sat with that.

Then:

“Were you expecting it to?”

“Yes.”

Honest.

“What?”

I thought.

Maybe grief smaller.

Maybe guilt.

Maybe the job.

Maybe waking up without the pressure of unfinished revenge.

Maybe nothing specific.

“That part was stupid.”

“Yes.”

I looked at her.

“Could have softened it.”

“No.”

Good.

The road team arrived because Tracy had signaled.

Not Authority yet.

Medical.

Two people.

One wagon.

They saw Hale.

Saw me.

Saw her.

Nobody asked immediately.

Professional saints.

They loaded Hale separately.

Covered body.

That bothered me.

Then did not.

I rode sitting because lying down hurt more.

Tracy rode beside me.

At one point she asked:

“Finger.”

“What?”

“You still have his finger.”

Of course.

I closed my eyes.

“Yes.”

“What are you going to do with it?”

“I don’t know.”

“Keep it?”

The idea felt wrong immediately.

Interesting.

For nine years it had mattered.

Proof.

Fuel.

Promise.

Now?

Object.

Preserved flesh from a man I had finished killing.

Weird.

“I think it goes with him.”

Tracy nodded.

No symbolism speech.

Good.

Authority met us at the medical station.

Of course.

Two investigators.

One legal officer.

One very tired administrator who looked like I had personally ruined his week.

Fair.

They separated us.

Statement.

Again.

I told them everything.

Location.

Terms.

No third parties.

Mutual lethal intent.

Yield condition.

Fight.

Hale’s refusal.

Final question.

Final answer.

Kill.

The investigator stopped.

“You asked whether he yielded.”

“Yes.”

“He refused.”

“Yes.”

“And then?”

“I killed him.”

No euphemism.

Good.

The legal officer asked whether Hale had coerced me.

I laughed.

No.

Whether I had coerced Hale.

No.

Whether either had attempted withdrawal.

No.

Whether the fight endangered civilians.

No.

Whether Tracy participated.

“No.”

Good.

She had chosen road.

Stayed road.

Important.

Then they produced Hale’s statement.

Of course he had one.

Dated three days before the basin.

Personal sealed declaration.

Not permission.

Not pardon.

Acknowledgment.

He stated he intended to meet Greg voluntarily over unresolved personal blood debt arising from the town operation and subsequent mutilation.

Very Hale language.

Subsequent mutilation.

Clean.

He stated he expected lethal force.

Would not consider Greg’s participation coercion.

Did not request Authority intervention.

Requested that any resulting inquiry distinguish the private encounter from Greg’s professional field status.

I stared.

“He wrote that.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

The administrator looked at me.

“Because apparently both of you enjoy making my job difficult in unusually organized ways.”

Fair.

The statement did not make the fight legal.

Important.

Named Rankers do not get private murder exemptions because paperwork exists.

There was a review.

Again.

Suspension.

Again.

Longer.

Probation.

Restrictions.

Civil claims from Hale’s estate remained possible.

The Authority also reviewed whether Hale’s own declaration and documented acceptance of the duel reduced Greg’s culpability.

It did.

Not erased.

Reduced.

The final administrative finding used a phrase I hated:

MUTUAL LETHAL COMBAT ARISING FROM DOCUMENTED PERSONAL GRIEVANCE.

Accurate.

Awful.

I lost contracts.

Money.

Access.

Some people stopped calling.

Others called more.

Humans.

One newspaper made me a hero.

Another murderer.

Both sold copies.

I ignored both.

Mostly.

I went.

That surprised everyone.

Including me.

Not front.

Back.

No announcement.

Tracy came because she did not trust my judgment around funerals.

Reasonable.

Hale had family.

Of course.

A sister.

Two nephews.

An older woman I think was an aunt.

Friends.

Authority people.

Rankers.

People from places he had saved.

That hurt.

Good.

Truth should.

Nobody attacked me.

I saw it.

He did not approach.

Good.

Not my grief.

Important.

Briefly.

“You’re Greg.”

“Yes.”

She knew.

Of course.

“You killed him.”

“Yes.”

No reason to lie at funeral.

“He said you might.”

Not dramatic.

Small.

Medical glass.

His finger.

Nine years old and not really looking like a finger anymore.

Good.

“What is that?”

“Hale’s.”

Then she understood.

“Oh.”

“He gave it to me.”

“I know.”

Of course she knew too.

Hale told people strange things.

“I’m done carrying it.”

That was all.

She took the case.

Not gently.

Not roughly.

Looked at it.

Then at me.

“No.”

Good answer.

“Do you forgive him?”

“No.”

Fair.

Then:

“Neither do I.”

That surprised me.

I said nothing.

She looked toward the grave.

“He did something unforgivable.”

There.

Family.

Not denial.

“Yes.”

“Both.”

“Yes.”

Both.

Murderer.

Rescuer.

Brother.

Target.

Useful.

Dead.

Not my business anymore.

Bad food near the cemetery.

No plan.

No graph.

No Hale.

Not relief.

Schedule.

An empty place where a task had been.

“Today?”

“Yes.”

“Eat.”

“After?”

No answer.

Because she was right.

Of course.

The sister.

The basin.

Nessa.

There.

Mine now.
