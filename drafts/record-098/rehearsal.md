## RECORD 098

## THE LOOP

Day nine was a mistake.

Not because the idea failed.

Because it worked.

That distinction mattered later.

At the time it only made me happy.

I had been doing the boring things.

Mostly.

Socket fit.

Shoulder load.

Channel endurance.

Three items.

Tracy had been correct.

Annoyingly.

The socket stopped rubbing raw at the back edge.

My left shoulder hurt less during turns.

The cheap Barrier drill lasted longer before precision degraded.

Progress.

Slow.

Real.

Insufficient.

Hale remained alive.

That sentence contaminated everything.

Every exercise became measured against him.

Can this help me kill Hale?

Can this?

What about this?

Tracy had said rehabilitation first.

She was right.

I knew she was right.

Then on day nine I tested something else.

Not combat.

Not exactly.

A loop.

First-Life support moved outward.

That was the point.

Me to you.

You to another.

Load away from failure.

Pressure redistributed through bodies, equipment, terrain, structures, timing.

Relational.

Broad.

Beautiful when it worked.

Useless if nobody else was there.

That last sentence was not fair.

I believed it anyway.

Second-Life Greg had one immediate problem.

Hale.

One body.

Mine.

One target.

His.

No team.

No network.

No Tracy who trusted me.

No Arcrutus.

No party.

No years.

So I asked a bad question.

What if the network closed?

Me to object.

Object to ground.

Ground back to me.

Not enough to create power.

That would have been easy nonsense.

The point was failure.

Where does the failure go?

If my damaged side cannot carry impact, can the peg take part of the transition?

If the peg cannot, can the floor?

If the floor cannot, can a Barrier define the edge long enough to redirect the load back through a stronger line?

Not more strength.

Different route.

The first attempt did nothing.

Good.

The second hurt.

Also useful.

The third made the peg skid.

Bad.

The fourth nearly put me on the floor.

Worse.

Then I changed the boundary.

Not around the leg.

Around the contact.

Small.

Thin.

The cheap spell.

Define what crosses.

Control what does not.

The peg hit the floor.

Load entered.

Some moved through the socket.

Some through the hip.

Too much toward the shoulder.

I redirected.

Not outward.

Back.

Closed.

For less than a second, the body stopped being a body and became a circuit.

Peg.

Floor.

Barrier.

Hip.

Torso.

Shoulder.

Hand.

Back down.

Tiny.

Ugly.

Wrong.

Stable.

I stood there.

Breathing.

Nothing hurt more than expected.

That was the problem.

I did it again.

Better.

Again.

Worse.

Adjusted.

Again.

The fifth usable loop held long enough that I could put more weight through the right side than I had since Hale took the leg.

I laughed.

Alone.

That should have worried me.

It did not.

I spent two hours on it.

Tracy had told me channel endurance.

I told myself this was channel endurance.

Technically true.

Technically is where many bad decisions live.

The loop did not make me stronger.

It made me harder to move.

That was enough.

I started with standing.

Then turning.

Then a controlled shove against a wall.

Then a hanging weight.

Then a larger weight.

The loop let me catch pressure the body normally rejected.

Not safely.

Differently.

There is an important distinction between those words.

I learned it later.

The body could accept the route.

The channels hated it.

First-Life channels had spent decades learning open traffic.

Broad pathways.

Multiple targets.

Dynamic transfer.

This loop asked the same architecture to close.

Narrow.

Repeat.

Return.

No recipient.

No release.

Just me.

The sensation was efficient.

That should have scared me too.

It felt cleaner than relational support.

Not better.

Cleaner.

Less negotiation.

Less uncertainty.

No one else’s timing.

No trust.

No consent.

No other body deciding something stupid.

Just route.

Load.

Return.

I understood immediately why it was dangerous to me.

Then did it again.

On day eleven Tracy noticed.

Not the magic.

The shoulder.

She had me walk.

Turn.

Stop.

Again.

Then she said, “What did you change?”

“Nothing.”

She looked at me.

Bad answer.

“Training.”

“What training?”

“Barrier.”

“What kind?”

“Load.”

Her face closed.

Not old Tracy.

Current Tracy.

Angry.

“Show me.”

“No.”

That was worse.

She stared.

“Why not?”

“Because you’ll tell me to stop.”

“Greg.”

“Exactly.”

She put the clipboard down.

Very carefully.

That was new.

“What did you do?”

I showed her.

Not full load.

Small.

Peg.

Floor.

Boundary.

Return.

The loop closed.

Her eyes sharpened.

Not impressed.

Medical.

She stepped closer.

“Again.”

I did.

“Again.”

I did.

“Stop.”

I stopped.

She touched the shoulder.

Then the chest.

Then the side of my neck.

“Do you feel that?”

“No.”

“That is not good.”

“What?”

She pressed again.

“Flow lag.”

I knew.

Did not want to.

“The return path is arriving late.”

“Barely.”

“Yes.”

“Manageable.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because you are teaching the channel to expect return.”

I looked at her.

She was thinking now.

Fast.

Not enough history to know the whole architecture.

Enough to see the local problem.

“You’re closing something that usually vents.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Control.”

“For what?”

“Hale.”

There.

No maintenance language.

No lie.

Her mouth tightened.

“What happens when you need to send load outward?”

“I can still do that.”

“Now.”

“Yes.”

“And after a year?”

I said nothing.

She hated that.

“After five?”

Still nothing.

“Do you know?”

“Yes.”

Her face changed.

That was the moment.

Not anger.

Something colder.

“You know.”

“Yes.”

“You know this can make other pathways harder later.”

“Yes.”

“And you did it anyway.”

“Yes.”

She stepped back.

I almost reached for her.

Old instinct.

Wrong relationship.

Stopped.

Good.

“Why are you here?” she asked.

“What?”

“With me.”

“For consultation.”

“No.”

She pointed at the loop.

“You already decided.”

“I need help managing the cost.”

There.

Wrong sentence.

Her face went flat.

“You want me to make your bad decision cheaper.”

“Safer.”

“No.”

“Tracy.”

“No.”

“You haven’t heard the whole—”

“I do not need the whole plan.”

“You always say that.”

She stopped.

“What?”

Fuck.

Old Tracy again.

“You always—”

I cut myself off.

Too late.

Current Tracy heard it.

Her expression changed.

Not confusion.

Recognition of a pattern.

“Her.”

I said nothing.

“The other me.”

“Yes.”

“You are doing it again.”

“I know.”

“No, you don’t.”

Fair.

She sat down.

Not because the conversation softened.

Because she was angry enough to become precise.

“Did she help you do this?”

“No.”

“Would she have?”

I thought about First-Life Tracy.

Healthy Greg.

Outward Greg.

The woman who built redundancy because failure propagated.

The woman who wanted systems to survive absence.

“No.”

“Then why are you asking me?”

Because you are all I have left of her.

Terrible answer.

True.

I did not say it.

“Because you see costs early.”

“Yes.”

“And this has costs.”

“Yes.”

“So help me price them.”

She stared.

For a long time.

Then:

“No.”

Simple.

I felt anger.

Real.

Unfair.

“What am I paying you for?”

There.

The moment I said it, I knew.

Tracy did too.

Her expression did not change.

That was worse.

“You are paying for my judgment.”

“Yes.”

“I just gave it to you.”

Silence.

Current Tracy.

Not Number Two.

Not partner.

Not mine.

Consultant.

Paid to disagree.

I had forgotten the contract I had not made.

I looked away.

“Right.”

She stood.

Session over.

Not fifty-three minutes.

Thirty-one.

I did not mention the time.

Growth.

At the door she said:

“If you keep doing that, I will still treat you.”

I turned.

“But I will not help optimize it.”

“Why?”

“Because I think it is bad medicine.”

“It may be good survival.”

“Maybe.”

That mattered.

She did not pretend certainty.

“Then why refuse?”

“Because you are not asking whether it works.”

I waited.

“You already know it works.”

Yes.

“You are asking me to help you become the person who can tolerate using it.”

That hurt.

Accurate.

She opened the door.

“Not yet.”

I left.

The loop came with me.

Of course.

I did not stop.

That was the beginning of the trade.

Not the point of no return.

Not yet.

There were still years where I could have changed direction.

Still outward capacity I could have rebuilt.

Still choices.

That matters.

People like to look backward and find the single hinge.

Usually there are many.

Day nine was only the first one I recognized later.

Ithar watched me.

“You knew it would reduce the path back.”

“Yes.”

“And continued.”

“Yes.”

“Because of Hale.”

“At first.”

His eyes narrowed.

“At first?”

I looked at the peg.

Then my hands.

“Revenge makes a good reason for the first compromise.”

The cave stayed quiet.

“The next ones get easier.”
