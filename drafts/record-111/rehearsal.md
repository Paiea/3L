## RECORD 111

## THE TARGET

Named status changed less than people thought.

More paperwork.

More requests.

Better pay sometimes.

Worse assumptions.

People heard Peg-Leg Greg and imagined a person I had not met.

Some expected a joke.

Some expected a monster.

One expected a pirate.

Wrong world.

Mostly it meant I got invited into rooms sooner.

That mattered.

Authority briefings.

Named Ranker rosters.

Cross-district response meetings.

Access to sealed field summaries.

Not everything.

Enough.

For the first time since the town, Hale stopped being a distant shape I had to reconstruct through rumor.

He became administratively visible.

Assignments.

Availability.

Restrictions.

Regional movement.

Medical downtime.

Field reviews.

All legitimate.

All boring.

Excellent.

Revenge likes boring information.

The first Named coordination meeting I attended had twelve people.

Three combat.

Two support.

One engineering.

One healer.

Two people I did not understand.

A logistics specialist.

Hale.

Me.

He sat across the table.

Of course.

Missing finger on the paper.

My peg under the chair.

No symbolism required.

Everyone else pretended not to notice.

Professional.

The chair introduced me formally.

“Named support designation Peg-Leg Greg.”

Hale looked at me.

Barely.

I hated him for behaving.

Then the chair introduced him.

As if I did not know.

Hale Rusk.

Named A-class.

Regional response.

Combat reinforcement.

Emergency field authority under restriction.

Under restriction.

Still.

Years later.

Good.

Not erased.

Managed.

That mattered.

The meeting was about seasonal failure coverage.

Bridges.

Floods.

Monster migration.

Two mining regions.

Nothing to do with revenge.

I listened.

Actually listened.

Growth.

Hale spoke twice.

Short.

Useful.

He knew which district could absorb delayed response.

Knew which river crews were understaffed.

Knew one mountain route would close early if winter came hard.

Of course.

Useful man.

Again.

After the meeting he remained seated while everyone else left.

I did too.

Eventually it was only us.

Bad room.

Good lighting.

Very ordinary.

Hale looked at my designation card.

“Official.”

“Yes.”

“Congratulations.”

“Fuck you.”

He nodded.

Same.

Then:

“You are still coming.”

Not a question.

“Yes.”

“When?”

I looked at him.

“You want the date?”

“No.”

“Then why ask?”

“I did not ask when.”

Right.

He had said it.

Statement.

“You are still coming.”

“Yes.”

He looked at my hands.

Not the peg.

Hands.

That mattered.

“You are stronger.”

“Yes.”

“Not enough.”

“No.”

I hated agreeing.

Still true.

Hale leaned back.

“You took Joren.”

“Yes.”

“You killed Sera.”

“Yes.”

There.

No public euphemism.

No alleged.

No operation.

Killed.

“Do you think they were steps to me?”

I looked at him.

“Were they?”

“Answer.”

His face stayed flat.

“Yes.”

Good.

Finally explicit.

Hale was not one revenge target among many.

He was the line everything kept bending toward.

Joren had been information.

Sera had been responsibility and proof.

Others remained.

Names.

Participants.

People who had helped.

People who had done less.

People who had done more.

Some I would pursue.

Some Authority already had.

Some had died.

Some had changed.

Hale remained.

Not because he was the strongest.

Though he was.

Not because he took my leg.

Though he did.

Because when asked whether he would choose the town again, he still said yes.

That was the hinge.

I said:

“They were responsible.”

“Yes.”

“You were more.”

“Yes.”

“You still would.”

“Yes.”

There.

Nothing changed.

Good.

Bad.

Useful.

“Then yes.”

Hale looked at me.

“Steps.”

“Yes.”

He nodded.

Not offended.

That was worse.

“You are becoming capable.”

“Yes.”

“And narrower.”

I went still.

He saw it.

Of course.

“What?”

“Your support.”

“What about it?”

“At the aqueduct you opened.”

I said nothing.

“At the quarry you closed.”

He had read the report.

Of course.

“Different jobs.”

“Yes.”

He looked at me.

“But you preferred the second.”

That irritated me because I did not know if it was true.

“Why do you care?”

“I don’t.”

“Then stop.”

“I am curious.”

“You took my leg.”

“Yes.”

“You destroyed my town.”

“Yes.”

“You do not get curious about my development.”

Hale considered that.

“Fair.”

Again.

Infuriating.

I stood.

He said:

“Greg.”

I stopped.

“You understand I am not waiting in a room for you.”

“I know.”

“I will keep working.”

“I know.”

“I will keep getting better where I can.”

“I know.”

“I will not make this easy.”

“Good.”

That surprised him.

Small.

“Why good?”

“Because if I beat a worse version of you, I’ll know.”

Hale looked at me for a long time.

Then:

“Good.”

I hated that too.

I left.

Tracy was waiting downstairs.

Not for me.

Probably.

She had a file.

Saw my face.

“Meeting?”

“Yes.”

“Hale?”

“Yes.”

“Did you kill him?”

“No.”

“Good meeting.”

I almost laughed.

We walked.

She asked:

“Still him?”

I stopped.

“What?”

“Target.”

There.

Direct.

“Yes.”

“No change?”

“No.”

“Named status?”

“No.”

“Quarry?”

“No.”

“Work?”

“No.”

“Hale.”

“Yes.”

She nodded.

Not approval.

Inventory.

“Good.”

I looked at her.

“You keep saying good about bad things.”

“I like categories.”

Of course.

She opened the file.

“Then this matters.”

“What?”

She handed me the graph.

I hated the graph.

Four years now.

Outward recovery.

Closed-loop performance.

Relational bandwidth.

Return lag.

Fatigue.

Everything slow enough to ignore month by month.

Impossible to ignore across years.

Two lines crossing.

Not literally.

Close.

One rising.

One flattening.

Then falling.

Tracy pointed.

“If Hale is still the target, we need to stop pretending your goals are compatible.”

There.

The sentence I had been avoiding.

“They are compatible now.”

“Less every year.”

“Still.”

“For how long?”

I said nothing.

She did.

“Not long.”

We went back to the treatment room.

Same one?

No.

Better room now.

Current Tracy had moved.

Earned.

She put the graph on the table.

Then another page.

Two training plans.

I stared.

“What is this?”

“Choice.”

I looked at her.

She looked back.

“One preserves the path back.”

“And the other?”

“You know.”

Yes.

I did.

Hale.

The target finally had a price with a date attached.
