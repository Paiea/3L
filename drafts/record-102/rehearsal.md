## RECORD 102

## THE RETURN

Tracy found me at a prosthetics workshop.

Not dramatic.

I was arguing about leather.

The new socket harness used a thicker strap across the hip.

It rubbed.

The fitter said it would soften.

I said my skin would soften first.

He did not appreciate this.

Tracy entered halfway through.

Stopped.

Looked at me.

Then at the fitter.

“Is this a bad time?”

“Yes,” he said.

“No,” I said.

He looked betrayed.

Fair.

Tracy waited near the door.

I finished the argument.

Lost.

The strap stayed.

Temporary.

That word covered many sins.

Outside, Tracy said:

“You captured Joren Venn.”

“Yes.”

“You did not kill him.”

“No.”

“You nearly got three people killed doing it.”

“Two.”

Her face hardened.

“Do not.”

“Sorry.”

Good.

“You lied to me.”

“By omission.”

She stared.

“Still doing that.”

“Sorry.”

Better.

We stood on the sidewalk.

People moved around us.

Normal city.

Carts.

Food.

Someone yelling about fish.

No ruined town.

No sealed cistern.

Good.

Tracy said:

“The Guild infection desk heard about East Gate.”

“Why?”

“Because one of the combatants needed treatment and medical people gossip like priests.”

“Priests gossip?”

“Everyone gossips.”

Fair.

“They said you knew the old cistern.”

“Yes.”

“They said the route was sealed in an Authority annex.”

“Yes.”

“They said Hale Rusk himself asked how you knew.”

“Yes.”

She looked at me.

“I checked.”

Of course she did.

“How?”

“Friend of a friend.”

“Legal?”

She stared.

“Mostly.”

Good.

“The route is sealed.”

“Yes.”

“The appendix number exists.”

“Yes.”

“It is not public.”

“Yes.”

“The report is scheduled for archival review in eighteen years.”

“Close enough.”

Her expression changed.

Small.

Not belief.

Pressure.

“You said nineteen.”

“Yes.”

“Eighteen years and seven months.”

“Memory rounds.”

She looked annoyed.

That helped.

Perfect prophecy would have been less believable.

“Why do you know that?”

“First life.”

She looked away.

Long enough that I stopped speaking.

Then:

“I still do not believe in reincarnation.”

“Fine.”

“But I have fewer explanations.”

“Fine.”

“I dislike that.”

“I know.”

“Do not say that.”

Right.

“Sorry.”

She sighed.

Then looked at the workshop behind me.

“How is the leg?”

“Leather conspiracy.”

“Actual answer.”

“Better.”

“Shoulder?”

“Better.”

“Loop?”

There.

“Also better.”

She closed her eyes.

Not pleased.

“I am not asking because I approve.”

“I know.”

Her eyes opened.

I caught myself.

“Understood.”

Better.

“Why did you spare Joren?”

That was the real question.

Not technical.

Not reincarnation.

Joren.

“He was convicted.”

“After.”

“Yes.”

“You had him before that.”

“Yes.”

“You could have killed him.”

“Yes.”

“Wanted to?”

“Yes.”

“Then why didn’t you?”

I thought.

The easy answer was process.

Authority.

Punishment.

Those were true.

Not complete.

“He answered differently.”

“Differently from Hale?”

“Yes.”

“How?”

I told her.

Would he go after the people who killed Venn again?

Yes.

Would he block the road again after knowing civilians were trapped?

No.

Tracy listened.

Then:

“And that mattered.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because I am not trying to kill the past.”

That sentence surprised me.

Her too.

“What are you trying to kill?”

“Hale.”

“Bad answer.”

“Yes.”

She waited.

I rubbed the edge of the new strap through my trousers.

“Choices that are still alive.”

Tracy frowned.

“Explain.”

“Joren already changed one.”

“Maybe.”

“Yes.”

“Hale did not.”

“No.”

“He still says he would destroy the town again.”

“Yes.”

“So you think killing him is about preventing a future choice.”

“Partly.”

“Partly.”

“Yes.”

“And the rest?”

“My wife.”

There.

“My children.”

There.

“My leg.”

Less important.

Still there.

“My anger.”

True.

Tracy nodded slowly.

“That is a mess.”

“Yes.”

“Better.”

“What?”

“Better than pretending it is one clean reason.”

Good.

She looked toward the workshop.

Then back.

“I will consult again.”

Hope moved.

Careful.

“Okay.”

“One session.”

“Okay.”

“No field operations.”

“Okay.”

“No withholding relevant risk because you think I will say no.”

I hesitated.

She saw it.

“Then no.”

“Okay.”

“You said that too fast.”

“I mean it.”

“Do you?”

“Probably.”

“Greg.”

“Yes. I mean it.”

“Good.”

She started walking.

I followed.

Not because she invited me.

Because we were going the same way.

She noticed.

“Where are you going?”

“Food.”

“Where?”

I named a place.

She frowned.

“Bad.”

“You know it?”

“Yes.”

“Better option?”

She gave one.

I went there instead.

Current Tracy.

Not old ritual.

New information.

The place was excellent.

She ordered too much.

Good.

Some things required no reincarnation.

We ate.

For several minutes nobody discussed Hale.

That was healthy.

Then Tracy said:

“If your story is true, did we do this?”

“What?”

“Eat.”

“Yes.”

“Often?”

“Yes.”

“Because of me?”

“Mostly.”

She looked pleased.

Not sentimental.

Vindicated.

“Good.”

I laughed.

She pointed her fork.

“Do not make that weird.”

“Trying.”

“You are bad at it.”

“Yes.”

She ate.

Then asked:

“Was I rich?”

Very Tracy.

“Yes.”

“How rich?”

I gave an approximate number.

She stopped chewing.

“Fuck.”

I smiled.

She narrowed her eyes.

“Half?”

“Yes.”

“Still insane.”

“Yes.”

“Did I waste it?”

“No.”

“What did I buy?”

“Problems.”

She stared.

Then laughed.

That one was current too.

“What does that mean?”

“Transport first.”

“Why?”

“Because we were paying too much for transport.”

She put the fork down.

“Okay.”

There.

Danger.

I knew that look.

“No.”

“What?”

“You are about to have an idea.”

“I am not.”

“Face.”

She stared.

“What is face?”

“Never mind.”

“No.”

I laughed.

Wrong.

She pointed again.

“You keep doing that.”

“Sorry.”

“What is face?”

“In First Life, we accused each other of having expressions before bad ideas.”

She looked offended.

“That is stupid.”

“Yes.”

Pause.

Then:

“You have one.”

I stared.

She smiled.

There.

New joke.

Not recovered.

Rebuilt.

Small.

Important.

The second consultation happened three days later.

She reviewed the loop again.

Still hated it.

Then surprised me.

“If you are going to keep doing this, the socket cannot remain the weak point.”

I looked at her.

“You said you would not optimize it.”

“I am not optimizing the loop.”

She tapped the prosthetic notes.

“I am refusing to let bad leather decide your channel load.”

That distinction was Tracy.

Current.

Not First-Life copy.

She sent me to a different fitter.

More expensive.

Much better.

Told me to change the socket geometry before increasing load.

Told me to strengthen hip control.

Told me to stop using the left shoulder as emergency correction.

Then:

“Still three things.”

I smiled.

She saw.

“Do not.”

“Sorry.”

“Good.”

The relationship restarted there.

Not Number Two.

Not partner.

Not old friend.

Healer.

Consultant.

Occasionally food.

Increasingly arguments.

Enough.

Belief came slower.

Good.

Trust came slower.

Better.

And revenge kept moving while both happened.

Ithar watched me.

“How long before she believed the first life?”

“Months.”

“How long before she trusted you?”

“Longer.”

“How long before you trusted her?”

I thought.

“Immediately.”

His eyes narrowed.

“That sounds like the problem.”

“Yes.”

Good dragon.

I looked at the cave floor.

“She had to earn trust I had already given her.”

“No.”

I looked up.

Ithar continued.

“She had to earn trust another version of her had already earned.”

There.

Better.

“Yes.”

“And you?”

“What about me?”

“Did you earn hers?”

I thought about East Gate.

The omission.

The canceled consultations.

The new restaurant.

The better socket.

“No.”

Not yet.

That was the part I had to learn again.
