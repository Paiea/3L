## RECORD 113

## THE SECOND TRY

I sent Hale a note.

No greeting.

No explanation.

SOUTH CUT. THURSDAY. SUNSET.

That was all.

He came.

Of course.

That bothered Tracy.

Not that he came.

That I knew he would.

“You are both strange.”

“Yes.”

“You sent a murder appointment.”

“That is dramatic.”

“You intend to kill him.”

“Yes.”

“He knows.”

“Yes.”

“Appointment.”

Fair.

South Cut was an abandoned road quarry outside the western freight district.

Not Vael’s quarry.

Different one.

Smaller.

Open.

No civilians.

No buildings.

No excuse for scenery.

That mattered.

I told Tracy.

She approved of the location.

Not the purpose.

Good.

“You promised to tell me.”

“I told you.”

“Yes.”

“You’re welcome.”

She stared.

Wrong.

“Sorry.”

Better.

She did not come.

That was her choice.

Good.

I went alone.

Again.

But not like before.

First time I had gone alone because grief had made everything else too slow.

Now I went alone because the fight itself was the question.

Hale arrived before sunset.

No escort.

No Authority coat.

Field clothes.

Practical.

Missing finger.

No weapon visible.

He looked at my peg.

Then at me.

“Official now.”

“Unfortunately.”

“Congratulations.”

“Still fuck you.”

“Yes.”

Same.

We stood twenty feet apart.

Wind through cut stone.

Nothing else.

Good.

Hale asked:

“Is this it?”

“No.”

He understood.

Not final.

Test.

That should have insulted him.

It did not.

“Fine.”

I hated how easy he made some things.

“Any rules?” he asked.

“No civilians.”

He looked around.

“Agreed.”

“No Authority.”

“Agreed.”

“No one else.”

“Agreed.”

“Stop if either leaves the cut.”

He considered that.

“Why?”

“Because then the fight is over.”

“Not because you’ll stop trying later.”

“No.”

He nodded.

“Good.”

I hated good.

Then we started.

No countdown.

Hale moved first.

Of course.

Not full.

Also of course.

I expected that.

First fight he had underestimated me.

This time he would not.

Different problem.

He came low.

Missing-side hand back.

Good adaptation.

Lead shoulder narrower than before.

Weight slightly more central.

He had changed.

Of course.

Years work on both people.

His first strike was not meant to finish.

Probe.

I closed loop.

Peg to stone.

Barrier under contact.

Hip.

Torso.

Return.

The hit moved through me.

Pain.

Manageable.

I stayed.

Hale’s eyes sharpened.

Not surprise.

Data.

Good.

Second strike came from the other side.

I did not catch.

Redirected.

Barrier at elbow.

Tiny Draw at shoulder.

He felt it.

Changed route mid-strike.

A-class.

Still.

I barely cleared.

Good.

Then I attacked.

Not knife.

Not yet.

Palm.

Boundary.

Anchor his sleeve to his own hip for half a heartbeat.

He broke it.

Expected.

The break loaded his right side.

I hit the left knee.

Not damage.

Timing.

He shifted.

Peg planted.

Closed return.

I moved faster than a one-legged support specialist should have.

Stories would say that later.

Wrong.

I moved exactly as fast as years of ugly practice allowed.

Hale caught my wrist.

Same hand as first time.

Missing finger.

Grip changed.

I had spent years thinking about that grip.

Not obsessively.

Professionally obsessive.

Different.

I rotated into the missing space.

His hold failed one fraction sooner than he expected.

There.

Finger mattered.

Not blessing.

Consequence.

Mine and his.

I drove Barrier through the grip break.

Not into him.

Between us.

Thin.

He stepped.

I released.

His own correction brought his neck line open.

Same kill architecture as the first fight.

Almost.

Hale knew.

Of course.

He raised the hand early.

Too early.

Good.

I wanted that.

Knife came from the other side.

Small.

Hidden.

He saw it late.

Not late enough.

He caught my forearm.

This time no finger lost.

He slammed me into stone.

Hard.

Loop took some.

Ribs took rest.

Bad.

Hale said:

“You kept it.”

“What?”

“The sequence.”

I smiled.

“Improved it.”

He hit me again.

Fair.

We separated.

Breathing.

Not much time.

Already different from first fight.

Then he switched on fully.

No surprise now.

No pity.

No low-rank husband.

Named Ranker against Named Ranker.

A-class against C-class support with years of hostile architecture.

The gap remained.

Smaller.

Still there.

Hale became quieter.

Everything efficient.

Hands.

Feet.

No waste.

He attacked my contact.

Not body.

Peg first.

Floor second.

Barrier placement third.

He had read quarry reports too.

Of course.

He kicked loose stone under the peg.

I adapted.

Palm to wall.

He broke the wall.

I adapted.

Draw through his coat.

He shed the coat.

I adapted.

Every answer made the next problem worse.

Good fight.

Terrible.

I loved it.

That scared me.

Not killing.

Problem.

A body that answered.

Magic that required everything.

Hale saw that too.

“You missed this.”

“Shut up.”

He smiled.

Barely.

I hated him.

Then he changed tempo.

Slow.

That was worse.

He stopped feeding me load.

Small steps.

Grips.

Off-balancing.

No impact to redirect.

No big force.

He learned from Vael’s mistake without being there.

Reports.

Experience.

Me.

I had taught him by surviving publicly.

That was new.

Revenge was reciprocal development now.

Horrible.

He caught the peg behind the heel and lifted.

Simple.

I lost contact.

Closed route failed.

I used outward support.

On him.

That surprised both of us.

A tiny link.

Not help.

Stabilization.

I froze his shoulder into the position it already occupied.

Hostile support.

For half a second Hale’s own correction fought the stability I gave him.

That was enough.

I dropped.

Rolled.

Recovered contact.

Knife again.

Throat.

Always throat.

Hale turned.

Blade cut the old scar.

Same place.

Not deep.

Blood.

He stepped back.

Touched it.

Looked at the blood.

Then me.

For one beautiful second, I thought I had him.

No.

I had progress.

Different.

Hale smiled.

Not happy.

Recognition.

“You can do it now.”

I hated that.

“Not yet.”

He nodded.

Correct.

Then he broke me.

Not literally.

Mostly.

He attacked the shoulder.

Old injury.

Again.

Then hip.

Then socket.

No mercy.

Not cruelty.

Winning.

I lost the knife.

Barrier degraded.

Loop lagged.

He put me down.

I got up.

Again.

Down.

Up.

Again.

Third time slower.

Fourth time barely.

Hale stopped.

Not because I asked.

Because he saw the line.

One more and the damage became permanent.

That was also strange.

“You’re done.”

“No.”

“Yes.”

“I can still stand.”

“Barely.”

“That counts.”

“Not enough.”

I hated his standards.

He had been right before.

He was right now.

I stood anyway.

He did not attack.

That was worse.

“Fight.”

“No.”

“Hale.”

“No.”

“You said you’d wait.”

“I am.”

“Then fight.”

“You came to measure.”

I went still.

He understood.

Of course.

“You have your answer.”

I hated him.

“What is it?”

He looked at my stance.

The loop.

The shoulder.

The peg.

Then his own bleeding throat.

“Closer.”

That was not enough.

“How much?”

He almost laughed.

“No.”

“What?”

“You do not get my estimate.”

Fair.

I hated fair.

He picked up my knife.

Same as first time.

Looked at it.

Then set it on the stone between us.

Reachable.

Not immediate.

Same calculation.

No finger beside it this time.

Good.

He touched the scar.

“You nearly had me twice.”

“First time was closer.”

“Yes.”

That surprised me.

“Why?”

“I did not know what you were.”

“And now?”

“I do.”

There.

That was the new gap.

Not only rank.

Information.

Hale knew Greg now.

Peg-Leg Greg.

Named support.

Quarry.

Loop.

Hostile stabilization.

He had years of reports.

Years of seeing me work.

The surprise advantage was gone.

Good.

Real final fight should not depend on ignorance.

I looked at him.

“You’re better too.”

“Yes.”

“Because of me?”

He thought.

“Partly.”

I hated that.

We had become part of each other’s training.

No consent.

No friendship.

Still true.

Hale sat on a stone.

Not relaxed.

Enough.

I stayed standing because sitting felt like losing.

Stupid.

“You still want me dead,” he said.

“Yes.”

“Good.”

“Stop saying good.”

“No.”

I glared.

He continued:

“I worried work would change it.”

“What?”

“Your reason.”

“It did.”

His eyes narrowed.

Good.

“It got worse.”

“How?”

“At first I wanted you dead because you killed them.”

“Yes.”

“Then because you took my leg.”

He said nothing.

“Then because you kept living.”

Still.

“Working.”

Still.

“Saving people.”

His face changed slightly.

“And now?”

I looked at the blood on his throat.

“Now I know exactly what killing you costs.”

Hale waited.

“Seventeen at the flood.”

“Yes.”

“Aqueduct.”

“Yes.”

“Other work.”

“Yes.”

“If I kill you, the world loses something useful.”

“Yes.”

No defense.

No false modesty.

“And I still want to.”

“Yes.”

There.

That was worse.

Better.

Honest.

Hale looked away toward the road.

Then:

“Then you are finally choosing me.”

I stared.

Not family.

Not grief.

Not accident.

Him.

Fully informed.

Useful.

Guilty.

Changed in some ways.

Unchanged in the one way Greg cared about.

Still would choose the town.

Still target.

“Yes.”

Hale nodded.

No smile.

“When you come next, come to finish.”

I looked at him.

“That an invitation?”

“No.”

“What is it?”

“A request.”

That surprised me.

“Why?”

He touched the old scar.

“Because I am tired of measuring too.”

There.

Human.

I understood.

Did not forgive.

Different.

I picked up the knife.

Hale left first.

Walked.

No dramatic disappearance.

Just road.

I stood until he was gone.

Then sat because my leg hurt like hell.

Tracy was furious when she saw me.

Good.

That meant I had made it back.

She examined the shoulder.

Socket.

Ribs.

Then the knife cut on my hand.

“Hale?”

“Yes.”

“Alive?”

“Yes.”

“You?”

“Apparently.”

She glared.

“Useful.”

I laughed.

Bad idea.

Ribs.

Pain.

Worth it.

“What did you learn?”

I looked at her.

“That B works.”

She did not like that.

Neither did I.

“Enough?”

“No.”

“How far?”

I thought about Hale’s throat.

The missing finger.

The way he had changed tempo.

The way surprise was gone.

“Close enough that he asked me not to come back until I mean it.”

Tracy stopped.

Then:

“Do you?”

“Not yet.”

Good answer.

For once.

The second try did not end revenge.

It gave it a horizon.
