## RECORD 122

## THE LINE

The western Line failed politely.

At first.

No cities fell.

No mountains opened.

No ancient thing climbed out.

People prefer catastrophe to begin theatrically.

Usually maintenance notices come first.

Pressure variance.

Unexpected resonance.

A segment that needs recalibration twice instead of once.

Then three times.

Then somebody says:

Probably seasonal.

That word should be illegal.

The Line was not one wall.

Not exactly.

It was a chain of old reinforcement works across the western regions.

Barriers.

Pressure channels.

Anchors.

Transfer stations.

Buried structures nobody living fully understood.

Human maintenance kept the visible layers working.

Mostly.

The first public failure happened at Station Fourteen.

Not collapse.

Drift.

A reinforced transfer band began carrying load east instead of down.

Small.

Then larger.

Two maintenance crews corrected it.

The correction held six hours.

Then failed again.

Authority called specialists.

Then more specialists.

Then Named support.

Me.

Of course.

I arrived with Kelan.

Not as assistant.

Separate contract.

That mattered.

He had his own field rate now.

Complained it was too low.

Tracy would have been proud.

Current Tracy actually was.

She had her own clinic by then.

Three rooms.

Two junior healers.

One admin person she paid more than the Guild had paid her at the start.

I pointed that out once.

She said:

“Good.”

I stopped.

Growth.

Station Fourteen looked boring.

That scared me.

Old stone.

Metal bands.

Inspection platforms.

Water channel.

No dramatic damage.

The pressure readings were wrong anyway.

An Authority engineer named Seln walked me through the problem.

“Load enters here.”

“Yes.”

“Should ground through lower anchor.”

“Yes.”

“Instead it returns here.”

He pointed east.

I stared.

“What?”

Kelan asked.

I did not answer.

Closed return.

Not mine.

Older.

Much older.

Wrong scale.

The Line was doing something that looked uncomfortably like a loop.

Not exactly.

Systems repeat shapes without sharing causes.

Still.

I touched the stone.

Not useful scientifically.

Human habit.

Magic moved under it.

Slow.

Massive.

Not human.

Not active intelligence.

Structure.

Architecture.

I had felt something like that before.

Nhal.

Not her.

Her containment.

First Life.

Deep below Vey.

Load-bearing prison.

Pressure architecture that could not simply be broken because the prison was also holding something else together.

My stomach changed.

Kelan saw.

“What?”

“Nothing yet.”

Good answer.

We worked.

The local problem was solvable.

Mostly.

The east return band had begun carrying pressure because one buried anchor no longer accepted full load.

Why?

Unknown.

We could reroute temporarily.

That required distributed support across three stations.

Not one person.

Good.

Kelan took Station Fifteen.

Not with me.

There.

That mattered.

He had a six-person team.

Two reinforcement workers.

One barrier specialist.

One engineer.

One healer.

One Kelan.

He coordinated load across them.

Not like me.

His style.

Fast.

Aggressive.

He used the reinforcement workers as active partners instead of passive recipients.

Shared recoil.

Rotating load.

Short links.

Break.

Reform.

Combat rhythm applied to infrastructure.

Weird.

Effective.

Seln hated it.

Then liked the numbers.

Good.

I took Fourteen.

Another team took Sixteen.

We synchronized.

For twenty-three minutes the Line behaved.

Then twenty-four.

Then an hour.

Good.

Not fixed.

Stabilized.

We opened lower inspection.

That was where the fear began.

The human maintenance layer was intact.

Old.

Worn.

Repairable.

Below it, something had shifted.

Not broken.

Moved.

The original foundation route had changed load preference.

Seln said:

“That cannot happen.”

I looked at him.

“Apparently.”

“No. I mean it cannot.”

“Current data.”

Kelan looked at me.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

Wrong joke.

Different person.

I stopped.

Seln kept talking.

“The lower anchor is not adaptive.”

“You sure?”

“Yes.”

“How sure?”

He stopped.

Good.

Authority records went back centuries.

Not enough.

The original Line predates the current Authority structure.

Parts predate surviving maintenance language.

Humans had inherited operating procedures without design intent.

That is common.

Terrifying.

We knew how to keep it running.

Not why every piece existed.

I requested deeper records.

Denied.

Not classification.

Unavailable.

Worse.

The records did not exist locally.

Some had burned.

Some lost.

Some centralized.

Some never written in modern language.

Useful civilization.

Kelan found me after shift.

“You look terrible.”

“Thank you.”

“Not physically.”

“Worse.”

He sat.

Not invited.

Good.

“What is it?”

I thought about Nhal.

Ithar’s name.

The hidden route.

First-Life gift.

Things I had carried for decades.

Not yet.

“It resembles something.”

“What?”

“Old support architecture.”

“Yours?”

“No.”

That got his attention.

“Whose?”

“Older.”

“How old?”

“Very.”

Helpful.

He stared.

I sighed.

“There was a containment system in First Life.”

Kelan knew about First Life by then.

Mostly.

That took years.

Different record.

He still thought some parts sounded insane.

Reasonable.

“Nhal?”

I looked at him.

He remembered.

Good.

“Yes.”

“The monster.”

“Do not call her that.”

He raised hands.

“Fine.”

“Contained being.”

“That is worse.”

Fair.

“What about it?”

“The containment shared load.”

“Like the Line.”

“Maybe.”

His face changed.

There.

He understood enough to fear maybe.

“Is that bad?”

“I do not know.”

Good answer.

Line problems continued.

Station Nine.

Then Twenty-One.

Not failures.

Anomalies.

Drift.

Return.

Unexpected pressure transfer.

Different regions.

Same deep layer.

Authority formed a review group.

I joined.

Kelan joined later.

Not because I asked.

Because Station Fifteen reports made him useful.

There.

Independent value.

He had his own recommendations.

Sometimes different from mine.

Good.

One meeting he disagreed with me publicly.

Strongly.

I hated it.

Then realized he was right.

Worse.

“Your transfer plan overloads the rotating team.”

“No.”

“It does.”

“It stays under threshold.”

“For you.”

There.

Good.

He pointed at recovery lag.

His team model.

Not mine.

I changed the plan.

People noticed.

Good.

Support should survive ego.

Years passed.

The Line got worse slowly.

That was almost harder.

No single day justified panic.

Every year justified more.

Funding increased.

Maintenance crews expanded.

Authority promised resilience.

Politicians made speeches.

People built houses anyway.

Life.

Tracy’s clinic treated Line crews.

Kelan trained more support-capable combatants.

Not my program.

His.

That mattered.

I helped.

Less.

Good.

By the time I was in my late fifties, Kelan could carry a response I once would have considered mine.

Not S-class.

Not miracle.

Reliable.

Independent.

Needed.

That made something possible.

Leaving.

Ithar’s eyes narrowed.

“You were already considering the climb.”

“Yes.”

“Before the final Line failure.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

I looked at the cave around us.

Because Station Fourteen had felt like Nhal.

Because Nhal had given me Ithar’s hidden name and route.

Because the Line was failing below human maintenance.

Because I had one ancient source left that might understand foundational architecture.

All true.

Not all.

I said:

“Because I had a question humans could not answer.”

Ithar waited.

Of course.

“And?”

I looked away.

There was another question.

Not the Line.

Me.

Third run.

Not yet.

I said nothing.

Ithar noticed.

Good dragon.

He let me continue.

For now.
