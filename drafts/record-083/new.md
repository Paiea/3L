## RECORD 083

## THE SLIP

The lower fracture did not fail all at once.

That would have been easier.

Not safer.

Easier to understand.

The trace rose one count.

Then another.

Then stopped.

Pava sat up fully.

"Again."

Survey checked her own slate.

"Same."

Mael said, "Upper stations?"

The tension cord answered before the runner did.

Two pulls.

Change.

Then one.

Stable.

For now.

I hated those words.

Nhal's freed forepaw stayed flat against the floor.

Her claws had spread without digging in.

The blue paths around the empty restraint changed.

Not much.

She was using the leg.

Actually using it.

Weight through muscle.

Pressure through bone.

Something her body had not been allowed to do freely in a very long time.

And the foundation under us had picked that exact moment to become interesting.

Rude structure.

Mael looked at the lower access.

"We inspect."

The healer said, "She just finished an operation."

"We are not moving her."

"I meant all of you."

Mael looked at Pava.

Pava was already putting her boots back on.

The healer said, "That includes you."

Pava said, "Unfortunately, I am necessary."

"You are also attached to an arm."

"Still."

"For now."

Pava stopped lacing.

Good healer.

Mael reassigned.

That was what he did when reality argued.

Survey lead, structural specialist, Mael, me.

Arcrutus stayed with Nhal and the healer.

Pava stayed too.

She hated it.

That made it correct.

Before we went, I knelt beside the rods.

Nhal watched me.

"We are going below."

Left.

Yes.

"Is the change from the fracture?"

Both.

Not like that.

Of course.

I tried again.

"Is something below moving that should not move?"

Left.

"Has it happened before?"

Left.

That got everyone's attention.

Mael said, "Ask how often."

I looked at him.

"With what vocabulary?"

He considered the rods.

"Fair."

Nhal's mouth opened.

No sound came at first.

Her throat worked.

The healer immediately moved closer.

Not touching.

Ready.

Nhal tried again.

"Slow."

One word.

Rough.

Clear enough.

Pava stopped being annoyed about staying behind.

"Slow now?"

I asked.

Nhal pressed left.

"Always slow?"

Right.

No.

"Getting faster?"

Left.

There.

Mael did not waste time pretending the answer was reassuring.

"Move."

We went below.

The lower access felt different immediately.

Not dramatic.

The wall anchors hummed under my fingertips.

Very faint.

A vibration I would not have noticed three days earlier.

Now I hated it personally.

The structural specialist noticed too.

He crouched at the first brace.

"New."

Survey said, "Magnitude?"

"Small."

"Direction?"

He put two fingers on the stone and waited.

"Down and east."

Mael said, "Toward the fracture?"

"Parallel to it."

Worse.

A crack widening is one problem.

A crack sliding is another.

We descended.

The wound looked almost the same.

Almost is a dangerous word around structures.

The gap between the two black foundation faces was still narrow enough to step across in places if you were stupid.

Arcrutus had done it once.

Mael had threatened him.

Good memory.

The dead support spur remained dark.

The surviving blue relationships still crossed the break.

Nhal's tail relation remained the strongest by far.

But the pale line we had marked on the east face had moved.

Not much.

Half a finger width.

Survey knelt.

Measured.

Measured again.

Structural specialist checked her reference point.

Then a second.

He sat back on his heels.

"Three-eighths inch."

Mael said, "Since yesterday?"

"Since seven hours ago."

Nobody said anything.

Seven hours was worse.

Pava's voice came down the speaking tube from above.

"Say it."

Survey leaned toward the tube.

"East face translated three-eighths inch along the fracture plane."

Silence.

Then Pava.

"Load change?"

"Measuring."

I lowered my aura slightly.

Mael looked at me.

"Useful?"

"Maybe."

"Then five counts."

He had started giving my body numbers.

Annoying.

Probably wise.

I opened just enough to feel the held relationships across the break.

Nhal first.

Easy now.

Not because it was small.

Because it was distinct.

Huge stable relation.

Old.

Everything else seemed to have been built around it.

The next blue path carried alignment.

Another shared compression.

Another allowed tiny motion in one direction and rejected it in the other.

That one was straining.

Not more pressure exactly.

Wrong angle.

The two sides were trying to slide past one another while the relationship still insisted they remain aligned.

I raised my aura.

"She is not only holding them together."

Survey said, "We know."

"No. I mean the fracture faces are translating. Her relation is correcting geometry now, not just separation."

Structural specialist looked at the marks.

"That means leverage."

Mael said, "On the tail."

"Yes."

I did not like how quickly we all understood.

Nhal's natural anchor was seated into one side of the break.

Her magical relation reached across both.

If the faces pulled apart, she carried tension.

If they slid, the anchor became a lever.

A very strong lever.

Attached to her body.

Pava's voice came down.

"Can she compensate?"

I looked at the relationship again without opening fully.

"She already is."

"How much?"

"More than yesterday."

"Greg."

"I don't have a number."

Good sentence.

Mael pointed at the dead spur.

"Could translation be the reason that path failed?"

Structural specialist moved to it.

The spur entered the east face at a shallow angle.

He brushed black dust from the junction.

A fresh bright edge showed under the old mineral surface.

"Yes."

Survey said, "Certain?"

"No."

He pointed.

"But this material fractured in shear, not overload."

Mael crouched beside him.

"Meaning?"

"It was strong enough for the pressure. Wrong for the movement."

There.

A whole category of failure our map barely contained.

We had been mapping what carried how much.

The structure had started changing where the carrying happened.

I hated dynamic systems.

Unfair statement.

I had built my career around them.

Maybe I hated being inside one.

Survey unrolled the lower map.

"If the east face continues translating, these four paths change angle first."

Structural pointed.

"This one goes next."

"How long?"

He made the face people make when asked to predict weather from one cloud.

"At current rate? A day. Maybe two."

Mael said, "And if rate changes?"

"Then current rate stops being useful."

Good specialist.

Pava's voice came again.

"It is changing."

Everyone looked at the tube.

"Tail just rose two counts."

I touched the wall.

Vibration stronger.

Survey checked the mark.

"Movement."

We watched.

Nothing visible.

Then black dust fell from the east face.

A thin line.

The structural specialist swore.

Survey measured.

Another sixteenth.

Not much.

Enough.

Mael stood.

"We no longer have a day."

Nobody argued.

He looked at the map.

"What can stop translation?"

Structural specialist said, "Permanent? Equipment we do not have."

"Temporary."

"Bracing across the shear plane. Multiple points. Not one."

"How many?"

"At least three to keep it from rotating around a single brace."

Survey added, "And the brace has to permit separation correction while resisting lateral movement."

Mael looked at me.

I said, "Don't."

"Can you build that relation?"

"Yes."

"How long can you hold it?"

"Alone?"

"Yes."

"Not usefully."

"With us?"

That was different.

I looked at the fracture.

Three points.

Separate force sources.

One shared geometric condition.

Not enough to hold the foundation permanently.

Enough to stop the sliding while we transferred load elsewhere.

Maybe.

I said, "If Arcrutus and you provide the hard resistance, I can keep the three braces agreeing about what straight means."

Survey frowned.

"And the third source?"

Mael looked upward.

We all knew.

Pava.

The speaking tube stayed quiet.

Then she said, "I heard that."

Of course she did.

The healer said something in the background I could not make out.

Pava said, louder, "She says no."

The healer shouted down the tube.

"Correct."

I almost smiled.

Mael did not.

"Pava, current arm status?"

"Functional."

Healer shouted something.

Pava sighed.

"Functional with return strain."

Mael said, "Can you provide static shaping without pulse return?"

Pause.

Long enough that I knew Pava was actually thinking.

Good.

"Yes. Different technique. Lower peak."

"Enough?"

"Depends on your other two."

Mael looked at me.

"Greg?"

I hated being the arithmetic between monsters.

I pictured Arcrutus as one point.

Mael second.

Pava third.

Three S-class force sources.

Not equal.

Did not need equal.

My job would be preserving the relationship among them as the fracture tried to shear.

If Pava used static shaping, her forearm would still carry channel tension but not the violent return from a pulse.

Potentially manageable.

"Yes," I said.

The healer shouted, "I heard that too."

"Sorry."

"You will be."

Mael ignored us.

"We brace now. We do not attempt another restraint release. Objective is stop translation and protect Nhal's tail geometry. Once stable, reassess extraction."

Good.

Narrow.

Not rescue because panic had arrived.

Stabilize the problem that changed.

We marked three temporary brace stations around the wound.

Arcrutus took west high.

Mael east low.

Pava would shape across center from the chamber because her pressure work did not require physical contact with the fracture.

I stood where I could feel all three.

Terrible place.

Necessary place.

Preparation took ninety minutes.

Too long.

Also faster than safe.

We compromised by being terrified and competent.

Arcrutus came down carrying two expedition plates under one arm.

He looked at the shifted fracture.

"Oh."

That was the first time I heard him use that word with respect.

Mael said, "Position."

Arcrutus did not joke.

He set the plates.

Structural specialist corrected one angle.

Arcrutus moved it without complaint.

The second correction was smaller.

Still no complaint.

Fear had improved him.

Pava's static pressure descended through the chamber structure as a low controlled field.

I felt it arrive before the instruments did.

Survey called confirmation.

The healer called Pava's status from above.

"Stable."

Mael looked at me.

"Build relation."

I lowered my aura.

Three force sources.

Three brace points.

One fracture trying to become two places.

I defined boundaries around each station.

Not walls.

Conditions.

This point may separate within tolerance.

This point may not translate east.

This point may rotate only if the other two rotate with it.

It was ugly magic.

No spell name.

No elegant form.

Just three agreements and me standing in the middle convincing them they belonged to the same problem.

Arcrutus loaded west.

Mael loaded east.

Pava shaped center.

The fracture pushed back.

My channels lit up.

"Holding," I said.

Survey checked.

"Translation slowed."

Structural specialist watched the mark.

"More west."

Arcrutus increased.

I felt it immediately.

Too much compared with Mael.

"Half back."

Arcrutus obeyed.

Mael increased east.

Pava adjusted center.

The three points came into agreement.

Not equal force.

Equal geometry.

That distinction mattered.

The vibration in the wall faded.

Survey watched the mark.

Thirty seconds.

One minute.

Two.

"No measurable translation."

Nobody celebrated.

Mael said, "Five minutes."

We held five.

Pava's breathing came through the tube at minute three.

Too fast.

Healer said, "Pava reduce."

Pava said, "If I reduce, center slips."

I checked the relation.

She was right.

Mael said, "I can take more."

"Not same direction," I said.

Arcrutus said, "Me."

"Also wrong geometry."

Pava laughed once through the tube.

"Good to be special."

Healer said, "Not funny."

Pava said, "A little funny."

I changed the link.

Not load.

Her return.

Static shaping still built channel tension every time the fracture pushed against her field.

Normally that tension returned through her forearm and shoulder.

I opened a narrow route from the shaping boundary into Mael's brace plate.

Not enough to make Mael carry her work.

Enough to give the recoil somewhere less stupid to go.

Pava inhaled sharply.

Then exhaled.

"Again."

I widened by almost nothing.

Healer said, "What did you do?"

Pava answered before I could.

"He gave my arm a drain."

"Greg."

"Established method."

"On an S-class pressure field?"

"New scale. Same principle."

"I hate that sentence."

"Fair."

Pava's breathing slowed.

Mael's brace plate groaned.

He looked at it.

Then at me.

"Manageable."

There.

That was the first time I supported an S-class person at the actual thing making them stop.

Not mana.

Not power.

The reason the power hurt to continue using.

Pava held center longer.

Not because I made her stronger.

She was already Pava Kett.

Because some of what normally made her quit was now being paid by a plate, Mael's stance, and my channels instead of her forearm alone.

Greg does not eliminate cost.

I had not said that yet.

I knew it anyway.

The fracture stopped moving for twenty-seven minutes.

Twenty-seven.

Long enough to install two physical shear keys from expedition stock.

Not permanent.

Better than people.

At minute twenty-four, structural specialist shouted, "Key two seated."

At twenty-six, "Key three."

Mael said, "Prepare release."

We stepped down slowly.

Pava first.

Then Mael.

Then Arcrutus.

I dismantled the shared relation last.

The shear keys took load.

The fracture held.

Survey watched the mark.

One minute.

Two.

No movement.

I raised my aura.

My knees immediately informed me that I was forty-eight.

Arcrutus caught my elbow.

Again.

"Stop doing that."

"Falling?"

"Catching me."

"No."

Fair.

Pava came down ten minutes later despite the healer's objections.

Her left forearm was wrapped again.

Her hand opened normally.

Mostly.

She looked at the shear keys.

Then at me.

"That was better."

"The keys?"

"My arm."

"Oh."

"You moved the return."

"Some."

"Do it again next time."

The healer said from behind her, "There should not be a next time today."

Pava ignored her.

"Greg."

I looked at her.

She flexed the hand.

"That was better."

Not gratitude.

Professional recognition.

From an S-class pressure specialist who had spent years knowing exactly where her own body stopped.

I understood why Arcrutus had put me in the bag.

I still did not forgive him.

We went back to the chamber.

Nhal's freed foreleg was still outside the restraint.

Good.

The tail trace had fallen four counts after the shear keys took over translation.

Better.

Mael showed her the new lower readings.

I explained what we had done.

"The fracture is braced against sliding for now."

Nhal listened.

"Does this reduce danger to your tail?"

Left.

Yes.

Arcrutus sat down hard.

Pava smiled.

Survey started updating the operation map.

The healer told everyone to eat.

For forty-three minutes, the system behaved.

Then shear key two snapped.

We heard it through the floor.

One hard metallic crack.

The lower tension cord pulled three times.

Emergency.

Pava was already standing when the second pull came.

The healer grabbed her wrapped arm.

"No."

Pava looked at her.

"That was center."

The third pull came.

Nhal's tail trace jumped nine counts.

The empty forelimb restraint slammed against the floor hard enough to chip stone.

Not around her.

Beside her.

One success still intact.

Everything else was moving.

Mael shouted, "Positions."

We ran.
