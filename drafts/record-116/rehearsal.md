## RECORD 116

## THE TERMS

Hale chose the basin.

Of course.

Not a quarry.

Too much stone history between us already.

An old flood-control basin west of the freight road.

Dry most years.

Wide.

Flat center.

Raised spill walls.

Broken maintenance stairs.

No houses.

No market.

No school.

No one living underneath anything.

Good.

His note contained four things.

WEST BASIN.

FOUR DAYS.

SUNRISE.

COME TO FINISH.

Tracy read it.

Then read it again.

“Four days.”

“Yes.”

“Why four?”

“I don’t know.”

“You’re going to ask?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because I do not care.”

She looked at me.

“You care about everything he does.”

Fair.

“Not this.”

She put the note down.

“You promised to tell me.”

“I am telling you.”

“Yes.”

“Growth.”

“Do not.”

“Sorry.”

She sat.

We were eating.

Of course.

Bad food this time.

My choice.

Punishment.

“What are the terms?” she asked.

“Those.”

“No.”

I waited.

“What happens if you get there and he brought people?”

“I leave.”

“If Authority is there?”

“I leave.”

“If civilians wander in?”

“Stop.”

“If he tries to move the fight outside?”

“Stop.”

“If you try?”

“I won’t.”

“That is not an answer.”

“Stop.”

Good.

“If he yields?”

I looked at her.

Hale would not.

“That was not my question.”

Good healer.

“If he yields, I stop.”

The answer surprised me.

Not because it was wrong.

Because I had not considered it.

Tracy saw.

“You had not thought about that.”

“No.”

“Then think.”

I did.

Hale surrendering.

Alive.

Choosing not to continue.

Could I stop?

Nessa.

Children.

Town.

Leg.

Nine years.

The lower anchor.

I know.

Could I stop?

“Yes.”

Tracy studied me.

“Why?”

“Because then he is not making the same choice again.”

There.

That was the answer.

Not forgiveness.

Not absolution.

If Hale finally chose not to carry revenge through another body when given the option, the thing I had spent nine years narrowing toward would change.

Maybe.

Tracy nodded.

“Good.”

“Stop.”

“No.”

“What if you yield?” she asked.

“I won’t.”

“Greg.”

“If I yield, he stops.”

“You know that?”

“Yes.”

“How?”

“Because he could have killed me twice.”

She looked at the peg.

“Yes.”

“And did not.”

“Yes.”

“First time he took your leg.”

“Yes.”

“Second time?”

“Stopped before permanent damage.”

She hated that.

So did I.

“Do you trust him?”

“No.”

“Do you trust that?”

“Yes.”

Different.

Important.

She wrote something on a napkin.

I stared.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Tracy.”

She turned it around.

IF GREG YIELDS, HALE STOPS.

Under it:

IF HALE YIELDS, GREG STOPS.

“Why write it?”

“Because words get stupid after blood loss.”

Fair.

She folded the napkin.

Put it in my coat pocket.

“I am not carrying duel paperwork.”

“You are now.”

I left it.

Four days.

That was enough time to become unbearable.

I worked the first two.

Small jobs.

Nothing dangerous.

Could not focus.

Did anyway.

Third day I trained lightly.

Tracy stopped me after twenty minutes.

“No more.”

“I am fine.”

“I know.”

“That is new.”

“Save it.”

For Hale.

She did not say the name.

Did not need to.

That evening she checked everything.

Socket.

Skin.

Shoulder.

Hip.

Channel response.

Closed-loop recovery.

Outward link.

I objected to the last.

“Why?”

“Because I want the data.”

“Now?”

“Yes.”

I linked to the weighted frame.

Clean.

Narrower than years ago.

Stable.

Good.

Sad.

Both.

Tracy marked it.

Then closed the book.

“Done.”

“Any advice?”

“Yes.”

I waited.

“Do not get stabbed.”

“That is weak.”

“Do not get hit.”

“Worse.”

“Come back.”

There.

Different category.

I looked at her.

She looked away first.

Good.

I said:

“I will try.”

“Bad answer.”

“Honest.”

She hated honest when it was not useful.

Current Tracy.

Good.

“Are you coming?”

She looked back.

“To the basin?”

“Near it.”

Long pause.

“Do you want me there?”

That was the right question.

Not old trust.

Current.

I thought.

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“In case I live.”

She stared.

“Asshole.”

“Yes.”

“In case he lives?”

There.

Harder.

“Yes.”

She nodded.

Healer.

Not executioner.

Not Number Two.

Herself.

“I will wait at the road.”

“Fine.”

“I will not enter unless the fight is over.”

“Fine.”

“If one of you is alive and asks for help, I help.”

I looked at her.

Including Hale.

She saw.

“Greg.”

“Fine.”

“Say it.”

“If Hale is alive and asks for medical help after the fight, you help.”

“Good.”

I hated good.

The morning came.

Of course.

Time does that.

Tracy drove.

Not because I could not.

Because she said my hip should not be stiff before a death match.

Medical reasoning has range.

We stopped half a mile from the basin.

Hale’s wagon was already there.

No driver.

No escort.

He had come alone.

Good.

Tracy saw it too.

“Looks clean.”

“Yes.”

She checked my harness one last time.

Then the contact plate.

Then the knife.

Not medical.

Still.

“You brought one.”

“Yes.”

“He will too.”

“Probably.”

“Any magic restrictions?”

“No.”

“Good.”

I looked at her.

“You approve?”

“No.”

“Then why good?”

“Rules discovered during a fight are expensive.”

There.

She handed me water.

I drank.

Too much.

She took it back.

“Enough.”

“You are very controlling.”

“Go.”

I did not.

Not immediately.

Tracy looked at me.

“What?”

There were things to say.

Thank you.

Sorry.

If I die.

First Life.

Second Life.

All terrible.

So I said:

“You were not Number Two.”

She stared.

“What?”

“In this life.”

“I know.”

“I am glad.”

Her expression changed.

Not hurt.

Understanding.

Good.

“So am I,” she said.

There.

That was enough.

I walked to the basin.

Hale stood in the center.

No coat.

No Authority marks.

Field trousers.

Shirt.

Boots.

Knife at the belt.

Nothing dramatic.

Nine fingers.

Old scar under the jaw.

He watched me approach.

My peg clicked against the dry stone.

Not rhythmically.

Slight hitch.

Always.

I stopped ten paces away.

Hale looked toward the road.

“Tracy?”

“Yes.”

“Healer?”

“Yes.”

“For you?”

“For whoever is alive after.”

He considered that.

“Good.”

“Stop saying that.”

“No.”

Same.

I looked around.

“No one else?”

“No.”

“Authority?”

“No.”

“Did you tell them?”

“I took leave.”

“For what?”

“Personal matter.”

I laughed.

Could not help it.

“That is one way to write it.”

“Yes.”

“What happens to your assignments?”

“Covered.”

Of course.

He had arranged continuity.

Useful man to the end.

“If you die?”

“Same.”

“Restitution?”

“Funded.”

That stopped me.

He had thought farther.

Of course.

“To the town?”

“Remaining claimants.”

“Why?”

He looked at me.

“Because dying does not erase debt.”

There.

His morality.

Still bizarre.

Still coherent.

I hated it.

“What about your people?”

“Which?”

“Family.”

Hale’s face changed.

Small.

So he had some.

Good to know after nine years.

“They know enough.”

“Enough for what?”

“That I may not return.”

I wanted names.

Did not ask.

Not now.

Hale looked at my peg.

“Ready?”

“Yes.”

He nodded.

Then:

“One thing.”

“What?”

“If you kill me, do not pretend it fixes anything.”

Anger.

Fast.

“I know.”

“Good.”

“Stop.”

“No.”

I almost smiled.

Did not.

“What about you?”

“What?”

“If you kill me?”

Hale thought.

Then:

“I bury you.”

That was not what I expected.

“Why?”

“You came.”

Simple.

Bizarre.

Fair.

“And the finger?”

He looked at the missing place.

“What about it?”

“I still have it.”

For the first time in nine years, Hale looked genuinely surprised.

“You kept it.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

I thought.

Proof.

Anger.

Trophy.

Evidence.

Fuel.

All changed over time.

“I took it.”

Hale stared.

Then laughed.

Once.

Actual laugh.

Small.

“That is mine.”

“No.”

He looked at the hand.

Then at me.

“No.”

Correct.

Not anymore.

The wind moved through the basin.

Dry.

Cool.

Sun just above the spill wall.

No spectators.

No song.

Good.

Hale drew his knife.

I drew mine.

He looked at me.

“No more measuring.”

“No.”

“One of us finishes.”

“Yes.”

“And if either yields?”

I touched the napkin in my pocket.

Ridiculous.

Useful.

“We stop.”

Hale’s eyes changed.

Not disappointment.

Respect maybe.

Bad word.

Recognition.

“Yes.”

Then nothing remained to negotiate.

Nine years had become ten paces.
