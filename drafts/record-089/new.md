## RECORD 089

## THE SECOND STEP

The right forelimb restraint was easier after we removed the collar.

That did not mean easy.

It meant fewer ways to die that we already knew about.

Progress.

The primary restraint carried six mapped functions.

Physical hold.

Lateral correction.

Chest alignment.

Tail interlock.

Two lower-fracture relations.

And one intermittent path that only appeared when Nhal shifted weight into the freed left leg.

I hated intermittent paths.

They are the magical equivalent of a noise your cart makes only when the mechanic is not listening.

Survey spent four hours provoking it without touching Nhal.

Change platform angle.

Change lower brace pressure.

Ask Nhal to load the left paw one count.

Unload.

Repeat.

The path appeared three times.

Then refused twice.

Arcrutus said, "It knows we're watching."

Survey said, "No."

Nhal made the low rough sound that meant amusement.

Survey looked at her.

"Do not encourage him."

Nhal showed teeth.

I liked this expedition less every hour.

Eventually Structural found the pattern.

The intermittent path did not respond to total load.

It responded to twist.

When Nhal's left shoulder moved forward while the tail anchor stayed fixed, the right restraint briefly accepted rotational correction across the chest.

Not much.

Enough to keep her spine from taking it.

The healer looked at the trace.

"So removing the right restraint could increase torsion through her chest."

Structural nodded.

"Unless stance changes before the load arrives."

Everyone looked at Nhal.

Nhal looked back.

I said, "This is the part where we ask the person with four legs how four legs work."

Arcrutus said, "Advanced support doctrine."

"Shut up."

We asked.

Not one question.

Nine.

Could she place the right forepaw forward immediately after release?

Yes.

Could she bear meaningful weight through it?

Yes.

Could she rotate the shoulder enough to reduce chest torsion?

Yes.

Could she do all three while keeping the tail relation inside current tolerance?

Three pulls.

Not like that.

Of course.

I tried again.

"Can you keep the tail relation stable while you move the right foreleg?"

Nhal spoke.

"Stable is moving."

I stared at her.

"You are doing this on purpose now."

"Yes."

Arcrutus laughed.

Survey said, "She is correct."

"I know she's correct."

That was why it hurt.

Nhal's tail relation was not a fixed value.

It was an active compensation.

Stable did not mean unchanged.

It meant changing in ways that kept the fracture from becoming worse.

Human instruments liked numbers.

Nhal lived in relationships.

I rewrote the question.

"Can you keep the fracture inside its current safe range while you move the right foreleg?"

One pull.

Yes.

Better.

Pava would have enjoyed that correction.

Fuck.

Grief arrived at stupid times.

I wrote the answer down.

We built the release around movement instead of restraint removal.

That distinction came from Nhal.

Not:

Take restraint off.

Then see what happens.

Instead:

Prepare every relation for the stance she intended to occupy.

Then let the restraint become unnecessary.

Crossing first.

Nhal first.

The operation map changed shape.

Survey drew expected motion rather than only existing paths.

Structural marked the right paw landing zone on the floor.

The healer checked joint range as best she could without contact.

Mael assigned the active corrections.

He stayed seated for most of planning because his knee had become the size of a fruit I did not like.

Nobody mentioned this comparison aloud.

Arcrutus took primary lateral correction.

Not because Mael could not.

Because Mael should not.

That was a new expedition skill too.

Using people who still had working joints.

Two physical anchors took chest alignment.

The flex plate took slow lower-fracture movement.

Survey's instrument web called fast precursor vibration.

Structural called twist.

I routed between them.

Not force.

Permission and correction.

If Arcrutus moved left, chest anchors did not mirror unless twist crossed threshold.

If the lower bridge flexed, the tail relation could change without my link fighting it.

If Nhal began the intended step, the right restraint was allowed to unload before her weight fully transferred.

Allowed.

That word had become important.

We prepared until Mael was satisfied.

Then Survey found one more problem.

Naturally.

"The right restraint still carries one old pressure return."

I looked.

"Where?"

She pointed at a trace Pava had marked two days earlier.

Tiny notation.

Crooked three again.

Survey had finally decoded it.

Not three.

A symbol Pava used for delayed return.

We had spent fourteen minutes reconstructing the wrong thing yesterday.

I laughed.

Survey looked offended.

"What?"

"She is still correcting us."

Survey looked at the mark.

Then smiled.

Small.

"Annoying woman."

Good.

We added the delayed return to anchor two.

Then rested another hour because competence is mostly refusing to be rushed by fear until fear becomes the actual deadline.

The foundation gave us the deadline itself.

Deep trace rose two counts.

Not catastrophic.

Consistent with the worsening rate from yesterday.

Mael said, "We proceed."

No one argued.

Nhal's final consent came through words.

"Proceed."

Her pronunciation was better.

The healer noticed too.

"Talking easier?"

Nhal breathed.

"A little."

"Pain?"

"Always."

The healer's face changed.

Nhal added, "Less."

That was somehow worse.

We took positions.

I lowered aura.

Found the six relationships.

Then stopped thinking of them as six lines.

That was my old mistake.

The restraint was one boundary with six kinds of consequence crossing it.

Force.

Rotation.

Alignment.

Permission.

Return.

Timing.

Different crossings.

Same piece of black material.

I set conditions around the crossing instead of pinning endpoints.

My channels settled cleaner.

Not comfortable.

Never comfortable.

But the old pulling sensation behind my shoulders was smaller.

Nhal noticed before the healer.

One mane filament lifted.

"Less pain?" she asked.

"Mine or yours?"

"Yours."

"Some."

"Good."

I looked at Arcrutus.

"Nobody say anything."

He looked innocent.

Poorly.

Mael said, "Begin."

Survey called baseline.

Structural confirmed bridge.

Arcrutus loaded lateral correction one count.

The right restraint loosened.

One mark.

Then another.

Nhal's shoulder shifted inside it.

The intermittent twist path appeared immediately.

Survey said, "Twist rise."

Structural said, "Expected."

I opened the chest-anchor permission.

Anchor one took half.

Anchor two took the delayed part.

The twist flattened.

"Good," Structural said.

Mael said, "Nhal."

Her movement.

The right paw lifted.

Not an inch this time.

Four.

Then six.

The primary restraint unloaded faster than expected.

Survey said, "Right restraint down three."

Arcrutus said, "I feel it."

"Do nothing," I told him.

"I know."

He sounded offended.

Nhal moved the paw forward.

The chest lattice brightened.

Structural called, "Twist two."

Survey said, "Lower bridge neutral."

I opened the second correction path.

Too slow.

The chest anchors hit late.

Nhal's body rotated before they caught.

The tail trace jumped.

Three counts.

The healer said, "Pain."

Mael said, "Hold."

Everything stopped except the things that could not.

Nhal's paw hovered above the floor.

Her shoulder trembled.

The restraint was half unloaded.

Worst possible position.

Arcrutus said, "I can take it."

I checked the geometry.

"Not all."

"How much?"

"One count left only."

He took one.

The tail trace fell one.

Not enough.

Survey said, "Chest twist sustained."

Structural said, "Landing fixes it if she can reach mark."

Healer said, "Can she?"

Nobody knew.

Except Nhal.

I looked at her.

"Can you finish the step?"

Her answer was not a rod.

"Yes."

Mael said, "Her choice. Finish."

Nhal moved.

Fast.

Not human fast.

Not S-class spectacle either.

Just faster than a body that size should have looked after centuries of immobility.

The paw hit the marked zone.

Claws struck black stone.

Her shoulder came forward.

Chest rotation reversed.

The anchors caught.

Tail trace dropped two.

Then one more.

The right restraint went slack.

Completely.

For one second nobody said anything.

Survey remembered her job first.

"Right interface load zero."

Structural said, "Primary restraint clear."

Healer said, "Weight bearing. Tremor moderate."

Mael said, "Hold configuration."

We held.

Nhal stood on both forelegs.

Not fully upright.

Her hindquarters remained fixed.

Chest still crossed by black structure.

Tail still buried in the fracture.

But both front paws were hers.

She shifted weight once.

Carefully.

The blue lattice rearranged around the movement.

Not against it.

Around it.

That was new.

Survey whispered, "It is adapting to her freedom."

I said, "Or she is adapting it."

Three pulls.

Not like that.

Nhal looked at us.

"Both."

Fair.

We maintained the new stance for twelve minutes.

Twice as long as the first leg.

My channels hurt less despite carrying more relationships.

That bothered me professionally.

Nhal's correction had mattered.

Not more mana.

Not more rank.

Better abstraction.

God, I hated when the answer was conceptual.

At minute eight, Arcrutus said, "Greg."

"What?"

"Your shoulders aren't shaking."

I checked.

They were not.

"Don't make it weird."

"Too late."

Mael said, "Focus."

We focused.

At minute twelve, Nhal lowered herself carefully into a resting stance that kept both forelegs outside the restraints.

The right black structure remained open beside her.

Two empty restraints now.

The chest interface load had fallen five counts simply because her shoulders could choose their own geometry.

Survey stared at the number.

"Five."

Structural checked.

"Confirmed."

I looked at the chest bands.

"That might be next."

The healer said, "Not today."

Mael said, "Correct."

Arcrutus said nothing.

Growth.

We dismantled temporary corrections.

Slowly.

The new stance held without them.

That mattered more than the release itself.

If freedom required six exhausted humans to continuously maintain it, we had only built a different prison.

This did not.

Not yet.

Nhal flexed both forepaws.

Then stretched one leg forward.

Slow.

Careful.

Her claws scraped four parallel lines in the dust.

She looked at them.

Then at Arcrutus.

"Two," she said.

He stared.

Then smiled.

"Two."

No laughter this time.

Just the number.

Later, after food, I sat with my notebook again.

Nhal watched.

Of course.

I drew the operation badly.

Crossing conditions.

Motion path.

Response windows.

The whole thing still looked like plumbing designed by a drunk spider.

Nhal said, "You make room."

I looked up.

"For what?"

She moved one freed paw.

"For what is already there."

I did not answer.

Arcrutus was pretending not to listen.

Mael was pretending to sleep.

Survey was definitely listening and pretending not to write.

Nhal looked at me.

"You do not give him strength."

She glanced at Arcrutus.

Then toward Mael.

"You do not give her shape."

Pava.

The absence in the sentence hurt.

Nhal continued.

"You make room for strength. For shape."

I looked at the notebook.

"Sometimes."

Nhal's eyes narrowed.

"Backward human."

"There it is."

Arcrutus laughed into his blanket.

I threw a folded scrap of map at him.

Missed.

S-class reflexes.

Unfair.

Nhal rested her chin near her freed paws.

For the first time she looked less suspended than lying down.

Still bound.

Still carrying the foundation.

Different posture.

Tiny difference.

Huge difference.

The chest interface loosened another fraction when she exhaled.

Survey's slate clicked.

We all looked.

Nhal did not.

She closed her eyes.

"Tomorrow," she said.

Mael opened one eye.

"Tomorrow," he agreed.
