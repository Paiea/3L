## RECORD 130

## THE DISCHARGE

“It was not a deadline.”

I looked at the sword.

“Within a year.”

“It was an estimate.”

“Deadline.”

“No.”

“Fine.”

I stood.

“Show me.”

Ithar’s eye narrowed.

“Show you what?”

I pointed at the sword.

“The reason you looked at the cave mouth when I asked how much it releases.”

“No.”

“That was suspicious.”

“It was practical.”

“Even better.”

Ithar looked at me.

Then at the sword.

Then back at me.

“You intend to retrieve this object in another life you may not have.”

“Yes.”

“You have not agreed to the experiment.”

“No.”

“You have not established that the experiment is possible.”

“No.”

“You have not established that the sword will remain where history placed it.”

“No.”

“And you wish to expend its stored charge now.”

“Yes.”

He was quiet.

I smiled.

“You hate that this is reasonable.”

“No.”

“You do.”

“I dislike that you have arrived at a correct conclusion for irresponsible reasons.”

“I’ll take it.”

He lifted the sword.

Carefully.

That got my attention.

Not because it was heavy.

Ithar had moved things heavier than houses without seeming to notice.

Careful meant something else.

He carried it toward the passage.

I followed.

The climb out of his hoard chamber took longer than I remembered.

Maybe because we were carrying a problem.

Not me.

Him.

Important distinction.

We emerged onto a shelf of black stone above the cloud line.

Morning had happened without asking us.

Good.

The world was pale gold along one edge.

Mountains everywhere.

Sharp.

Old.

Quiet.

Ithar walked past me.

The sword looked stupid in his claw.

Too small.

A human weapon carried by something large enough to make weapons feel decorative.

He stopped near the edge.

I looked across the valley.

“Which mountain?”

His head turned.

“What?”

“If we’re doing this, I would like to know which one stops being a mountain.”

“It will remain a mountain.”

“Less exciting.”

“It will lose part of itself.”

“Better.”

He indicated a ridge across the valley.

Far.

Very far.

I squinted.

“That one?”

“Yes.”

“Anybody there?”

“No.”

“You checked?”

“Yes.”

“When?”

“Recently.”

“Dragon recently?”

His eye moved toward me.

“Yesterday.”

Good.

Important clarification.

I stepped back.

“How does it work?”

“You release the reservoir.”

“All at once?”

“Yes.”

“Can you release less?”

“Yes.”

I looked at him.

“You were going to leave that out.”

“You asked what the full discharge does.”

“Fair.”

“What triggers it?”

“Intent, contact, route.”

“Very descriptive.”

“You are not using it.”

“Also fair.”

Ithar lowered the blade.

Not like a swordsman.

No stance.

No flourish.

The point angled toward the distant ridge.

Nothing happened.

For one second.

Then the sword stopped being quiet.

I felt it before I saw anything.

Pressure collapsed toward the blade.

Not from the air.

From inside it.

Something stored too densely to feel like normal mana unfolded in one direction.

White crossed the valley.

Not light.

Too solid.

Too fast.

The ridge disappeared behind it.

There was no dramatic pause.

No time for the mountain to consider.

The upper third of the ridge simply ceased agreeing with its previous shape.

Stone erupted sideways.

Then sound arrived.

The shelf beneath my feet moved.

Clouds tore apart below us.

A flock of something very large abandoned the next valley at once.

I stared.

Across the distance, where a long shoulder of mountain had been, there was now an open wound of pale stone and falling debris.

Not gone.

Ithar had been technically correct.

Still a mountain.

Less mountain.

Much less.

Dust climbed into the morning.

I looked at the sword.

Then at Ithar.

Then back at the ridge.

“Fucking hell.”

“Yes.”

“That was full?”

“Yes.”

“How long?”

“To charge?”

“Yes.”

Ithar considered.

“Four years.”

I stared.

“Four years?”

“Approximately.”

“That is ridiculous.”

“It is an old weapon.”

“That does not make four years less ridiculous.”

“It was not built for impatient humans.”

“Was it built for dragons?”

“No.”

“Then whoever built it had terrible planning.”

Ithar looked toward the ruined ridge.

“They built that.”

Fair.

I walked closer to the sword.

It looked the same.

That annoyed me.

No glow.

No cracking.

No smoke.

Just old metal.

“You said it draws from contact.”

“Yes.”

“It’s empty now.”

“Yes.”

I reached.

“Do not.”

I stopped.

There.

Good.

“How empty?”

“Enough.”

“Useful.”

I crouched.

The sword lay across Ithar’s claw.

“Put it down.”

“No.”

“I want to test something.”

“No.”

“That is why we came outside.”

“No. We came outside because you insisted on seeing the discharge.”

“Which created a new test condition.”

Ithar stared at me.

I smiled.

He put the sword on the stone.

Good dragon.

Bad decision.

I reached slowly.

“Greg.”

“I heard you.”

“That is rarely the same as listening.”

My fingers touched the grip.

The world went gray.

Not gradually.

One moment I had mana.

The next I had a sword attached to every open part of me asking where the rest was.

I let go.

Too slow.

My knees hit stone.

Air left my lungs.

My stomach folded.

For a second I thought I might vomit over the side of Ithar’s mountain.

That would have been undignified.

Ithar’s claw moved between me and the sword.

Not touching it.

Blocking.

I sat back.

“Fuck.”

“Yes.”

“That was immediate.”

“Yes.”

“You could have said immediate.”

“I said do not.”

“Different information.”

My hands shook.

Not badly.

Enough.

I checked my channels.

Still there.

Thin.

Drained.

Angry.

The sword lay six feet away looking innocent.

I hated it immediately.

Good sign.

“How much did it take?”

“Enough.”

“Ithar.”

“I do not know your exact reserves.”

“I’m B-class.”

“Human category.”

“Useful human category.”

“It consumed a meaningful portion before you released it.”

“A meaningful portion.”

“Yes.”

“I almost passed out.”

“Yes.”

“From touching it for what?”

“Less than a second.”

I looked at the sword.

Then at him.

“Nineteen-year-old me cannot move that after using it.”

“No.”

There.

Important.

Not theoretical.

Not later.

Clean.

“If I fire the big one, I lose the sword.”

“Unless someone else moves it.”

“Someone with more mana.”

“Yes.”

“How much more?”

“I do not know.”

“Could Arcrutus?”

Ithar looked at me.

That was an answer.

“Interesting.”

“It was not.”

“He has stupid reserves.”

“Yes.”

“I know him.”

“Not when you are nineteen.”

Right.

Fuck.

Different problem.

I looked at the sword again.

“What if I wrap it?”

“No.”

“You answered before I finished.”

“Cloth will not stop it.”

“Leather?”

“No.”

“Wood?”

“No.”

“Gloves?”

“No.”

“Very thick gloves?”

“No.”

I stared.

“You’re enjoying this.”

“No.”

“Dragon liar.”

His nostril opened.

Good.

I pulled a strip of cloth from the inside of my pack.

Old.

Clean enough.

Wrapped it around the grip.

Ithar watched.

He did not stop me.

That should have stopped me.

It did not.

I touched the cloth.

The pull hit immediately.

Less direct.

Not less hungry.

I jerked back.

“Fuck.”

“Yes.”

“That is stupid.”

“No.”

“It is touching cloth.”

“You are touching the cloth.”

“Yes.”

“The cloth is touching the sword.”

“Yes.”

“The route is continuous.”

I stared at him.

There.

Not physical contact.

Connection.

Better.

Worse.

I looked at the sword.

“Barrier.”

Ithar’s head shifted slightly.

There.

Good question.

I formed one around my hand.

Thin.

Dense.

A clean separation.

Hand here.

Everything else there.

I reached.

The Barrier touched the wrapped grip.

For half a heartbeat, nothing happened.

Then my Barrier thinned.

Not shattered.

Not pierced.

Consumed.

I pulled away before the sword found my hand behind it.

I stared.

Ithar stared at me.

“That,” I said, “was interesting.”

“No.”

“You saw it.”

“Yes.”

“It ate the Barrier.”

“No.”

I looked at him.

“No?”

“It drew from an active structure connected to it.”

“That is eating the Barrier.”

“It is not.”

“What would you call it?”

“Drawing.”

“Into itself.”

“Yes.”

“Until the Barrier stops existing.”

“Yes.”

“Eating.”

“No.”

I smiled.

There.

This was going to be fun.

I stood.

Slowly.

My mana had not recovered.

My curiosity had.

Bad combination.

“So.”

I pointed at the sword.

“When full, it is relatively safe.”

“Yes.”

“Because it does not need anything.”

“Crude.”

“Correct?”

“Yes.”

“When empty, it pulls from whatever active magical structure it can reach.”

“Through a viable route.”

“Sure.”

“Important distinction.”

“Fine.”

I pointed toward my hand.

“Body.”

“Yes.”

“Barrier.”

“Yes.”

“Ward?”

Ithar was silent.

There.

I smiled.

“Ward.”

“It would depend on the ward.”

“Good.”

“Do not.”

“I didn’t say anything.”

“You are going to.”

“What if I touch it to a defensive ward?”

“It would draw.”

“Until?”

“Until the route breaks, the ward is dropped, the sword fills, or one of the structures fails.”

I looked across the valley.

Dust still climbed where the ridge used to be.

Then back at the sword.

“So it blows up mountains when full.”

“Yes.”

“And when empty, it eats defenses.”

“No.”

I smiled.

“You really hate useful terminology.”

“That is not its purpose.”

“I do not care about its purpose.”

“That is obvious.”

I crouched again.

Farther away this time.

Learning.

“What happens if somebody catches it?”

“Empty?”

“Yes.”

“They lose mana.”

“How fast?”

“Depends.”

“Could it interrupt reinforcement?”

“Yes.”

“Spell concentration?”

“Yes.”

“Knock somebody unconscious?”

“Yes.”

“Kill them?”

“Yes.”

I stared at the sword.

Then laughed.

Ithar’s eye narrowed.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“No.”

“Just imagining throwing it at someone.”

“No.”

“If they catch it…”

“No.”

“They catch swords.”

“Greg.”

“That is literally a trained response.”

“Do not build a combat doctrine around opponents voluntarily holding your weapon.”

“It’s not the whole doctrine.”

“You have had the weapon for less than an hour.”

“I do not have it.”

“Good.”

“Yet.”

His eyes narrowed.

Good.

I looked at the sword again.

One mountain attack.

Then hunger.

Not useless after discharge.

Different.

Maybe worse.

Maybe better.

Ithar watched my face.

“That expression is becoming expensive.”

I smiled.

“You showed me a mountain destroyer.”

“It is considerably more sophisticated than that.”

“Sure.”

“And now?”

I pointed at the empty blade.

“Now you showed me the interesting part.”
