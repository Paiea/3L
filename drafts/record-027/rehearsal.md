## RECORD 027

## THE LEASEHOLDER

We signed for five years because Mina threatened to rent the room to musicians.

That was not the only reason.

It was the fastest one.

The room was behind ours.

Same floor.

Different door.

For years it belonged to an old tailor named Siva who worked downstairs until his eyes became bad enough that thread started winning.

Then his daughter moved him north.

The room sat empty six weeks.

I did not notice.

Nessa did.

Of course.

We were coming upstairs after dinner at my parents' when she stopped on the landing.

The door beside ours stood open.

Mina was inside with a broom.

Dust rolled around her feet.

“Siva gone?” Nessa asked.

“Six weeks.”

I looked at her.

“You knew?”

“Yes.”

“Why didn't I?”

“You thought he was sleeping.”

“For six weeks?”

“You do not monitor neighbors well.”

Fair.

Mina leaned on the broom.

“You want it?”

“No,” I said.

“Yes,” Nessa said.

Mina smiled.

Traitor.

The room was half again larger than ours.

Two windows. Small stove. Separate wash basin. One narrow back room that could hold a bed if the person using it had no objection to walls.

Nessa walked straight to the windows.

I walked straight to the floor.

Uneven.

“Absolutely not.”

“You haven't heard the rent.”

“Floor slopes.”

Mina said, “Toward the drain.”

“There is no drain.”

“Then toward where a drain should be.”

“This building is a crime.”

“You live here.”

“Yes. I have evidence.”

The rent for both rooms together was cheaper than two separate apartments and much more than one.

Obviously.

Numbers are cruel that way.

Mina offered permission to open a doorway between them.

I stared at her.

“You want me to open your wall.”

“You fixed the bakery feed.”

“That is not masonry.”

“You know masons.”

“That is closer.”

Nessa had already started measuring where her district desk could go.

Not our desk.

Hers.

Good sign.

Bad for my negotiation position.

“We don't need it,” I said later.

Household ledger open.

Of course.

Nessa had written the new rent beside the old and circled the difference.

“We don't need most things.”

“That is not an argument for buying them.”

“Renting.”

“Worse.”

“Why?”

“After five years we own nothing.”

Nessa stared.

“That sounded like your father.”

“Take it back.”

“No.”

I looked at the numbers.

We could afford it.

That was not the same as comfortable.

If the answer had been no, arithmetic would have protected me from deciding.

Instead it said possible.

Nessa tapped the narrow room on Mina's sketch.

“Tools.”

“I have a tool cabinet.”

“You have tools under the bed.”

“Temporary.”

“Seven years.”

“Long temporary.”

“Desk for me in front. Tools and records in back.”

“Where do my records go?”

“You have district office.”

“I also have records.”

“Then we share shelves.”

I made a face.

Nessa saw it.

“Your old station books do not deserve more room than my current schedules.”

“I did not say they did.”

“Face.”

“Security flaw.”

She smiled.

There was another reason for the room neither of us named.

Space.

Enough that life could become something else without requiring an immediate move.

Guest.

Family.

Work.

Storage.

A sick parent for a week.

Lena and Eli after a bridge failure.

Someone not yet imagined.

Some doors are easier to open when you do not demand to know which future walks through them.

The lease was harder.

Mina wanted five years.

I wanted one.

Nessa wanted five before hearing why.

“Why five?” I asked Mina.

“Because opening the wall costs me money if you leave.”

“We pay the wall.”

“Then because I don't want to find tenants next year.”

“Honest.”

“Yes.”

“One year.”

“No.”

“Two.”

“Five.”

“Three.”

“Five.”

“You are bad at negotiation.”

“I own the building.”

Strong position.

Nessa asked, “Rent fixed?”

Mina looked at her.

“First three years.”

“Five.”

“No.”

“Four fixed, fifth capped.”

I turned.

“What are you doing?”

“Negotiating.”

“I was negotiating.”

“You were counting down.”

“That is a recognized technique.”

“Not when she only says five.”

Mina smiled.

They negotiated without me for ten minutes.

I resented efficiency when excluded from it.

Five-year lease.

Rent fixed three years. Fourth increase limited. Fifth could rise more but required four months' notice. We paid for the doorway. Mina paid plaster finish. Structural repairs hers. Damage caused by my improvements ours.

That last line had my name on it without using my name.

Then came the signature.

Not like East Two.

No workers under my judgment.

No safety condition.

Just five years.

That was somehow heavier.

First-life Greg had signed leases near contracts and warehouses and temporary houses.

I remembered none of those signatures.

Most places had been containers around work.

This one felt like a statement.

Not marriage.

Not ownership.

Not forever.

Five years was aggressively not forever.

Still long enough that I could not call the apartment temporary housing between important events.

Nessa signed first.

NESSA VALE.

I signed GREGORY.

I hated the full name here too.

Mina witnessed, took our deposit, and immediately became less friendly.

Landlord transformation.

The doorway took two days.

I did not open it myself.

Growth.

Also the wall contained an old drain sleeve I did not trust.

A mason named Davi opened it while I stood nearby offering suggestions until he asked whether I wanted to pay double.

I left.

We moved Nessa's district desk into the front room.

Mine went beside it eventually.

Two desks.

Different heights.

Her shelves neat.

Mine vertical piles defended as active reference.

The back room became tools.

Then records.

Then winter storage.

Then tools again after Nessa found oily fittings beside blankets.

“We need categories.”

“We have categories.”

“Everything is under MISCELLANEOUS.”

“That is a category.”

“It is surrender.”

We bought another chair.

A proper one.

The old stool moved to the back.

The table stayed.

Of course.

It looked smaller in the larger space.

That bothered me for a day.

Then people filled around it.

Lena and Oren came for dinner.

Eli slept in the narrow room when the bridge tram stopped during a storm.

My mother stayed two nights after a healer procedure because our stairs were fewer than hers.

My father complained that the second room was wasted on paper until Nessa showed him the district map. Then he started giving opinions about it.

Lorn used the desk to write private work sheets when his house was too loud.

Perrin drew something obscene in the margin of one.

We kept it until a client nearly saw.

The room did not decide what our family became.

Space is not destiny.

Neither is a lease.

We simply made room before we knew every reason we might need it.

The fifth year seemed absurdly far away when we signed.

Then it arrived with the casual cruelty of calendars.

We renewed.

Higher rent.

Different terms.

Mina claimed the building had improved.

I pointed at the floor.

Still sloped.

She said character had appreciated.

Nessa laughed.

I lost again.

I remember the first lease because it was the first time staying acquired a duration.

Before that, staying was what happened when I did not leave.

Four weeks became eleven years.

One night became thousands.

Nessa moved in because rent was stupid.

Life accumulated by default.

The lease was different.

Five years.

Written down.

Chosen.

Not forever.

Long enough.
