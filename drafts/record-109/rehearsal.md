## RECORD 109

## THE YEARS BETWEEN

People remember revenge as a line.

It was not.

It was laundry.

Invoices.

Training.

Bad sleep.

Good meals.

Paperwork.

Three months where nothing happened.

Then one week where everything did.

Then more nothing.

Hale remained alive.

That did not mean I spent every morning sharpening something.

Mostly I went to work.

Revenge is expensive.

So is being disabled.

So is living.

The first full year after the town cost more than I had.

Prosthetics.

Healing.

Travel.

Investigators.

Information.

Legal fees.

Lost work.

Then work resumed and money returned slowly.

Authority restrictions eased.

Not vanished.

Managed.

I took structural support contracts.

Field reinforcement.

Emergency stabilization.

A few ugly jobs nobody wanted.

Not because I had become noble.

Because they paid.

Also because useful work kept me from thinking about Hale every hour.

Sometimes only every ten minutes.

Progress.

Tracy remained one consultation at a time.

That lasted longer than either of us expected.

No formal partnership.

No half.

No Number Two.

She billed me.

I paid.

Sometimes late.

She charged late fees.

Of course.

“You charged me two copper.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because you were late.”

“It cost you nothing.”

“It cost me irritation.”

“That is not billable.”

“It is now.”

I paid.

The plan changed.

Body.

Magic.

Behavior.

Still three columns.

More rows.

The socket improved.

The peg improved.

My skin adapted.

Then broke down again after a bad wet season.

The shoulder got stronger.

Then worse.

Then stronger differently.

Bodies are irritating because progress does not respect narrative.

You can improve for six months and wake Tuesday with inflammation because you slept badly.

Tracy loved this.

Not the pain.

The evidence against arrogance.

“See?”

“No.”

“Bodies are not linear.”

“I know.”

“You behave like they are.”

“No.”

“Graph.”

I hated the graph.

She kept it.

The loop grew.

Slowly.

At first I measured seconds.

Then movement.

Then complexity.

Could I hold it while walking?

While turning?

While receiving impact?

While casting Barrier?

While using Draw?

While supporting an object?

While supporting another person?

That last one mattered.

The answer became yes.

Poorly.

Then better.

The paths did not exclude each other immediately.

That would have been convenient.

Instead they competed.

Bandwidth.

Recovery.

Adaptation.

The closed route strengthened faster.

The outward route still worked.

Sometimes beautifully.

That made the choice harder.

If something had broken cleanly, I could have stopped.

Nothing did.

The decline was percentage points.

Small.

Tracy tracked them.

Of course.

Year two.

Joren remained in custody.

Sera remained dead.

Hale worked.

Flood response.

Escort.

Two monster suppressions.

One mining disaster.

I knew because I tracked him.

Not obsessively.

Professionally obsessive.

Different.

Tracy disagreed.

She had stopped arguing that I should stop revenge entirely.

That was new.

Instead she argued specifics.

Bad lead.

Unnecessary risk.

Wrong target.

Too much money.

Too little sleep.

Bad information.

Once:

“You are paying this man for rumors.”

“He has contacts.”

“He has alcoholism.”

“Both can be true.”

“Not at this rate.”

She renegotiated him down.

That bothered me for days.

“You are helping.”

“I am reducing waste.”

“On revenge.”

“Waste remains waste.”

Current Tracy.

Dangerous.

She never became old Tracy.

Good.

She became something else.

Not less.

Different.

She knew the town story.

She knew Nessa.

Not personally.

Through me.

The twins remained harder.

I used son and daughter too often.

Names hurt.

That became obvious.

Tracy did not push.

Good.

One consultation ended early because I said my daughter’s name and could not continue.

Tracy charged the full hour.

I appreciated that.

Normality.

Year three.

I reached C-class execution reliably.

Not rank exactly.

Capability.

Authority categories lagged because my path was strange.

Support.

Solo support.

Structural.

Combat-adjacent.

Hostile stabilization.

People argued.

I worked.

B-class people began treating me differently.

Not as equal.

As annoying.

Useful threshold.

Peg-Leg Greg spread.

I stopped correcting everyone.

Mostly.

That saved time.

Tracy noticed.

“You accepted it.”

“No.”

“You answer.”

“Operational necessity.”

“Mm.”

I hated mm.

Hale heard about my work.

Sometimes through Authority.

Sometimes because people talk.

Once a message arrived after a tunnel job.

No greeting.

No signature needed.

HEARD YOU HELD THE EAST CUT.

I knew the handwriting.

I wrote back:

HEARD YOU STILL HAVE NINE FINGERS.

Petty.

He replied:

CORRECT.

I hated him.

Tracy saw the note.

“Are you pen pals?”

“No.”

“Looks like correspondence.”

“Do not.”

She smiled.

Good.

Hale never stopped being the destination.

But the destination moved farther away as I understood the road better.

At first I thought power was the gap.

Then technique.

Then body.

Then access.

Then information.

Then timing.

Then law.

Then reputation.

Then money.

Revenge had systems too.

Annoying.

Year four.

The graph changed category.

Not much.

Enough.

Tracy circled the outward-link recovery line.

Then wrote:

PERSISTENT.

I looked at her.

“How persistent?”

“Three months.”

“Could recover.”

“Yes.”

“Will?”

“I do not know.”

There.

Point approaching.

Not crossed.

Still choices.

“You said you would tell me.”

“I am telling you.”

I looked at the inward metrics.

Better.

Much.

The loop had become architecture.

Not trick.

My body expected it.

That was the first truly dangerous sign.

Closed return no longer felt foreign.

Outward extension did.

Slightly.

That hurt more than the shoulder.

Tracy watched me read.

“What do you want?”

“Time.”

“Take it.”

“Hale gets time too.”

“Yes.”

I looked at her.

She did not apologize.

Good.

“He ages,” she said.

“Slowly.”

“You age.”

“Faster.”

“Correct.”

“Helpful.”

“Current data.”

I laughed.

She did too.

Then we ate.

That mattered.

The quarry happened near the end of year four.

Not revenge.

Not Hale.

Work.

Of course.

Authority had been chasing a B-class reinforcement criminal named Vael Orren for six months.

Not famous.

Not Named.

Dangerous enough.

Good at breaking containment.

Better at escaping.

He used old quarries and cut roads because reinforcement fighters love terrain that gives them things to break.

Authority asked for support.

Not me specifically.

Then the local captain saw the roster.

“Peg-Leg Greg?”

I sighed.

“Yes.”

“Good.”

That was new.

Not the name.

Good.

People had started asking for me.

Not because of Hale.

Not because of the town.

Because of work.

That should have felt better.

It did.

Mostly.

The quarry had three levels.

Stone ramps.

Cut faces.

Old cranes.

Drain trenches.

Loose aggregate everywhere.

Terrible for a peg.

Excellent for load.

Vael Orren knew the terrain.

So did I after one hour.

The plan was simple.

Team enters from two sides.

I support containment.

Nobody fights him alone.

Good plan.

Naturally it failed.

A blast closed the upper ramp.

Second team lost access.

Our lead got thrown thirty feet and broke an arm.

Two people trapped behind a stone slide.

Vael came through the dust toward me.

B-class reinforcement.

Healthy.

Two legs.

Combat specialist.

I was C-class support on paper.

One leg.

Tired.

And for the first time in years, completely calm.

Not because I thought I was stronger.

Because the quarry had become a map of load.

Vael looked at me.

Then the peg.

Then smiled.

Wrong.

Behind him, somebody shouted:

“Greg, fall back!”

I planted the peg.

Stone under it.

Good stone.

Not a blessing.

A leg would have been better.

Still.

This was what I had.

I closed the loop.

Vael started running.

Ithar interrupted.

“You fought a B-class combatant alone.”

“Yes.”

“As C-class support.”

“Yes.”

“With one leg.”

“Part of one.”

“And won.”

I looked at him.

“Eventually.”

He waited.

I smiled.

“Not because I hit harder.”

The quarry had plenty of things stronger than both of us.
