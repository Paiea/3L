## RECORD 124

## THE DEPARTURE

Kelan wanted to come.

Of course.

“No.”

He stared at me.

“Why?”

“Line.”

“There are other people.”

“Yes.”

“You are going alone.”

“Yes.”

“That is stupid.”

“Yes.”

“Then why?”

Good question.

We were standing in the Station Fourteen planning room.

Years after the first drift.

Same stone.

Different equipment.

More sensors.

More people.

More fear.

The Line had stopped failing politely.

Still no apocalypse.

Worse in another way.

Frequent.

Station Seven lost lower anchoring for three minutes.

Station Nineteen required permanent rerouting.

Two western districts had evacuation plans now.

Authority had stopped saying seasonal.

Growth.

Kelan had his own table.

Not mine.

Maps.

Crew rotations.

Training notes.

A list of people I did not know well enough.

Good.

He had become expensive too.

Different.

“Take someone,” he said.

“No.”

“Me.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because I need you here.”

He laughed once.

Not pleased.

“That sounds convenient.”

“It is.”

“Greg.”

He was not twenty-two anymore.

Good.

Mid-thirties.

Stronger.

Better.

His spear remained.

Support too.

Not S-class.

Not me.

Good.

“If the Line shifts while you’re gone,” I said, “who takes Fifteen?”

“Kera.”

“She takes Fifteen. Who takes Fourteen if Seven also moves?”

He stopped.

Good.

“Him.”

I pointed at his own name on the board.

He hated that.

“I could reassign.”

“Yes.”

“And?”

“Then someone worse takes your work.”

“Someone worse takes yours when you leave.”

“There.”

He stopped.

Different.

That was the answer.

My work had replacements now.

Not equal.

Enough.

Kelan’s did too.

Not enough in both places.

The system had become distributed.

That was the point.

No one person should be civilization.

First-Life Greg had learned that late.

Tracy taught it earlier.

Kelan lived it.

“Still want to come?” I asked.

“Yes.”

Of course.

“Why?”

“Because you’re going to meet an ancient dragon.”

Fair.

“That is a good reason.”

“Then?”

“No.”

He swore.

Long.

Good.

Tracy entered halfway through.

Her clinic had expanded again.

She had silver in her hair now.

Not much.

Enough.

So did I.

More.

Rude.

She put a medical pack on the table.

“For the climb.”

“I have one.”

“This one is better.”

“Expensive?”

“Yes.”

“Of course.”

She looked at Kelan.

“He trying to come?”

“Yes.”

“No,” she said.

Kelan threw both hands up.

“Why does everyone decide this for me?”

“Because you have work.”

“So does he.”

“He is old.”

I stared.

“Fuck you.”

“Current data.”

Kelan laughed.

Traitor.

Tracy looked at the board.

Then at me.

“Route?”

I had one.

Nhal’s gift.

First Life.

Hidden name.

Access conditions.

Not map exactly.

Instructions.

Timing.

Markers.

A way through old territory people avoided for good reasons.

I had kept it decades.

Used none.

Now.

“I know it.”

“That is not what I asked.”

“Six days if weather holds.”

“And if it does not?”

“Eight.”

“Food?”

“Yes.”

“Water?”

“Yes.”

“Leg?”

I looked down.

Peg.

Latest version.

Better than the first.

Still worse than a leg.

“Annoying.”

“Skin?”

“Good.”

“Shoulder?”

“Fine.”

She stared.

“Good.”

“Fine.”

“Bad answer.”

“Stable.”

“Better.”

There.

Years.

Still us.

Kelan watched.

“You two are exhausting.”

“Yes,” both of us said.

Too fast.

He laughed again.

Good.

We went over the Line response plan one more time.

Not because they needed me.

Because leaving makes people perform care through redundancy.

Station Fourteen.

Fifteen.

Seven.

Nineteen.

Escalation thresholds.

Authority contacts.

Emergency release.

Evacuation.

Kelan corrected one assumption.

I accepted.

Good.

Then he asked:

“What exactly are you asking Ithar?”

There.

The room changed.

Tracy looked at me too.

They both knew the public answer.

The Line.

Foundational support architecture.

Nhal containment.

Ancient systems.

Maybe Ithar knew enough to explain why lower anchors were adapting.

Maybe not.

Still worth climb.

That was true.

Not complete.

I said:

“The Line.”

Kelan stared.

“Only?”

Bad student.

Good.

I looked at Tracy.

She knew about the return research.

Not Kelan.

Not fully.

Her expression stayed neutral.

Helpful.

“No,” I said.

Kelan’s face changed.

“What else?”

I could tell him.

Third run.

Possibility.

The private question.

The thing Ithar had just dragged out of me here in the cave.

Memory nested badly.

At the time, before the climb, I did not.

I said:

“Personal.”

Kelan frowned.

“That is weak.”

“Yes.”

“Dangerous?”

“Probably not.”

“Probably?”

“Ancient dragon.”

Fair.

He looked angry.

Good.

Not child anger.

Colleague.

Friend.

Person excluded from a decision that affected someone he cared about.

He deserved more.

I still did not give it.

That remains mine.

Not proud.

True.

“Do I need to know?”

He asked.

I thought.

“If I do not come back, no.”

His face hardened.

“Terrible answer.”

“Yes.”

“If you do?”

“Maybe.”

He looked like he wanted to hit me.

Good.

Could probably do it now.

That was comforting.

Tracy interrupted.

“You are coming back.”

I looked at her.

“Bad medical certainty.”

“Yes.”

“Unprofessional.”

“Yes.”

“Fine.”

Good.

The morning I left, Kelan came anyway.

Not to climb.

Road.

First mile.

Tracy too.

No ceremony.

Good.

We walked until the western road split.

One route toward stations.

One toward old high country.

Kelan stopped.

“This is where I turn.”

“Yes.”

He looked up the mountain route.

Then at me.

“If the dragon eats you, I’m going to be very annoyed.”

“Reasonable.”

“If you learn anything useful?”

“I send it.”

“If you learn anything useless?”

“Also.”

“Good.”

Stop.

No.

He held out a hand.

I took it.

Not hug.

Fine.

His grip had changed over years.

Reinforcement disciplined.

Support underneath.

His.

I felt it because of course I did.

“You did good,” I said.

Kelan’s face closed.

Bad.

Past tense.

I corrected.

“You do good.”

Better.

He nodded.

“Do not make this weird.”

“Trying.”

“You’re bad at it.”

Apparently everyone agreed.

He turned back toward the Line.

That mattered.

Not standing there waiting for Greg.

Work.

His.

Tracy stayed another half mile.

Of course.

Then stopped too.

She handed me one last packet.

“What?”

“Food.”

“I have food.”

“This is better.”

I opened it.

She was right.

Annoying.

We stood.

No dramatic goodbye.

We had survived enough of those.

“Do you think he knows?” she asked.

“Kelan?”

“Yes.”

“About what?”

She stared.

Bad answer.

“The other question.”

There.

“No.”

“Should he?”

“I do not know.”

She nodded.

Not approval.

Classification.

Then:

“Do you know what you want?”

“No.”

“Better.”

I looked at her.

“Do not become Ithar.”

“Someone has to practice.”

Fair.

She adjusted one strap on the medical pack.

Tiny.

Old habit.

Current relationship.

Earned.

“Come back.”

“I’ll try.”

“Bad answer.”

“Honest.”

She hated that.

Still.

Then she hugged me.

Unexpected.

Brief.

Good.

“Do not make that weird either.”

“I was not going to.”

“You were.”

Probably.

She turned back.

I watched until she disappeared around the road bend.

Then I climbed.

Six days.

Stone.

Cold.

Peg.

Pain.

Old markers.

Nhal’s instructions.

The hidden route.

Ithar.

Everything eventually became this cave.

The dragon looked at me now.

Memory caught up to present.

For the first time in many records, there was no later version of me hiding behind narration.

Only me.

Here.

Ithar lowered his head.

“Kelan remained with the Line.”

“Yes.”

“Because he was needed.”

“Yes.”

“And because you did not want him exposed to this.”

“That too.”

“Why?”

I looked at him.

“Because this is my question.”

“Which one?”

There.

Still.

The Line.

Nhal.

Another beginning.

All braided now.

I looked toward the cave mouth.

Far below, humans maintained a failing system without me.

Good.

Necessary.

Then back at Ithar.

“The one I still do not know how to ask.”

He settled against the stone.

“Then ask badly.”

Fuck.

Good dragon.
