## RECORD 133

## THE LAST MEAL

I finished.

That was stranger than I expected.

For a while, neither of us spoke.

There was nothing left for me to explain.

No missing year.

No important correction.

No person I had skipped because telling Ithar about them hurt too much.

I had told him about Nhal.

Pava.

Arcrutus.

The Line.

Nessa.

The children.

Hale.

The years after.

The things I had changed because I remembered them.

The things that happened anyway.

I had told him how I died.

Then I had told him about waking up nineteen again.

All of it.

Ithar had asked questions.

A lot of questions.

More than I expected.

Most of them were annoying.

Some were useful.

A few had made me stop talking for a while.

Now the dragon rested across the stone opposite me with his forelegs folded beneath him.

The light from the opening above caught one side of his face.

Black glass.

That was still the best description I had.

Not black scales.

Glass.

The surface took the light and held it differently depending on where I looked.

I had spent several days staring at him and still occasionally lost the edge of his body in the dark.

One enormous eye remained on me.

I rubbed my face.

"That's it."

"I know."

"You could sound happier."

"Why?"

"I just told you my entire life."

"Two."

"Right."

I lowered my hand.

"See? That's the kind of thing I'm talking about."

His eye closed.

For a few seconds I thought that was the end of the conversation.

Then Ithar stood.

The movement displaced enough air to move my coat.

"Come."

I looked toward the deeper part of the cavern.

"Where?"

"Food."

That was not what I expected.

I pushed myself upright.

My leg complained.

I ignored it.

That had worked poorly for several decades and I saw no reason to change methods now.

"You're feeding me?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because you have been speaking for most of the day."

"That's almost considerate."

"It is practical."

"Of course."

I followed him.

The meal was already there.

I had stopped asking how Ithar arranged things.

There were questions that produced useful information.

There were questions that produced dragon answers.

This was usually the second kind.

A low stone near the wall held bread, roasted meat, fruit, and a bowl of something that smelled strongly of onion.

The bread was still warm.

I sat.

Ithar did not.

He settled several paces away.

I tore off a piece of bread.

"Did you know I was going to finish today?"

"Yes."

"You planned lunch around my autobiography?"

"No."

I waited.

He did not continue.

"Fine."

The meat was good.

That annoyed me slightly.

I had spent years eating whatever could survive a pack, a cart, a siege, a mountain, or Bren.

I had crossed half a kingdom to beg an ancient dragon for help and somehow the bastard fed me better than most inns.

I ate.

Ithar watched.

After a while I pointed at him with the bread.

"You got what you wanted."

"Yes."

"You understand the Line?"

"No."

I stopped chewing.

"No?"

"I understand more than I did."

"That's a much worse answer."

"It is the accurate one."

I swallowed.

"You know what Nhal was carrying."

"I know what you believe she was carrying."

"I was there."

"Yes."

"You've been very impressed by that fact until now."

"I remain impressed."

"Wonderful."

I took another bite.

For the first time since reaching the mountain, I felt something close to relief.

Not hope.

I had learned not to confuse the two.

Relief was smaller.

More useful.

I had gotten here.

I had convinced him.

He believed me.

Whatever happened to the kingdom next, there was at least one creature alive who understood enough to see the problem.

Maybe that would matter.

Maybe it wouldn't.

At least I had done the part I came to do.

Ithar lowered his head slightly.

"Was it worth it?"

I looked up.

"What?"

"Your second life."

I leaned back.

"That's a terrible question."

"It is simple."

"No, it's short."

He waited.

I looked down at the food.

Nessa came to mind.

Not dead.

That was usually how memory brought her back when I wasn't trying.

At the table.

Hair tied up badly.

Doing something else while listening to me complain.

The twins arguing somewhere they had been specifically told not to argue.

A house that needed three repairs and had money for one.

Then other things.

Blood.

Smoke.

Years.

I picked up my cup.

"Most days."

Ithar considered that.

Then he nodded once.

I drank.

"That's all I get?"

"What more do you require?"

"I don't know. Ancient wisdom."

"You have demonstrated little ability to use ordinary wisdom."

I laughed.

Actually laughed.

It hurt a little.

"Fair."

The meal continued.

That should have bothered me.

It didn't.

I was tired.

Not the kind sleep fixed.

The kind that came from finishing something that had been unfinished for too long.

For once, nobody was waiting for me to explain the next step.

Nobody needed me to hold anything together.

The Line was still failing.

The kingdom was still in danger.

But for the length of one meal in a cave, none of it was physically in my hands.

I ate slowly.

When I finished, I wiped my fingers on the cloth beside the plate.

"All right."

Ithar watched me.

"What now?"

"Now?"

"Yes."

I looked toward the passage we had entered through days ago.

"I assume I walk back down the mountain."

"No."

I looked at him.

The word had been quiet.

Not threatening.

That made it worse.

"No?"

"No."

I waited.

He did not elaborate.

"Ithar."

"Greg."

Something in my stomach tightened.

Not fear yet.

Recognition.

I knew what a room felt like when a decision had already been made inside it.

I had spent too much of my life arriving after those decisions.

"You have your answers."

"Yes."

"You believe me."

"Yes."

"Then what's the problem?"

Ithar's head shifted.

Light moved along the side of his neck.

"You."

I stared at him.

"Me."

"Yes."

I almost laughed again.

I didn't.

"What about me?"

"You are dangerous."

There it was.

Simple.

Almost disappointing.

I leaned forward.

"That's your conclusion?"

"It is one."

"After all of that?"

"Because of all of that."

I looked toward the exit again.

Nothing blocked it.

Nothing needed to.

"You think I'm going to hurt you?"

"No."

"Then who?"

"Anyone."

"That's vague."

"It is broad."

I hated when he did that.

I stood.

My leg took my weight.

Barely.

I shifted.

"Explain."

Ithar's attention sharpened.

That was what I had wanted from him when I first arrived.

Funny.

"You possess knowledge no living human should possess. You have demonstrated that you will alter events when you believe you understand their consequences. You know names that were not given to you. You know places you have never visited in this life. You have reached conclusions about systems your species no longer understands because you remember watching those systems fail."

"Yes."

"You have lived one sequence of events and entered another carrying the first with you."

"Also yes."

"You consider these advantages."

"They are."

"That is why you are dangerous."

I spread my hands.

"So your solution is what? Lock me in the cave?"

"No."

My hands stopped.

Ithar looked at me.

I understood.

"No."

"Yes."

"That's stupid."

"It may be."

"You don't even know what happens if I die."

"No."

"I told you what happened last time."

"You did."

"I died and woke up at nineteen."

"Yes."

"So killing me might do exactly that again."

"Yes."

I stared at him.

"Then what the fuck are we doing?"

Ithar was quiet for long enough that I thought, briefly, I had gotten through.

Then he said, "Removing you."

"From where?"

"Here."

"That's not a solution."

"It is a solution to my problem."

I actually laughed then.

One short sound.

"Your problem."

"Yes."

"And if I wake up nineteen again?"

"Then you will not be here."

"I could come back."

"Perhaps."

"To you."

"No."

That stopped me.

Ithar's eye narrowed slightly.

"If what you describe occurs again, and the world returns to a state before this conversation, I will not remember it."

"Obviously."

"Then the creature you meet will not be the creature speaking to you now."

I stared at him.

"You're serious."

"Yes."

"That's still you."

"To you."

The cavern felt colder.

I looked at him for a long moment.

"So if I come back up this mountain and tell you that you killed me, your answer is going to be what?"

"I will not have killed you."

"You will have."

"I will not remember doing so."

"That doesn't make it not happen."

"It makes it not mine."

I shook my head.

"That is unbelievably convenient."

"No."

His voice changed.

Not louder.

Bigger.

The word seemed to occupy more space than it needed.

"It is inconvenient for you."

I stopped.

I had nothing for that.

Ithar rose.

Slowly.

There was no dramatic flare of power.

No roar.

No warning in the stone.

Just mass unfolding from rest.

I took one step backward.

My body had already started doing calculations.

Distance.

Angle.

Exit.

Barrier.

Draw.

Nothing useful.

Of course nothing useful.

I had once made a career out of recognizing when other people's power was too large to use safely.

Ithar did not have that problem.

"Wait."

He did.

That surprised me.

I swallowed.

"You really believe me?"

"Yes."

"All of it?"

"Enough."

"Nhal?"

"Yes."

"The Line?"

"Yes."

"The second life?"

"Yes."

I breathed out.

That was something.

Stupid thing to care about.

Still.

Something.

I looked at the remains of the meal.

"Was that my last meal?"

"No."

I looked back at him.

"No?"

"You have eaten many meals."

I stared.

"Fuck you."

"I understand the expression."

"Good."

I shifted my weight.

My channels opened.

Barrier first.

Automatic.

Old habit.

Ithar noticed.

Of course he noticed.

His head tilted.

"Greg."

"Yeah?"

"You were right about one thing."

I waited.

"Which thing?"

"Knowing what happens does not make you ready for it."

The first layer of Barrier formed.

I never felt the second.
