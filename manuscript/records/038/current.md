## RECORD 038

## THE MEASURE

I went back to the beginning.

That took longer than expected.

Not because I had forgotten the fight.

Because Arcrutus kept rejecting my answers.

"Blue opened with distance control."

"Meaning."

"He took center."

"Meaning."

"He stood two feet closer to the west boundary than his opponent."

"Better."

I looked at him.

"This is going to be annoying, isn't it?"

"Yes."

At least he was honest.

The ring was nearly empty now.

Workers washed blood toward the drain grooves.

One barrier technician crouched beside the cracked west brace and argued with another about whether the damage was old.

The crowd had moved toward food and the next bracket.

Arcrutus stayed where he was.

I did too.

Apparently that was how lessons began when nobody admitted they were lessons.

"Again," he said.

I started over.

"Blue's rear foot was wider in the second minute."

"How much?"

"I don't know."

"Then say wider."

"His left hand opened twice between exchanges."

"Good."

"His support moved closer after the ninth exchange."

"How much closer?"

"One step. Maybe two."

"One or two?"

"One."

"You changed that quickly."

"Fine. I don't know. Closer."

"Good."

This was deeply unsatisfying.

I wanted the clever answer.

Arcrutus wanted the boring parts.

"Blue breathed through his mouth after the long clinch."

"Before?"

"Nose mostly."

"Mostly."

"I wasn't counting nostrils."

"You were counting my chestnuts."

Fair.

"His guard started coming up before his rear foot settled."

Arcrutus nodded once.

"That one you said before."

"Because it mattered."

"You are doing it again."

I closed my mouth.

He waited.

I hated that waiting.

Not because it pressured me.

Because it refused to rescue me.

"His guard came up before the rear foot settled," I repeated.

"Good."

"That is all?"

"That is what you saw."

"And then I interpret it."

"Eventually."

"When?"

"After you stop changing the observation to fit the interpretation."

That landed unpleasantly.

I looked back at the ring.

The wash crew had already erased most of the evidence.

Blood gone.

Dust darkened.

A worker pried splintered wood from the boundary brace.

"So what actually happened?" I asked.

"You tell me."

"Blue accumulated load."

"Everyone accumulates load."

"His recovery changed."

"Yes."

"His support was compensating."

Arcrutus said nothing.

That usually meant I was close enough to continue hurting myself.

"The support moved closer. Blue started showing readiness before he was actually ready. His breathing changed. His stance widened."

"Yes."

"So the support saw deterioration and spent more to hide it."

Arcrutus looked toward the now-empty corner where Blue's support had stood.

"Maybe."

I stared.

"You just made me spend ten minutes being precise so you could say maybe."

"Precision does not create certainty."

That was irritating enough to remember for forty years.

Ithar interrupted.

"You remember the sentence."

"Obviously."

"You remember many things Arcrutus said."

"He didn't say much."

"That may have helped."

I looked at him.

The dragon's head still rested against the stone.

One eye open.

Watching.

"Are you making a point?"

"Not yet."

"Good."

"Continue."

The final was that afternoon.

Not the final final.

Tournaments have too many finals.

Open division final.

The one everybody had been waiting for.

Two A-class fighters.

Neither from Vey.

One belonged to a river clan east of the capital.

The other had no clan banner at all and enough sponsorship marks on his coat to prove independence can become expensive.

I do not remember their names reliably.

I remember the support teams.

That tells you something about me.

Or about memory.

Probably both.

The river fighter used a long spear.

Not like Arcrutus.

I did not know that yet.

At twenty-three, spear was spear until somebody embarrassed you with the difference.

The independent fighter used a short curved sword and a body reinforcement style built around explosive entries.

Fast.

Very fast.

The first exchange nearly ended the match.

Sword crossed half the ring before the spear finished lowering.

Crowd rose.

The spear fighter turned his hips, gave six inches of space, and let the blade pass his ribs by less than a hand.

His return thrust stopped one inch from the other man's throat because the boundary judge called break.

Nobody breathed correctly for several seconds.

I looked at Arcrutus.

He was watching the spear fighter's feet.

Of course.

The match restarted.

This time I tried not to ask what anything meant.

Harder than it sounds.

Humans make stories automatically.

Fast fighter aggressive.

Spear fighter patient.

Support nervous.

Crowd biased.

All of that arrives before observation finishes.

I made myself count instead.

Sword fighter entered high twice.

Low once.

Changed lead foot after every reset.

Spear fighter retreated straight only on first exchange.

After that, always angled left.

His support stood twelve feet back.

Sword fighter's support stood closer.

Eight feet maybe.

No.

Closer than the other support.

Better.

Arcrutus was ruining my nouns.

By the fourth minute both fighters looked fine.

That was suspicious now.

Before Arcrutus, fine meant fine.

Now fine meant I had failed to notice something.

The sword fighter's support raised two fingers after a heavy entry.

No visible spell.

The fighter rolled one shoulder.

Continued.

Next exchange, his left heel landed a little farther out.

I noticed.

Did not interpret.

Good student.

Arcrutus was not looking at me.

That helped.

Two exchanges later the sword fighter came in again.

Same speed.

Same violence.

The spear fighter gave ground.

Sword recovered.

Looked normal.

Support moved one step closer.

I saw that clearly.

One.

Not maybe two.

One.

The crowd started chanting somebody's name.

I ignored them.

Another entry.

Sword fighter exhaled before impact instead of after.

Another.

Left hand opened once during reset.

Another.

Support's jaw tightened.

I had seen that before in the shield match.

Not meaning.

Observation.

Jaw tightened.

Hands too loose.

Breathing shallow.

The support was working.

Harder than he wanted anyone to know.

I looked at Arcrutus.

He was already looking at me.

He lifted one finger.

Not enough to mean anything to anyone else.

To me it meant keep going.

Probably.

Maybe.

Fuck him.

I kept going.

Sword fighter still looked good.

That became the problem.

He had not slowed visibly.

His guard remained sharp.

Entries still violent.

He was still winning more exchanges than he lost.

But his support had moved closer again.

The support's hands were no longer loose.

One thumb pressed into the opposite palm between exchanges.

Channel strain maybe.

No.

Thumb in palm.

That was what I saw.

Spear fighter's support had barely moved all match.

Not because useless.

I had finally learned that lesson.

The spear fighter took a heavy cut across the outer shoulder barrier.

Barrier flared.

He gave one step.

His support did nothing visible.

He returned to position.

Normal.

Sword fighter landed cleanly from another angle.

Crowd erupted.

His own support stepped closer.

That was backwards.

I felt something click.

Not understanding.

Direction.

Arcrutus spoke beside me.

I had not noticed him come down from the gallery.

That was becoming a theme.

"Who loses?"

I almost jumped.

Did not.

Mostly.

"Sword."

"Why?"

"His support is compensating harder every exchange."

"Meaning."

"He keeps moving closer."

"Observation."

"His jaw is tight. Thumb into palm. He stopped blinking during the last sustained exchange."

"Good."

"Sword's left heel is wider on landing. His guard is still sharp. His speed looks the same."

"Looks."

"Yes."

Arcrutus watched the ring.

"So why sword?"

I hated him.

I watched.

Another exchange.

Sword attacked.

Spear yielded.

Sword recovered.

Fine.

Support's shoulders dropped after the reset.

Not relaxed.

Dropped.

Like somebody had set down weight.

Then picked it up again.

"Because he isn't fine," I said.

Arcrutus said nothing.

"He looks fine because the support is spending more and more to keep him looking fine."

Still nothing.

The sword fighter exploded forward again.

Beautiful entry.

Fast enough the spear fighter lost half a step.

The crowd screamed.

The support behind Sword flinched.

Tiny.

Both knees bent.

Recovered.

"He isn't holding together," I said.

Arcrutus looked at me.

I corrected myself.

"No."

The next words came before I knew I had them.

"Someone is holding him together."

Arcrutus's face changed.

Barely.

I saw it.

That was enough.

The fight lasted another three minutes.

Sword did not collapse.

His support did not fail.

Nobody proved my theory dramatically for the crowd.

That mattered.

The support was good.

Very good.

He kept Sword dangerous long after the cost had started climbing.

But every correction spent something.

Every stabilized landing asked for another.

Every preserved shoulder left another place carrying load.

I could not see the whole system.

Not yet.

I could see the bill arriving.

The final exchange looked sudden to everyone else.

Sword entered.

Spear gave the angle he had been giving all match.

Sword committed farther than before.

Just a little.

Rear heel slipped.

Not much.

Enough.

Spear changed line.

Butt of the weapon struck the ribs.

Not the point.

Short end.

Ugly.

Efficient.

Sword folded.

The spear point stopped against his throat.

Match.

Crowd exploded.

I looked at Sword's support.

He sat down.

Not fell.

Sat.

Right where he was standing.

Hands shaking.

The fighter in the ring was still conscious.

Still trying to stand.

His support could not.

That was the first time I understood that the visible body is not always where the cost is being paid.

Not fully.

Enough to remember.

Arcrutus was beside me.

"When did he lose?" he asked.

I looked at the ring.

Sword on one knee.

Spear fighter stepping back.

Healers entering.

People cheering the final strike.

"Before that."

"When?"

"I don't know."

Arcrutus nodded.

That surprised me.

"Good."

"Good?"

"You do not know."

"Usually people prefer answers."

"People prefer closure."

"Different?"

"Often."

I looked toward the support still sitting outside the ring.

A healer had crouched beside him now.

"He did his job."

"Yes."

"And they still lost."

"Yes."

"So what was the point?"

Arcrutus looked at me.

Full attention again.

"You tell me."

I wanted to say winning.

Obviously.

Tournament.

Bracket.

Prize money.

That answer felt too small now.

"He kept his fighter dangerous longer."

"Yes."

"Maybe long enough to win if one exchange changed."

"Yes."

"He made failure arrive later."

Arcrutus's gaze stayed on me.

"Sometimes that is the job."

There it was.

Not profound when written down.

At twenty-three it rearranged furniture.

Support did not need to make someone stronger in the way I had imagined strength.

It could make the point of failure farther away.

Move it.

Delay it.

Distribute it.

Hide it.

Dangerous word, hide.

Manage it.

I was still missing most of the architecture.

But the shape had appeared.

Ithar interrupted.

"You learned this from Arcrutus."

"Some of it."

"Did he teach you?"

"He asked questions."

"That is teaching."

"Do not encourage him retroactively."

"He is dead."

I looked at the dragon.

"Probably."

Ithar's eye narrowed.

"You do not know?"

"Different life. Different history. He was old when I was young. I have not exactly checked the current census of ancient violent clan heads."

"You remember him fondly."

I rubbed my thumb against one knuckle.

"That sounds excessive."

"You threw food at him."

"That is not evidence of affection."

"You have unusual standards."

"You noticed."

Silence.

Then Ithar asked:

"Did you keep separating observation from interpretation?"

"Yes."

The answer came too fast.

I heard it.

So did he.

Ithar waited.

I sighed.

"Sometimes."

Still waiting.

"When I remembered."

The dragon's pupil narrowed.

"And when you did not?"

I looked toward the cave mouth.

Light across stone.

My boot near the dead fire.

The goat bones farther back.

The pack I had brought too little food in.

The mountain I had climbed assuming arrival mattered more than departure.

"I concluded aggressively."

"Frequently?"

"Fuck you."

"Available."

I laughed once.

Then stopped.

Back at the tournament, the final had ended.

People were leaving already.

Amazing how quickly an event becomes cleanup.

Banners came down.

Vendors counted money.

Losers found excuses.

Winners found relatives.

Guild staff found blood.

Always blood.

I was folding stabilization cloth when someone blocked the light at the tent entrance.

Arcrutus.

No chestnuts.

That felt wrong now.

"You work tomorrow?" he asked.

"Here?"

"Yes."

"Morning shift."

"After."

I waited.

He did not continue.

"After what?"

"Work."

"What happens after work?"

"You find me."

I stared at him.

"Why?"

Arcrutus looked at the cloth in my hands.

Then at me.

"You ask too many questions."

"That is not an answer."

"I know."

Then he left.

I watched him cross the grounds.

People made room before he reached them.

Same as the first day.

Only now I was watching differently.

Not what it meant.

What I saw.

Old man.

No weapon.

Uneven knuckles.

One scar at the jaw.

People moving early.

Nobody asking him to hurry.

Nobody asking why.

Arcrutus did not look back.

I noticed that too.
