## RECORD 065

## THE RE-EVALUATION

The Guild charged me full price.

Sel had been correct.

Again.

I was beginning to suspect this was why she raised her rate.

The second B-class support evaluation took place eleven months after the first.

Long enough that the clerk had to find my failed assessment in storage.

Not long enough that she had forgotten me.

"Vane."

"Yes."

She looked at the old sheet.

"Specialized attached-team allocation."

"Yes."

"Unsafe allied Draw history."

"Less unsafe now."

She looked up.

"Wonderful wording."

"Improved allied Draw safety."

"Better."

"Still honest."

"Debatable."

She stamped the new packet.

B-class evaluation had four sections that year.

Standards changed slightly depending on region and season.

Same purpose.

Could a contractor hire you at B without needing to know your private legend first?

That was not written anywhere.

It was what the paperwork meant.

Section one was static formation support.

Of course.

Sel sat in the observation gallery.

I saw her.

She saw me see her.

She smiled.

Terrible woman.

The evaluation team gave me four unfamiliar fighters.

No introductions beyond names and class.

One high-C shield.

Two B combat.

One B ranged caster.

Standard mixed line.

I had three minutes before the start.

First evaluation, I had used the time to ask everyone how they moved.

Too much.

This time I asked three questions.

"What are you holding reserve for?"

Answers.

"If support leaves you unexpectedly, what fails first?"

Better answers.

"Anything I should not reinforce without asking?"

One fighter said right shoulder.

Old channel injury.

Good.

Done.

The shield asked, "That's it?"

"Probably."

"Probably?"

"If I ask enough questions to know you properly, the test will be over."

He laughed.

Good.

People breathed better after laughing.

I did not write that down.

Growth.

The static phase lasted eight minutes.

Timed impacts.

Changing directions.

One simulated breach.

No custom roster movement allowed beyond standard calls.

I stayed in the support pocket.

Maintained self-aura.

Used broad reinforcement when broad reinforcement was correct.

Did not move left because I saw a better angle.

At minute five the ranged caster's footing began to fail under recoil.

I wanted to solve her ankle specifically.

Did not know her ankle.

Reinforced lower stance broadly for two cycles.

Wasteful.

Safe.

She recovered.

At minute seven the shield overcommitted to a high impact.

I could have caught his shoulder.

Instead I reinforced the line behind him and let his own aura do its job.

No secondary failure.

Eight minutes.

Done.

Sel looked bored.

Best compliment she had ever given me.

Section two was emergency stabilization.

I hated it less this time.

Three scenarios.

Simulated channel strain.

Falling injury.

Contamination exposure.

No healer inside the first ninety seconds.

The evaluators were not testing whether I could fix everything.

They were testing whether I would make anything worse while pretending competence.

First scenario: channel strain after an overpowered cast.

The volunteer's right arm locked.

Aura cycling erratic.

Old me would have become fascinated.

Current me stopped further reinforcement, stabilized the arm position, lowered environmental pressure, and called healer.

One evaluator asked, "Draw?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Unknown injury. Unknown consent. Unknown failure path."

"Could reduce pressure."

"Could also remove the thing preventing further tearing."

He nodded.

Good.

Second scenario: fall with possible spine injury.

I did less.

That felt like progress.

Third: chemical powder on skin and clothes.

Barrier boundary around contaminated area.

No airflow inward.

No cleverness.

Wash protocol.

Healer.

Done.

Section three was the one that failed me last time.

Unfamiliar moving team.

Five fighters.

Mixed terrain.

Problems changed without warning.

One evaluator replaced a fighter halfway through.

No locator tabs.

No pre-trained signals.

No Aren asking why only one direction.

No Sella filtering my thoughts for me.

Just people.

I had five minutes.

I used four.

Same three questions.

Then one more.

"If your intended next action changes and I cannot obviously see why, say my name and the action. Nothing else unless I ask."

A fighter with a hooked sword frowned.

"Example?"

"Greg. Retreat."

"That's it?"

"If you have time for a speech, we are probably fine."

The test began.

First two minutes were ordinary.

That was harder than dramatic work.

Ordinary movement reveals whether a system needs crisis to look useful.

I supported conventionally.

Small reinforcement.

Barrier catch.

Release.

Self-aura stayed up.

No Draw.

The hooked-sword fighter spent fast and recovered fast.

One heavy fighter spent slowly but needed more support during turns.

Ranged caster held reserve for a marked third phase.

Good.

I learned enough while working.

Then they added two moving threats and changed the terrain markers.

Everyone accelerated.

Heavy fighter and hooked sword both created useful openings at once.

Old Greg would have tried to decide which person deserved the better line.

Current Greg looked at consequence.

Heavy fighter could carry his own next contact.

Hooked sword could not carry the turn after hers.

Support hooked sword.

Release.

Heavy fighter succeeded anyway.

More capability had become margin instead of noise.

Small example.

Still.

I noticed.

At four minutes the ranged caster said, "Greg. Early cast."

No explanation.

I shifted reserve expectation immediately.

Her prepared third-phase spell was coming now.

I stopped planning around mana she no longer intended to hold.

She fired.

The cast removed two threats.

Good.

At six minutes the evaluator blew a whistle.

One B fighter left.

A replacement entered from the opposite side.

Different weapon.

Different gait.

No briefing.

First evaluation, that would have wrecked me for a full minute.

This time I gave him one sentence while moving.

"Tell me changed intent, injuries, and anything you are holding reserve for."

He said, "Holding one burst. Left ankle old but fine."

Enough.

I did not attempt to make him fit the person he replaced.

He was standing there.

Current state.

Corin had taught me that by kneeling in mud and being annoyed.

The replacement fought more aggressively.

Fine.

That gave the shield less work and gave me more recovery pressure to manage.

Different shape.

Not bad shape.

At minute nine the evaluator created the ugly overlap on purpose.

Two fighters committed.

Shield took impact.

Caster recovered.

Replacement called, "Greg. Second burst."

Four useful demands.

I could not optimize all four.

So I did not.

Broad Barrier behind shield.

Let first fighter self-carry.

Small reinforcement to second because his failure would close the caster's exit.

Ignore caster recovery because she was safe.

Replacement used his second burst without me.

Not perfect.

Operation continued.

That was enough.

At minute ten the whistle ended the section.

I was breathing hard.

Not exhausted.

The hooked-sword fighter looked at me.

"You drop support early."

"Sometimes."

"I thought I lost you twice."

"You did."

She considered that.

"Worked."

"Mostly."

"Annoying."

"Also mostly."

She smiled.

Section four was specialty demonstration.

This part had not existed in my first evaluation.

Or maybe it had and I had failed too early to earn it.

I did not ask.

The panel could choose any demonstrated professional specialty and test whether it added B-grade value rather than merely personal weirdness.

Mine was listed as:

DYNAMIC ATTACHED-TEAM ALLOCATION / LIMITED ALLIED REDISTRIBUTION.

Ugly.

Accurate enough.

They gave me six people.

That was deliberate.

Two from section three.

Four new.

No one from my party.

Good.

One evaluator said, "You may establish a temporary protocol. Ten minutes."

Ten minutes was not enough to build Red Basin.

That was the point.

I did not try.

I gave them locator tabs.

The evaluator raised an eyebrow.

"Approved survey equipment," I said.

"I know what they are."

"Then we're efficient."

Five tabs.

One spare stayed in my pocket.

I taught only what could survive ten minutes.

Locator means location only.

No Draw unless I ask and receive explicit current consent.

No means no.

If your next action changes in a way I cannot see, call it.

If you see a nearby failure I am missing, call the person's name.

You are not commanding me.

You are giving me local priority information.

One fighter asked, "What if you're wrong?"

"Then complain quickly."

Fair.

We ran.

Not Red Basin good.

Not close.

But portable enough.

The unfamiliar six began rough.

Too many calls.

One person narrated reserve.

Another called names every time somebody moved.

I stopped them after ninety seconds.

"Less."

We reset.

Second run improved.

At three minutes a shield fighter called the ranged caster's name.

I looked.

Caster had not been hit.

No obvious problem.

Shield pointed at footing.

Mud under the caster's rear heel.

I reinforced before the slip.

Good local information.

At four minutes one fighter offered two fingers from the availability signal.

I ignored it.

No useful destination.

Mana transfer was not a goal by itself.

At five minutes another fighter offered during recovery.

This time the shield was carrying repeated impact and my own reserve had dropped.

I asked.

"Small?"

"Yes."

"Still one full intended action after?"

"Yes."

Formation position safe.

Aura down.

Tiny Draw.

Immediate Barrier reinforcement behind shield.

Aura up.

Closed.

No one died.

No one applauded.

Professional success.

At seven minutes two fighters overlapped hard.

For one second I felt the old six-person noise.

Too many useful options.

Then the shield called one name.

Ranged caster said, "Holding mine."

One local priority.

One state removed from consideration.

Space opened in my attention.

I supported the named fighter.

The other carried herself.

Operation continued.

At ten minutes the whistle blew.

I wanted another minute.

That was probably why they stopped.

The panel sent me outside.

Sel was waiting in the hall.

"How bad?"

"I don't know."

"Good."

"Why?"

"Last time you knew."

Fair.

We waited thirty-seven minutes.

I counted because stress turns adults into clocks.

The clerk called me back.

Three evaluators.

Same table.

New paper.

The senior evaluator read from it.

"Emergency breadth remains below median for B-class general support."

Sel looked pleased.

I ignored her.

"Static formation support acceptable. Self-protection acceptable. Unfamiliar-team movement acceptable. Casualty stabilization safe within scope."

Safe within scope.

Sel had put that phrase into me with approximately forty silver and emotional violence.

"Specialty performance," the evaluator continued, "demonstrates above-standard allocation efficiency when team communication is established, with improved portability compared to prior evaluation. Allied Draw remains conditional and should not be represented as general-use support practice."

"Agreed."

He looked up.

Maybe surprised.

Also growth.

"The board's concern last year was not that your specialty lacked value. It was that contractors needed to build too much of the operating environment for you before that value appeared."

I waited.

"That concern is reduced sufficiently for B classification."

I did not understand the sentence for half a breath.

Then I did.

"Passed?"

The evaluator looked at the paper.

"That is what I said."

"You used more words."

Sel coughed behind me.

Probably laughing.

The clerk updated the registry that afternoon.

C became B.

One letter.

Eleven months.

Several contracts.

Seven silver in locator tabs.

More than forty silver to Sel.

One broken brace.

One nearly weaponized children's bell.

Reasonable.

I expected the change to feel larger.

It did not.

The Guild hallway remained the same.

My coat still needed a button replaced.

Sel still thought I was bad at emergencies compared to her.

Correctly.

Outside, rain had started.

I stood under the awning and looked at the new credential sheet until the ink stopped feeling temporary.

Sel stood beside me.

"Well?"

"The fee was excessive."

"Obviously."

"You're still better at half the test."

"More than half."

"Debatable."

"You're buying drinks."

I looked at her.

"Why?"

"Because you're B-class now."

"That does not create money."

"Your rate says otherwise."

Unfortunately true.

By evening Aren, Sella, Corin, Jessa, and Varo had all heard.

I still do not know how.

Guild confidentiality is a decorative concept.

Aren sent one line.

ABOUT TIME.

Sella sent:

GOOD. NOW STOP TRYING TO EARN IT DURING CONTRACTS.

Corin asked whether B-class paid for better bridge food.

Varo claimed partial credit because being difficult had expanded my concurrency.

Jessa arrived at the tavern before I did and had already ordered the expensive bottle.

Sel sat beside her.

Two terrible women cooperating.

I paid.
