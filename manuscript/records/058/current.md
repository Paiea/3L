## RECORD 058

## THE ROSTER

Red Basin was the first contract where I arrived after the party.

Not late.

Selected.

Different humiliation.

Six weeks after White Stair, three offers had reached me.

Not applications I had sent.

Offers.

One canal-security company wanted twelve weeks of crawler suppression with a repeat crew.

One quarry consortium wanted me for rotating stabilization teams because somebody had read the North Mason report badly and believed I could teach cycling in an afternoon.

No.

The third came from Red Basin.

Aren's handwriting occupied the bottom.

COME LOOK BEFORE SAYING NO.

I went.

The contract office sat above a freight depot two towns west of Vey.

Big room.

Big map.

Too many red marks.

Red Basin was not actually red.

Mostly gray stone and dead grass.

The name came from iron runoff in spring water.

It stained everything eventually.

Boots.

Rope.

Walls.

People's expectations.

The job was four months.

Old extraction roads reopened for a new mineral survey.

Hazard clearance.

Deep-route escort.

Collapsed works.

Possible hostile fauna.

More importantly, the crews would operate three to six days from the nearest station.

No easy replacement.

No healer within an hour.

No changing the party because somebody annoyed you at dinner.

Good conditions for regret.

Aren was already there.

So was Sella.

That surprised me.

"You came."

"Money," she said.

"I thought curiosity wasn't hazard compensation."

"It isn't. They added hazard compensation."

Consistent woman.

Merek had taken a city contract because his sister was expecting a child and apparently family outranked limestone.

Good.

Iven returned to survey work and remained attached to Red Basin's mapping crew, but not our combat group.

Two new people waited at the table.

The first was Jessa Rook.

A-class channel caster.

Thirty-eight.

Small woman.

Large reputation.

She specialized in pressure lance and shaped discharge, which meant most rooms were temporarily smaller after she became annoyed.

I knew her name.

That was becoming inconvenient.

The second was Corin Hart.

From North Mason.

Not nominally impressive.

Still B-class by then, newly certified.

Controlled output.

Reliable recovery.

The kind of fighter people forgot to describe dramatically because he kept being available when everyone else had become exciting.

I liked him immediately.

Aren had apparently remembered why.

The contractor's roster slate listed:

AREN DRAY — A COMBAT

JESSA ROOK — A CHANNEL

SELLA TOR — B SHIELD

CORIN HART — B COMBAT

GREG VANE — C SUPPORT

I stared at the last line.

Jessa noticed.

"Embarrassing?"

"Visually unbalanced."

"We can cross out C."

"Guild objects."

"Guild isn't here."

I liked her cautiously.

Then I looked at the names again.

Not ranks.

Names.

"Why these four?" I asked.

Aren glanced at the slate.

"Because I thought they'd fit."

"Fit what?"

"You."

There it was too quickly.

I disliked it on principle.

"You barely know how I work."

"I know enough to know what makes it hard."

He tapped Sella's name.

"She can hold without demanding constant attention."

Sella said, "Romantic."

He tapped Corin.

"He spends when other people don't."

Corin nodded as if being described as useful between exciting people was a compliment.

It was.

Then Jessa.

"She spends enormous amounts in discrete events and then has real recovery instead of pretending she's continuously useful."

Jessa looked offended.

"I am continuously useful."

"You are continuously expensive."

"Better."

Then Aren pointed at himself.

"And I can create pressure without needing Greg to make me dangerous first."

I stared.

"You built a party around the support."

"Mostly."

"Nobody does that."

"You've mentioned."

Sella said, "Repeatedly."

I looked at Corin.

"There were stronger options."

"Yes," Aren said.

"You knew that."

"That was why I chose him."

"That sentence is professionally offensive."

Aren shrugged.

"Varo and I solve the same ten seconds. Corin solves the ten after."

I stopped.

That was better language than mine.

Annoying family.

The contractor representative entered before I could recover.

Hollen Marr.

Older than everyone except possibly the table.

He had the expression of a man who had already spent money on us and resented that we still required food.

He pointed at me.

"Vane."

"Yes."

"Dray says the roster waits for you."

I looked at Aren.

"That sounds worse when somebody else says it."

"Still true."

Hollen sat.

"I have a problem with this party."

Sella said, "Only one?"

"Two A-class field rates. One B shield. One B combat. Then C support."

"Still me," I said.

"I know who you are."

Good.

Not sure how I felt about that.

Hollen continued.

"I can get B support for less argument."

Aren said, "Then get one."

Hollen looked at him.

"And you leave."

"Yes."

Jessa raised one finger.

"Me too."

I looked at her.

"Why?"

"I want to see the thing."

"You have not seen the thing."

"Exactly."

Sella sighed.

"Professionals."

Corin said nothing.

Useful man.

Hollen rubbed his forehead.

"Nobody builds a four-month hazard roster around support."

Aren said, "Apparently we do."

There it was again.

Not theory anymore.

Money.

Contracts.

A-class fighters willing to walk.

The professional habit had become expensive enough to argue about.

Hollen looked at me.

"Is this your party?"

I almost said no.

Automatic.

Support didn't own parties.

Support joined them.

Then I looked at the slate.

Aren had requested me first.

Sella had returned because her steady work created room.

Corin had been chosen partly because he filled demand valleys other fighters ignored.

Jessa had been invited because her huge bursts created sharp before-and-after states instead of constant middling need.

Aren had apparently considered himself another roster component instead of the roster's center.

That may have been the strangest part.

Four months of distance meant whatever this was would either become real or become medical paperwork.

"Not yet," I said.

Hollen frowned.

"What does that mean?"

"I need to see Jessa."

Jessa smiled.

"Buy dinner first."

"Casting."

"Less interesting."

Hollen stared at Aren.

"You delayed signing for this?"

"Yes."

I said, "I also need to see the alternate roster."

That surprised everyone except Aren.

Hollen opened another folder.

There were six names.

Two better than Corin on paper.

One better than Sella if the only question was shield output.

A high-B duelist named Varo Tess had more direct combat reputation than Corin and substantially more speed.

I knew Varo slightly.

Good fighter.

Terrible fit.

Currently.

Important word.

"Why not Varo?" Hollen asked.

Aren looked at me.

Not answering.

Mine now.

I hated that.

"Because he and Aren spend together."

Hollen waited.

"Meaning?"

"Both like two hard forward commitments close together. Both recover body slower than reserve. Put them beside each other and the party gets two excellent people who become expensive at the same time."

"They're A and high-B."

"Yes."

"Corin is B."

"Also yes."

"You are choosing a weaker fighter."

"For me. Right now."

Hollen's eyebrows moved.

Aren looked at me too.

I continued because apparently I had found another argument in public.

"That isn't the same as saying Corin is better. It means I can use Corin's differences better than I can use Varo's extra capability if it arrives at the same time as Aren's."

"So stronger is bad."

"No."

That answer came immediately.

I looked at Varo's name again.

More speed.

More pressure.

More reserve.

More self-protection.

Those were not defects.

The defect was that I could not yet keep enough of those options useful at once.

"Stronger gives me more margin if I can control it," I said. "I can't control all of that margin yet."

Hollen looked unimpressed by my professional confession.

Fair.

Aren did not.

That was worse somehow.

Varo would have beaten Corin in most direct evaluations.

Possibly badly.

That did not make him more useful to **current me** every minute of a four-month operation.

The current part mattered.

I wrote it down later.

Hollen did not like the roster.

Good.

Neither did part of me.

Two years earlier I would have taken the strongest bodies available and considered adaptation my problem.

Now I was doing something almost opposite.

Not selecting weak people.

Selecting **differences I could actually use**.

Jessa leaned back.

"What about me?"

"Don't know yet."

"Excellent start."

We went to the yard.

Jessa's casting was beautiful.

I hated it immediately.

Not personally.

Professionally.

She carried mana high.

Very high.

Then spent in violent packets.

Pressure lance.

Release.

Three breaths.

Again.

Her aura remained stronger than most casters during the cast because backlash ran through her shoulders and spine.

Draw windows looked obvious.

They were not.

I tried a supervised touch pull after she offered.

Tiny.

Her aura bit back hard enough to numb two fingers.

"Ow."

Jessa looked delighted.

"Again?"

"No."

"Coward."

"Alive."

Second attempt after a full recovery was cleaner.

Still expensive.

Third after she deliberately lowered her aura was excellent.

Then she winced.

"Backlash?"

"Shoulder."

There it was.

Her aura had been doing work even while reserve looked available.

Another full cup that was not surplus.

I stopped.

"You are bad for Draw."

Jessa looked offended.

"I'm A-class."

"Congratulations."

Aren laughed.

I continued.

"But your spending rhythm is useful. You go high, hard, then you have real recovery periods if we can keep people off you."

"Normal support says reinforce recovery."

"Maybe."

"You hate maybe."

"No. Other people hate when I say it."

We trained three days before signing.

Hollen paid training rate because Aren made that a condition.

Another reversal.

People had started budgeting for the support to learn them.

More importantly, Aren had budgeted for the **party** to learn why these people had been selected together.

That was newer.

I wrote too much.

Jessa spent most of day one irritated that I would not Draw from her.

Useful, but not in the Aren way.

She cast worse annoyed.

Too aggressive.

Opened channels early.

Wasted pressure.

I wrote that down too.

"Are you judging my personality?"

"Operationally."

"That is worse."

"Probably."

Sella said, "He does this now."

"Why do you tolerate it?"

Sella thought.

"He stopped telling me I'm safe before bad rooms."

Jessa stared at her.

"That is your standard?"

"High bar."

Corin's rhythm remained exactly why I wanted him.

He spent little when nothing mattered.

Not passive.

Patient.

Then one clean burst.

Recovery short.

Reserve rebuilt steadily.

He almost never gave me a giant reservoir.

He gave me frequent small availability.

More useful to me then.

Aren adapted fastest because he had started before the rest of us knew we were doing something unusual.

By day three he had changed the timing of one forward sequence to land Jessa's recovery behind his strongest pressure window.

I noticed.

"You did that for her."

"For you."

"How?"

"If she recovers while I'm spending, you don't have to support both."

Jessa looked at him.

"You changed your attack for my breathing?"

"Three seconds."

"That's disgusting."

Sella said, "I said that first."

Professional consensus.

Then Jessa did it back.

That mattered more.

Next drill Aren committed deep.

Normally Jessa would have followed with a pressure lance immediately.

She held.

One breath.

Two.

Aren released.

Corin moved.

I crossed.

Then Jessa fired.

Her attack was later.

The **operation** was faster.

We reset.

I stared at her.

"What?" she said.

"You waited."

"He waits for you."

"That is not a reason."

"It worked."

Aren said nothing.

Smugness has posture.

I wrote it down.

Not the smugness.

Maybe the smugness.

By the end of the third day I signed off on the roster.

That sentence felt wrong.

Hollen made me repeat it.

"You approve?"

"Yes."

"No changes?"

"No."

"And if I replaced Corin with Varo?"

"I would need to retrain the timing."

"Would you leave?"

I looked at Aren.

He did not help.

Good.

My answer.

"If you changed it after we built the contract around this method without giving us training time? Yes."

Hollen leaned back.

"You're C-class."

There it was.

Not cruel.

Fact.

I felt the old reflex.

Apologize with usefulness.

Fit in.

Make the expensive people comfortable.

Then Jessa said, "He's also the reason I signed."

Sella nodded.

Corin did too.

Aren had already threatened to leave.

I looked at Hollen.

"Then hire another support."

He stared at me.

Long enough that my confidence became mostly decorative.

Then he laughed.

"Fine."

Contract signed.

Red Basin began in late summer.

The first month was not dramatic.

Good.

Long roads.

Old cuts.

Hot days.

Cold nights because basins enjoy punishment.

We learned how everyone smelled after three days without proper washing.

Important information.

Jessa snored lightly.

Sella snored like structural failure.

Corin woke first and never admitted it.

Aren made terrible tea.

I began carrying my own.

The stronger party changed my work immediately.

Not because they needed less support.

Sometimes they did.

The important part was that they created bigger differences between ordinary and exceptional moments.

Aren did not need reinforcement to kill most things.

So supporting every strike was waste.

Jessa did not need more mana for ordinary pressure work.

She needed room to recover from the attacks worth spending on.

Sella could hold a line by herself longer than most shield fighters I knew.

Which meant the moment she actually needed me was expensive.

Corin's value was not peak output.

It was that he could keep taking small burdens while everybody else moved through large ones.

More capability did not simplify allocation.

It sharpened it.

And Red Basin had been deliberately built so those expensive moments did not all arrive together.

That was training for me as much as roster design.

A cleaner problem.

Not an easier one.

One afternoon we cleared a broken incline where shellbacks had nested beneath ore carts.

Sixteen animals.

Open ground.

Bad cover.

Jessa could have destroyed half the incline.

Contractor preferred we keep the road.

Limitations create technique.

Aren went first.

No support.

Didn't need it.

Sella caught the left group.

I reinforced her rear hip for one impact.

Gone.

Corin gave two fingers.

I did not draw.

Nothing worth spending it on yet.

He closed.

Jessa waited.

Three shellbacks compressed around Aren.

There.

I moved before he asked.

Barrier to his outside line.

Not protection.

Boundary.

The third animal couldn't use the angle it wanted.

Aren saw the plane.

Changed his spear path around it.

Again.

He was using my support as terrain.

Jessa fired through the opening he created.

One lance.

Three animals down.

Her aura held backlash.

No Draw.

Corin picked up the next pressure.

Sella released.

Two fingers.

Now.

Small Draw from Sella.

Barrier to Corin's exposed side.

Release.

Aren recovering.

Two fingers.

Draw.

Not to Jessa.

To my reserve, then immediately into a low plane that gave Sella one safe step to rotate.

The whole thing felt less like supporting four people.

More like keeping useful differences from disappearing at the same time.

We cleared the incline.

No injuries.

Road mostly intact.

Hollen's field observer wrote:

SUPPORT APPEARS LOW-OUTPUT RELATIVE TO ROSTER.

I found this offensive until the next sentence.

OPERATIONAL UPTIME HIGH.

Aren read it over my shoulder.

"Low-output."

"I saw."

"Should we find you a smaller badge?"

"I will put your tea in your boots."

"Temper management?"

"Science."

He smiled.

I did not need him angry that day.

By the second month contractors stopped asking why the roster had been built around a C-class support.

Not because they understood.

Because nobody had been hurt yet and the work was ahead of schedule.

Results make curiosity cheaper.

Then requests started reaching camp.

Not for Aren.

He already received those.

Not for Jessa.

Same.

For me.

A salvage company wanted winter availability.

A military contractor asked whether I trained attached teams.

A Guild evaluator requested copies of Red Basin support logs.

One letter was addressed:

GREG VANE, SUPPORT, RED BASIN PARTY.

No class.

I read that twice.

Sella caught me.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You have the face."

Everybody had the face now.

I put the letter away.

The strange part was not that stronger people wanted support.

Strong people had always wanted support.

The strange part was that one of them had looked at the roster **before I arrived** and asked which people would give my support the most useful differences to work with.

Then the others had started asking what they needed to change to make those differences easier to use.

That was not a normal roster anymore.

It was the beginning of an architecture.

A crude one.

Built partly around my limitations.

That last part mattered.

If I ever became good enough, I should not need Corin to be weaker in the convenient directions.

More speed should give me more options.

More reserve should give me more margin.

More self-sufficiency should buy me more time.

The fact that some stronger people were harder for me to use did not prove strength was the problem.

It proved I still was.
