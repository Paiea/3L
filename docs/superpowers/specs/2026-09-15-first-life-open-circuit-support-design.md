# First-Life Open-Circuit Support Design

Date: 2026-09-15
Status: design authority pending implementation into story authority files

## Purpose

Make Greg's climb from useful C-class-adjacent support to the era's S-class support logically earned, professionally contested, physically embodied, and mechanically continuous with both Barrier and the later Life-Two solo-support inversion.

Canonical prose wins every conflict. This document describes durable development pressure, not fixed chapter choreography.

## Core thesis

Greg does not become extraordinary because he discovers a secret power nobody else could have noticed. He becomes extraordinary because he specializes deeply in a common primitive, studies its counters, notices a second primitive manipulates the same underlying system from the opposite direction, and spends years combining them in a way conventional support doctrine reasonably considers inefficient, unsafe, and over-specialized.

His rise is driven by a compounding loop:

observe -> intervene -> solicit feedback -> compare expectation to result -> revise -> repeat

Greg is unusually good at noticing physical and magical feedback, unusually willing to admit a method failed, and unusually persistent about extracting a better model from failure. He often interprets too quickly; experts and teammates must correct him. His advantage is not infallibility. It is fast iteration.

## Normal support meta

Conventional support doctrine is rational and should remain strong.

A professional support typically:
- maintains a personal aura/self-reinforcement because losing the support can collapse a party;
- operates from a relatively stable protected pocket;
- minimizes target switching because reacquisition, distance, motion, and changed geometry reduce precision and waste mana;
- applies broad, standardized reinforcement and stabilization patterns that work adequately with unfamiliar teams;
- concentrates support on predictable high-value roles such as the primary anchor/frontline, with planned bursts toward damage or casters;
- values breadth, reliability, certification, and day-one deployability over narrow individualized optimization;
- treats aggressive ward-breaking techniques as inappropriate for routine ally support.

This meta is not stupid. It is easier to teach, easier to certify, safer with strangers, more robust if the support is interrupted, and usually good enough.

Greg should repeatedly encounter experts who tell him he is doing support incorrectly. Some criticisms are fully correct. His early implementations should injure people, waste capacity, arrive late, or create unacceptable risk. Greg may be right that the accepted answer is incomplete while critics are right that his current version is unsafe.

## Barrier specialization

Barrier remains the root of Greg's support discipline.

Barrier is a cheap common utility spell whose deep primitive is:

- define a boundary;
- decide what crosses;
- decide what remains stable;
- decide where force or pressure is permitted to go.

Greg learned it for an ordinary contraceptive use, then kept specializing because his precision with it produced disproportionate practical value.

A serious Barrier specialist studies what defeats barriers. This is Greg's natural route into Draw.

## Draw

Draw is not normally a support spell. It is primarily a ward-breaking / draining technique.

A large maintained barrier or ward can be beaten by brute force, but Draw attacks the sustaining flow instead: establish a pressure differential, pull mana from the ward faster than it can replenish, weaken sections, and eventually collapse the structure.

Greg learns Draw because he wants to understand what beats Barrier.

His important realization is not "mana can move." Transfer, storage, and draining may already be known. His realization is that Barrier and Draw manipulate the same underlying system from opposite directions:

Barrier defines the valve/boundary.
Draw creates or redirects flow across it.

Greg begins asking where drained mana goes, whether only part of a ward can be relieved, whether pressure can be drawn from an overloaded allied reinforcement rather than an enemy ward, and whether mana drawn from one place can be spent somewhere else before it becomes useless load inside him.

## Aura conflict / open-circuit cost

Personal aura is effectively a continuously maintained self-boundary/reinforcement field. It keeps Greg's mana coherent around himself, resists foreign pressure, and provides passive durability.

Full personal aura materially interferes with tactical Draw-routing. Aura tries to keep mana bound to and normalized within Greg; Draw-routing requires mana to enter, pass through, and leave again. The two systems fight each other.

Greg's heretical solution is to stop maintaining continuous aura while operating his network.

This is a real sacrifice, not a free efficiency trick.

Without continuous aura Greg is more vulnerable to:
- stray impacts;
- falls and debris;
- magical pressure;
- interruption while concentrating;
- fatigue-driven defensive mistakes.

He can still use Barrier reactively and can later learn brief localized self-protection, but he cannot simply run full passive aura while preserving the mature Draw-routing network. The sacrifice remains meaningful at high level.

This creates the First-Life architectural premise:

OPEN CIRCUIT
Greg spends less capacity maintaining himself as a closed durable unit so he can act as a controlled transit path between other people.

## First tactical misuse of Draw

The first ally application should grow out of a real support problem, not a theoretical invention.

A teammate's aura, ward, reinforcement channel, or supported structure becomes overloaded. Greg knows Draw can relieve sustaining pressure. He applies a tiny controlled Draw to an ally.

The ally experiences this as alarming because Greg has technically attacked/drained their protection.

It works partially.

Greg now holds mana he did not want. Another party member needs support. Greg spends the drawn mana there.

The important observation is simple:

he took capacity from where there was temporarily too much or unused capacity and moved it to where there was temporarily too little.

Early versions are lossy, uncomfortable, unsafe, and professionally indefensible.

## Mana cycling

Greg eventually stops thinking of party members as isolated mana economies.

Cycling does not create mana and must never become an infinite battery. Every transfer has cost:
- transfer loss;
- range loss;
- switching/reacquisition cost;
- foreign-mana incompatibility or control friction;
- throughput limit;
- risk to the source if Greg draws too much;
- risk to the target if Greg pushes too much;
- Greg's own cognitive and physical bandwidth.

The network becomes valuable because party demand is uneven over time.

One member bursts and then recovers.
One spends steadily.
One has a large reserve but slow recovery.
One regenerates quickly but has shallow storage.
One has a high-output window followed by physical recovery while magical reserve remains.

Greg learns to route small amounts of capacity toward the current bottleneck rather than keep all members maximally full.

A full reservoir may also waste potential regeneration if there is no room for recovery to add usable mana. Greg may deliberately draw a member slightly below full, route that mana to active demand, and allow the source's natural regeneration to refill the margin. This does not create energy; it harvests regeneration bandwidth that would otherwise be idle.

Greg should not equalize everyone. He learns individual useful operating windows and preserves margin based on actual technique, fatigue, body, and role.

## Feedback / diagnostic Draw

Draw also becomes a diagnostic instrument.

A very small controlled pull can reveal resistance and flow behavior:
- reserve pressure;
- strained channels;
- poor recovery in one pathway;
- unusual bottlenecks;
- regeneration state;
- reinforcement that is carrying more load than the user realizes.

It does not read thoughts or emotions.

This reinforces Greg's existing talent: he already studies posture, compensation, fatigue, timing, food, pain, equipment, and technique. Draw gives him another feedback channel that must still be interpreted correctly.

## Adaptive allocation

Greg's mature insight is not "buff everyone." His mana, attention, position, and switching speed remain scarce.

He becomes exceptional at determining the marginal value of support in the present moment:

- the tank may already be safe enough;
- the striker may have a two-second kill window;
- the caster may need stabilization now rather than more reserve later;
- someone who just completed a burst may temporarily become a source rather than a target;
- terrain or equipment may be the best place to carry a consequence.

The value of support moves during a real operation. Greg becomes unusually good at moving with it.

Normal doctrine can outperform him in clean, predictable, unfamiliar, or mass-support scenarios. Greg's method becomes strongest with repeated partners, high-capability teammates, complex changing conditions, narrow timing windows, and operations where individual failure modes interact.

## Mobility as delivery system

Cycling is the engine. Mobility is the delivery system.

Support quality degrades with distance and motion. Close range provides better feedback, lower loss, faster reacquisition, and more precise Barrier/Draw control. Conventional supports solve this by making the party remain near the support.

Greg instead trains himself to move through the party.

His C-class warrior background matters as chassis rather than hidden combat greatness. He already knows hostile spacing, footwork, falling, route choice, and functioning while tired and threatened.

He then spends years deliberately building extreme work capacity:
- road running;
- hills;
- intervals;
- loaded movement;
- casting while jogging and sprinting;
- changing targets while moving;
- maintaining fine Barrier work under high heart rate;
- reacquiring targets after direction changes;
- long-duration field conditioning.

Aerobic fatigue should visibly degrade fine magic early: tremor, slower acquisition, poorer edges, bad judgment, longer recovery. Progress is the ability to remain precise deeper into fatigue.

Greg does not become the fastest fighter. He becomes efficient at positioning and interception. His observation lets him move before party geometry changes.

His mature promise to elite partners is effectively: you fight; I will keep up.

## Arcrutus and deliberate workshop

Greg actively seeks edge cases and asks powerful people what fails first when they push hard.

He workshops with fighters, supports, medics, artificers, engineers, runners, climbers, and other specialists. His development method includes studying counters and importing useful abstractions from other trades.

Arcrutus remains a sparse catalyst, not a mentor engine. Greg brings him specific questions. Arcrutus often attacks Greg's framing rather than providing theory.

Arcrutus can identify Greg's physical bottleneck and train it through absurd but useful pressure, including releasing controlled beasts to chase Greg while Greg must run, think, maintain Barriers, protect fragile targets, or manage competing priorities.

Arcrutus may give one strange exercise. Greg is the one who spends years turning the lesson into a method.

## Greg's own party

Greg eventually helps create or intentionally assembles a party around the network rather than only joining conventional compositions.

He does not abolish tanks, strikers, casters, or normal roles. Best-case conventional allocations remain correct when the fight remains clean.

His party is built to survive adaptive reallocation.

Members value:
- self-sufficiency when Greg switches away;
- honest feedback;
- predictable recovery/failure profiles;
- complementary demand rhythms;
- ability to receive and lose support cleanly;
- willingness to train handoffs and bad configurations;
- enough trust to permit controlled Draw.

Greg may choose a nominally weaker fighter over a stronger one if the weaker member's demand and recovery rhythm complements the network better.

The party trains transitions and failure handoffs, not only ideal formations.

The human coordination should precede the mature magical link. Teammates learn to feel when Greg's support arrives and disappears, adjust their output, create movement lanes for him, and protect his routes rather than simply park him behind a tank.

## Why powerful people want Greg

Most support experiences diminishing returns as the target approaches the human ceiling. Elite fighters already possess enormous aura, reinforcement, reserve, and technique.

Greg's style can produce increasing returns because he does not need to add raw capability. He manages the reasons existing capability cannot be used continuously or safely.

With an S-class fighter, the valuable intervention may be only a fraction of a second:
- relieve a channel bottleneck;
- catch recoil;
- route support mana from another recovering member;
- stabilize one transition;
- preserve a joint or reinforcement path long enough for another maximal action.

Greg does not require elite fighters to reshape their entire style around the support's preferred formation. He learns their rhythms, moves with them, and intervenes when the marginal value is highest.

This is why S-class people can become unusually protective of access to him: he lets people who already know their ceiling discover usable margin above the ceiling they thought they knew.

The stronger the party, the richer the network can become, but the harder it is to control. Higher capability means larger reserves, faster regeneration, more powerful bursts, narrower windows, more dangerous feedback, and greater consequences for bad routing.

Greg scales with partner quality because high-capability people possess more exploitable margin, not because their mana becomes free power.

## Party-size ceiling

Greg remains a bottleneck.

Each additional member adds another resource curve, failure profile, transfer route, geometry problem, and interaction with every other member. Complexity grows faster than headcount.

Early Greg may barely manage two useful live relationships.
Mature party Greg can cycle a small group.
A-class Greg can maintain a dangerous multi-person network.
Nhal forces him beyond the scale he considers sane.

This provides a non-game-stat measure of development without allowing Greg to link armies casually.

## Progression pressure

The exact record count remains flexible. The climb should feel earned without mechanically requiring thirty chapters before A-class.

A likely pressure sequence after Record 044:

1. field party / reserve / mobility / conventional doctrine pushback;
2. Barrier specialization deepens;
3. Greg deliberately studies Barrier counters;
4. Draw appears first as ward-breaking technique;
5. first diagnostic and ally-pressure-relief misuse;
6. ugly early transfer failures and justified criticism;
7. Greg begins cycling small amounts within a repeat party;
8. own-party / party-redesign phase around complementary rhythms and handoffs;
9. sustained mobility and stamina development;
10. B-class/high-B practical value;
11. elite operations and institutional classification friction;
12. A-class recognition while practical team value already exceeds the letter;
13. S-class workshop / Arcrutus edge cases;
14. Nhal expedition proves S-class scale rather than creating Greg from nothing.

The ascent may accelerate once Greg has learned how to learn. Calendar years can burn faster than transformational experiences.

## Professional pushback rule

Future prose should include substantially more disagreement than a simple genius-against-stupid-establishment pattern.

Experts should tell Greg:
- his breadth is poor;
- Draw on allies is unsafe;
- dropping aura is reckless;
- switching targets wastes mana;
- repeat-team dependence makes him less deployable;
- his methods are hard to certify and reproduce;
- his party composition is fragile if he goes down;
- some conventional methods outperform his in clean scenarios.

Often they are correct.

Greg's response is evidence, iteration, and narrower claims rather than speeches about revolution. A failed implementation can validate the critic while preserving the underlying question.

The strongest version of Greg's career is not "everyone was wrong and Greg was right." It is:

professional doctrine correctly identified real risks; Greg discovered those risks were manageable under a narrower, stranger specialization and spent years becoming capable of paying its costs.

## First-Life / Life-Two mirror

First-Life peak support is relational and open:
- outward links;
- repeated partners;
- mobility;
- trust;
- other people's reserves and recovery rhythms;
- minimal continuous self-aura while the network is active.

After the Life-Two catastrophe Greg deliberately begins building the opposite architecture before losing the leg:
- self-reinforcement;
- self-stabilization;
- terrain anchoring;
- hostile Draw/routing;
- stored mana and artifacts;
- narrow or closed loops;
- reduced dependence on partners.

The ruined leg later makes the old mobile-relational doctrine even harder to recover, but disability is not the original cause of the divergence. Choice comes first. Injury compounds it.

The thematic contrast is:

First Life: I am strongest between people.
Life Two revenge: I will make myself a complete circuit.

The later peg can become a practical anchor only if prose earns it. It is never a hidden upgrade that makes the loss beneficial.

## Nhal convergence

By the Nhal expedition Greg is already a monster at support and officially A-class. Nhal does not teach him the basics.

The expedition expands his existing network logic beyond ordinary party scale:
- multiple S-class humans;
- artifacts;
- terrain;
- ancient structures;
- containment;
- foundational load.

Greg's mature questions naturally apply:
- what is the boundary;
- what sustains it;
- what is being drawn or carried;
- where does the load go;
- what happens if a node is removed;
- who or what can safely inherit the consequence.

After one S-class human dies, Greg continues to manage a system operating at S-class consequence scale. This proves his recognition. It does not suddenly grant it.

## Record 045 pressure

Record 045 should continue directly from THE ATTACHMENT rather than use the preserved Life-Two Juna chapter at this location.

It should remain early enough that Greg has not discovered tactical Draw cycling yet.

Primary jobs:
- deepen the three-month party contract;
- establish conventional support doctrine as something competent people actually believe;
- make Greg feel the physical cost of being the support who moves too much;
- show his emerging feedback habit and willingness to ask teammates what support actually felt like;
- introduce meaningful disagreement where Greg's instinct is interesting but his current execution is not automatically correct;
- seed the idea that support value changes with situation and that Greg may need to move to where support matters rather than support everything continuously;
- preserve scene dominance, ordinary field life, listen-first action grammar, humor, and material detail;
- do not introduce full Draw/cycling before it has been earned;
- avoid ending with another polished conceptual theorem. Let the chapter land in lived consequence or unresolved friction.

The old THE RIVAL material remains salvageable Life-Two developmental material for later placement, but it should not govern Record 045's current location.
